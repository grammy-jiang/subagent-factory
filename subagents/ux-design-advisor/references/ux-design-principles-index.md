# UX Design Principles Index

## Information Architecture Foundations

- **P006** — Treat information environments as places: use semantic cues, ordering, labels, typologies, and familiar concepts to shape users' understanding of where they are and what they can do.
- **P008** — Generate candidate labels from content analysis, extraction tools, authors, SMEs, user advocates, card sorting, free listing, and search logs, but validate them before treating them as final.
- **P012** — Design labels as compact representations that use user language, distinguish destinations, avoid organizational jargon, and are tested in their actual channel context.
- **P013** — Design labels as a scoped, consistent system by managing style, presentation, syntax, granularity, comprehensiveness, audience terminology, and modular boundaries.
- **P017** — Analyze each architecture problem through users, content, and context instead of applying generic best practices or competitor patterns uncritically.
- **P019** — Evaluate IA through its component systems: organization, labeling, navigation, search, content/task elements, and invisible metadata or vocabulary systems.
- **P025** — Design cross-channel coherence from abstract semantic structures that can be expressed differently in each channel while remaining recognizable and consistent.
- **P026** — Begin architecture exploration with a hierarchy, then balance mutual exclusivity, cross-listing, breadth, depth, and expected growth through user testing.
- **P028** — Include content-management implications in strategy work, aligning IA with content strategy, rules, roles, resources, templates, metadata, and thesauri.
- **P038** — Frame architecture work as technology-independent design of information environments, not as guidance tied to one site, device, software package, or job title.
- **P039** — Design organization and labeling with awareness that classification shapes understanding, reflects perspectives, and carries organizational politics.
- **P041** — Design thesauri as semantic networks of equivalence, hierarchy, and associative relationships, using professional judgment and research rather than expecting one correct design.
- **P065** — Preserve stable semantic structures while allowing controlled adaptation, separating fast-changing areas from slower architectural foundations.
- **P067** — Plan for IA maintenance after launch because content, people, and context keep changing and the architecture must evolve intentionally.
- **P068** — Select organization schemes by user knowledge and task: exact schemes support known-item access, ambiguous schemes support exploration, and combinations are often necessary.
- **P069** — Use hierarchy, database-oriented structure, hypertext, and tagging as complementary organization structures, selecting each for the part of the environment where its strengths fit.
- **P070** — Use controlled vocabularies and metadata as system glue, shifting from placing documents in one taxonomy to describing them for navigation and retrieval.

- **P020** — Choose retrieval algorithms, stemming, query builders, and structured-field search according to the user's recall-versus-precision needs.
- **P027** — Support integrated, iterative finding behavior by letting users move fluidly among search, browse, asking, similar-item paths, and subsite-level navigation.
- **P029** — Provide supplemental navigation such as sitemaps, indexes, guides, configurators, and search as backup paths because taxonomies and embedded navigation will not satisfy every user or task.
- **P030** — Select sorting, ranking, best bets, popularity, ratings, paid placement, or clustering based on task, content heterogeneity, available metadata, and user trust implications.
- **P040** — Design contextual links and headings around user expectations, surrounding context, visual hierarchy, and process sequence rather than ad hoc author preference.
- **P058** — Judge navigation by how hard each click is rather than the raw count: keep every click a mindless, unambiguous choice with a strong 'scent of information', use progressive disclosure to avoid confronting users with everything at once, an...
- **P064** — Respect established navigation conventions and provide clear place cues so users know where they are, what the environment is, and where they can go after deep entry.
