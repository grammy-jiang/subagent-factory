# Product principles index

The catalogue of product-design principles behind this advisor's product-management skills. Each row gives the principle id (as cited across the skills), a paraphrased one-line statement, and the predominant source. Principles were synthesized across the six product-management works, so the source column names the work most of a principle's evidence came from, not its only source; the human-centered-AI principles are catalogued separately in `human-ai-interaction-guidelines.md`.

**Source legend.** Inspired = Cagan, *Inspired*; CDH = Torres, *Continuous Discovery Habits*; EBT = Perri, *Escaping the Build Trap*; USM = Patton, *User Story Mapping*; ShapeUp = Singer, *Shape Up*; LeanUX = Gothelf & Seiden, *Lean UX*.

## Product strategy and outcomes

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P002 | Shift the organization from output to outcomes; empower teams to choose the features and replace roadmaps with a risk-prioritized hypothesis backlog. | LeanUX |
| P004 | Avoid the build trap of measuring success by features shipped; strategy lives in the opportunity space, not a feature list. | CDH |
| P044 | Start from the problem and the why before any solution; treat solution ideas as biased guesses to validate. | EBT |
| P047 | Treat strategy as a deployable decision framework connecting vision to outcomes, not a detailed plan; never commit a vision or feature set without validation. | EBT |
| P048 | Tell strategy as stories at the right scale for each level; set goals that are neither too prescriptive nor too broad. | EBT |
| P049 | Close the knowledge, alignment, and effects gaps by communicating strategic intent and granting autonomy, not by demanding detail or adding controls. | EBT |
| P057 | Measure, judge, and reward teams by outcomes (customer and business value), not outputs; optimizing output volume is the build trap. | EBT |
| P058 | Frame decisions by aligning the team on the exact problem, persona, goals, and priority order; reason transparently and drive to buy-in over escalation. | Inspired |
| P059 | Prioritize by specific target outcomes; each outcome is behavior change for specific people, so choosing one chooses whom to serve first. | USM |
| P060 | Do not equate launch with success; define success and failure metrics in advance and run a rapid-response phase at launch. | Inspired |
| P064 | Treat value as the outcome for customer and business, not the artifact; tie every feature to a business outcome and never count features as value. | EBT |
| P065 | Re-evaluate reward structures; deliverable scorecards drive box-checking, so align incentives with the outcome behavior you want. | EBT |

## Continuous discovery and research

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P010 | Aim for valuable, usable, and feasible at their intersection, found by a small cross-functional discovery triad. | USM |
| P013 | Build lightweight personas together from facts and honestly labeled assumptions to create shared empathy. | USM |
| P018 | When customers are hard to reach, get creative and back up from proposed solutions to ask why — it is your job to ask the right questions. | CDH |
| P025 | Be wary of what people say they want; stated preferences are guesses, and the proof is whether they choose to use the product. | Inspired |
| P029 | Run a lightweight opportunity assessment and get an explicit go or no-go from management before building. | Inspired |
| P037 | Use a charter customer program to gain deep insight and secure a handful of real reference customers from the true target market. | Inspired |
| P043 | Use personas to make hard product choices; focus each release on one primary persona and verify with real users. | Inspired |
| P046 | Do not rely on market research to decide what to build; winning products come from deep user understanding plus what is newly possible. | Inspired |
| P061 | Target users' most acute frustration; intense early adopters reveal true value while technology-lovers can mislead. | Inspired |
| P072 | After launch, continuously harness organization-wide customer intelligence — service agents, feedback channels, search logs, and analytics. | LeanUX |
| P083 | Run discovery first in startups and new products — validate a high-fidelity prototype before hiring the engineering team. | Inspired |
| P084 | Apply design thinking end to end: empathize, define and focus, ideate, prototype, and test with real users on real tasks. | USM |

## Assumptions, hypotheses, and MVP experiments

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P003 | Treat every design as a testable hypothesis; build the smallest MVP (need not be code) in a build-measure-learn loop and treat it as disposable learning. | LeanUX |
| P012 | Learning you were wrong early is good news; celebrate learning and use stories fast and informally in discovery. | EBT |
| P073 | Use lightweight non-prototype MVP techniques (email, ads, landing-page facade, button-to-nowhere) to test demand and value. | LeanUX |
| P085 | Define the MVP as the smallest release that achieves the outcome — minimal to your customers, not the highest-paid person's opinion. | USM |
| P092 | Before testing, define what you want to learn and collect data on what people actually do; simulate the minimal right moment to observe behavior. | CDH |
| P093 | Frame work as testable hypotheses about outcomes; express beliefs as hypotheses to remove subjective and political noise from decisions. | LeanUX |
| P100 | If a solution is too expensive, step back to the problem; break big-but-affordable work into small deliverable slices to learn sooner. | USM |
| P107 | Integrate Lean UX into Scrum's events as mileposts; write stories as end-user benefits and actively groom the prioritized backlog. | LeanUX |

## Prototyping and usability testing

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P030 | Choose prototype fidelity by the trade-offs of each level (paper, clickable, mid- and high-fidelity, coded), and trial several tools. | LeanUX |
| P031 | Fix Waterfall's late-validation flaw — prototype and resolve feasibility risk before design and implementation. | Inspired |
| P033 | Define the minimal product up front as a high-fidelity prototype with estimates, validate with real users, then do not cut further. | Inspired |
| P036 | Use a tested high-fidelity prototype as the spec; paper specs are slow, unread, and give false comfort. | Inspired |
| P053 | Administer usability tests well — keep users in use mode, stay quiet, note task outcomes, and find where their model conflicts with the software. | Inspired |
| P090 | Test wherever convenient without a formal lab; treat remote testing as a supplement, and have the product manager attend every test. | Inspired |
| P099 | Take software to real users on a regular cadence and watch them pursue a real goal — not show-and-tell. | USM |

## Story mapping and workshops

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P011 | Story-map by assuming the solution exists and mapping what users do to get value, sequencing steps along the successful path. | CDH |
| P023 | Make work visible on a big, transparent wall of sticky notes so developers and stakeholders can participate. | USM |
| P028 | Stop perfecting documents and get together to tell stories to build shared understanding; the goal is the best solution, not correct requirements. | USM |
| P034 | There is no single right story size — the right size is the one relevant to the current conversation. | USM |
| P050 | Organize a map with a backbone of big steps read left-to-right, with persona thumbnails above it to track who you are discussing. | USM |
| P070 | Use the "As a… I want… so that…" template only to start conversations, never as a specification. | USM |
| P071 | Crowds do not collaborate; let members opt in and use a fishbowl pattern when everyone wants in. | USM |
| P101 | Run story workshops well — three to five people, dive into who/how/what, agree on done, speak in examples, and split oversized stories. | USM |

## Shaping and betting work

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P038 | Scope by appetite, not estimate: fix the time an idea is worth, treat time as fixed and scope as variable, and use appetite as a creative constraint. | ShapeUp |
| P051 | Organize work by the structure of the project into independently finishable scopes, not by person or role. | ShapeUp |
| P062 | When finding solution elements, move fast, explore many directions, and use words-not-pictures notation to stay at the right abstraction. | ShapeUp |
| P063 | Shape at the right level of abstraction — avoid over-specified mockups too early and avoid vague words that block trade-offs. | ShapeUp |
| P067 | Eliminate Big Design Up Front and put speed first; use the fastest early artifact and reserve polish for the visual-design stage. | LeanUX |
| P104 | Before writing a pitch, walk the concept past technical experts as "just an idea," ask if it is possible within the appetite, and hunt for time bombs. | ShapeUp |
| P105 | Write every pitch with problem, appetite, solution, rabbit holes, and no-gos, always pairing a problem with its solution. | ShapeUp |
| P106 | Decide when to stop by comparing down to the baseline, not up to an ideal — judge good-enough against how customers cope today. | ShapeUp |

## Empowered product teams and leadership

| ID | Principle (paraphrased) | Predominant source |
|----|-------------------------|--------------------|
| P006 | Manage stakeholders and dependencies with proactive, outcome-framed communication, since small-batch planning and no roadmap remove coordination tools. | LeanUX |
| P019 | Grant capable teams autonomy aligned to strategic intent; align every level on the why and let the next layer decide the how. | EBT |
| P021 | Lead as a product manager through influence, not authority; a PM is not a mini-CEO. | EBT |
| P042 | Hire product managers for hard-to-teach traits (passion, empathy, intelligence, work ethic, integrity, confidence); skills can be learned. | Inspired |
| P045 | After a cycle, hold a team-only review of the product, the plan, and the process before widening the audience. | USM |
| P082 | Structure product teams around value streams or strategic goals, not technical components or individual features. | EBT |
| P089 | Make the PM's job to produce value, not push personal ideas; stay humble, leverage the team, and treat beliefs as assumptions to validate. | EBT |
| P091 | Organize teams around outcome goals with clear ownership; keep the number small to force prioritization and create knowledge redundancy. | EBT |
| P097 | Escaping the build trap requires organizational support (culture, policies, structure), not just process and strategy. | EBT |
| P098 | Secure leadership buy-in to outcomes; transformations fail when leaders say they want results but still measure features. | EBT |
| P103 | After the team review, widen to a stakeholder review that connects the work to the bigger picture and covers discovery and delivery. | USM |
