# Executive firing root-cause analysis

## Purpose

Diagnose *why* a senior executive must be let go before the firing happens, and
run the separation so it treats the person fairly and improves the company. The
source's premise is that, at the executive level, the firing is almost never a
pure performance failure by an individual who slipped through hiring — at this
level companies screen for skill, motivation, and track record. The usual real
cause is a hiring or integration decision by the CEO. Skipping the root-cause
step means repeating the same hire.

## When to use

- A founder-CEO has concluded a senior executive is not working out and asks how
  to proceed.
- The CEO needs to separate the *decision* (the firing is justified) from the
  *diagnosis* (what actually went wrong) so the next hire does not fail the same
  way.
- The CEO needs the firing sequenced so the board is informed, the conversation
  preserves dignity, and the company communication is accurate and respectful.

Do not use for a layoff (company-wide, financially driven — see
`references/layoff-execution-checklist.md`) or for non-executive performance
management.

## Procedure

This is a four-step process. Steps run in order; do not begin the conversation
(Step 3) before the board is informed (Step 2).

1. **Root-cause analysis — diagnose before deciding how to communicate.**
   Treat the firing as the result of a *prior* decision, not an executive
   failure. Work through the common real causes:
   - **Hired for a generic position.** There is no great head of sales in the
     abstract — only a great head of sales for *this* company over the next
     12–24 months. The role at a 25-person startup is not the role at a large
     incumbent. Test whether the executive was hired against the company's
     actual stage-specific needs.
   - **Wrong kind of ambition.** Ambition for personal advancement over company
     success causes the organization to reject the executive despite their
     skills. Check whether the misfit was a values/ambition mismatch.
   - **Failed integration.** A new senior hire faces quick judgment, undefined
     scope, and mismatched expectations. Ask whether the company gave the
     executive a real integration plan.
   - **Scaling.** When headcount multiplies, the management job becomes a
     brand-new job; the old job and the new job are not the same. The executive
     may simply have outgrown — or been outgrown by — the role.
   Name which cause(s) apply. The diagnosis feeds back into the hiring and
   integration process so the failure is not repeated.

2. **Inform the board before acting.** Walk each board member through the
   root-cause finding and get input and approval for the separation package.
   Executive packages are larger than standard severance — it takes far longer
   for an executive to find a comparable role. Do this through individual
   conversations, not a dramatic reveal in a board meeting, especially where a
   board member introduced the executive. Finalize details only once everyone
   has agreed individually.

3. **Prepare for the conversation.** Once the cause is known and the board is
   informed, tell the executive as quickly as possible. Script or rehearse what
   you will say so you do not misspeak — the executive will remember this
   conversation for a very long time. Review prior performance reviews so your
   account is consistent with what the person was previously told. Be direct and
   decisive; do not soften the decision into ambiguity.

4. **Prepare the company communication.** Preserve the reputation of the fired
   executive. The failure was very likely a team effort; portray it that way.
   Trashing a former report does not make the CEO look good and erodes board
   confidence — and it is the fair and decent thing to do. The announcement must
   be accurate and respectful.

## Inputs

- Identity, tenure, and role of the executive, and their relationship to the CEO.
- Company stage and headcount now versus when the executive was hired.
- Any prior performance reviews or documented expectations.
- Board composition, including who (if anyone) sourced the executive.

## Output

- A named root cause (generic hire / wrong ambition / failed integration /
  scaling) with the evidence for it.
- A sequenced plan: board notification approach + approved separation package,
  a rehearsed conversation script, and a respectful company announcement.
- An explicit handback: the CEO owns the final decision; legal counsel should
  review the separation agreement and severance terms before anything is
  finalized.
