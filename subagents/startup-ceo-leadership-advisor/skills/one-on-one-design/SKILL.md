# One-on-one design

## Purpose

Design a recurring one-on-one so that the best ideas, the biggest problems, and
the most intense employee-life issues actually reach the people who can deal with
them. The source's core design point is that the one-on-one is the *employee's*
meeting, not the manager's status update — the agenda belongs to the employee
and the manager mostly listens and draws issues out. This is the practical
mechanism behind the agent's people-first priority (take care of the people, then
the products, then the profits).

## When to use

- A CEO or manager wants a repeatable structure for surfacing problems before
  they become crises.
- Information is not flowing up — the CEO learns about issues too late.
- Managing introverted reports (e.g. engineers) whose concerns must be actively
  drawn out.

## Procedure

1. **Hand the agenda to the employee.** The employee, not the manager, sets the
   agenda and does most of the talking. The manager's job is to listen and to
   draw out the key issues — the more introverted the report, the more important
   this becomes.

2. **Use open questions to draw issues out.** When the employee is quiet, prompt
   with questions that invite candor rather than yes/no answers, such as:
   - If we could improve in any one way, how would we do it?
   - What is the number-one problem with our organization? Why?
   - What is not fun about working here?
   - Who is really excelling? Whom do you admire?
   - If you were me, what changes would you make?
   - What don't you like about the product?
   - What is the biggest opportunity we are missing?
   - What are we not doing that we should be doing?
   - Are you happy working here?

3. **Make feedback a dialogue, not a monologue.** Even as CEO, assume the
   employee knows their function and has more data than you — you may be wrong.
   The goal is to open discussion up, not close it down: encourage people to
   challenge your judgment and argue to a conclusion. Be direct but not mean —
   watered-down feedback ("it's good, just tighten the conclusion" when you mean
   "I couldn't follow it") is worse than none because it is deceptive.

4. **Route what surfaces to whoever can act on it.** The point of the meeting is
   not the meeting; it is that the surfaced ideas, problems, and life issues get
   to the people who can deal with them. Close each one-on-one knowing what you
   will carry forward and to whom.

## Inputs

- The reporting relationship and cadence (recurring, employee-owned slot).
- Knowledge of the report's temperament (how much drawing-out they need).

## Output

- A live list of the report's top problems, ideas, and concerns.
- Concrete follow-ups routed to the people who can act on them.
