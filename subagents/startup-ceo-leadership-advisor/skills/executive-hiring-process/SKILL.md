# Executive hiring process

## Purpose

Give a founder-CEO a structured way to hire a senior executive for a function
the CEO has never personally run. The trap the source warns against is hiring
for the *generic* role — "a great head of sales" — instead of the specific role
this company needs over the next 12–24 months, and hiring for lack of weakness
instead of for the strength that matters most. This skill defines the position,
runs a process that finds the right match, and plans integration.

## When to use

- A CEO must fill a senior role (sales, marketing, engineering, finance) they
  have not held themselves and lacks a built-in standard for evaluating
  candidates.
- The CEO is at risk of defaulting to a "central casting" hire — an impressive
  generalist from a famous company — rather than the right fit for this stage.

Do not use for individual-contributor hiring or for firing/separation (see
`skills/executive-firing-root-cause`).

## Procedure

1. **Set the standard — know what you want before you look.** You cannot run a
   good search for a role whose requirements you cannot articulate.
   - **Act in the role yourself** long enough to learn what it actually demands
     at your stage.
   - **Bring in domain experts.** Interview a great head of the function, learn
     what made them great, and identify which of those strengths most directly
     match your company's needs. Include the expert in interviews if possible —
     but do **not** defer the decision to them: they know the function, not your
     company, how it works, or what it needs.
   - **Be explicit about expectations on joining:** what the person does in the
     first 30 days, what their motivation for joining should be, and whether you
     want them building a large org immediately or hiring just one or two people
     over the next year.

2. **Run a process that finds the right match.** Translate the standard into
   selection criteria.
   - **Write down the strengths you want and the weaknesses you are willing to
     tolerate.** Hiring for strength means accepting that a candidate strong
     where it counts will have weaknesses elsewhere; the failure mode is
     screening for absence-of-weakness and getting a candidate with no sharp
     edges.
   - Cover completeness across the subdivisions the source uses, e.g.: will the
     executive be world-class at *running the function*? Are they *outstanding
     operationally* (managing direct reports, decision-making, core processes
     such as performance management, employee integration, metric and org
     design)? Can they *work effectively with peers* on your staff?
   - Use the domain expert and structured interview questions to test against
     the written criteria, not against a generic résumé impression.

3. **Integrate the hire.** A senior hire arrives to quick judgment from existing
   employees, possibly mismatched expectations, and a largely undefined job.
   Build and run an explicit integration plan — onboarding, context handoff,
   early wins, and a clear definition of the role — because failed integration
   is one of the most common reasons an otherwise capable executive is fired
   later. Review and improve this plan continually.

## Inputs

- The function to hire for and the company's stage / headcount.
- The CEO's articulated needs for the role over the next 12–24 months.
- Access to at least one domain expert in the function.
- The written strengths-wanted / tolerable-weaknesses list.

## Output

- A role definition specific to this company and stage (not a generic spec).
- A scored shortlist against the written strength/weakness criteria.
- A 30/60/90-style integration plan with an explicit early-mandate (build big
  vs. hire one or two).
