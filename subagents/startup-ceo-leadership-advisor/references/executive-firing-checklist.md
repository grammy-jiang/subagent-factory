# Executive firing checklist

Firing an executive correctly is mostly preparation. The act itself is often
easier than firing an individual contributor — executives are professional and
have been on the other side — but doing it *correctly* matters because if you do
not learn the right lessons, you will be firing again soon. Four steps, in order.

| # | Step | Checklist |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Root-cause analysis** | ☐ Reject "the executive sucks" as the default explanation. ☐ Identify the real cause: hired for a generic role / wrong kind of ambition / failed integration / outgrown by scaling. ☐ Feed the finding back into the hiring and integration process so the failure is not repeated. |
| 2 | **Inform the board before acting** | ☐ Walk each member through the root cause individually (phone calls, not a board-meeting reveal). ☐ Get input and approval for the separation package (executive packages are larger — it takes ~10× longer for an executive to find a new role). ☐ Be especially careful where a board member introduced the executive. ☐ Finalize only after everyone agrees individually. |
| 3 | **Prepare for the conversation** | ☐ Tell the executive as quickly as possible once the board knows. ☐ Script or rehearse so you do not misspeak — they will remember this for a long time. ☐ Review prior performance reviews for consistency. ☐ Be direct and decisive; no ambiguity. |
| 4 | **Prepare the company communication** | ☐ Portray the failure as the team effort it likely was; do not trash the person. ☐ Keep the announcement accurate and respectful. ☐ Preserve the fired executive's reputation. |

## Guardrails

- **Dignity is non-negotiable** — acknowledge contributions, no disparagement.
- **Do not bypass due process** — board notification, root-cause analysis,
  severance preparation, and dignified communication are required steps.
- **Legal review** — separation agreement, severance terms, and equity treatment
  should be reviewed by counsel before anything is finalized.
