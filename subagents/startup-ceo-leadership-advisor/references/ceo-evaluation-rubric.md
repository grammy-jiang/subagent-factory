# CEO evaluation rubric

A rubric for judging a CEO (or a CEO-level candidate) along three questions. A
great CEO needs all three; most are stronger on one than the others. Internal
candidates tend to outperform external ones because *knowledge* — of technology,
prior decisions, culture, and people — is harder to acquire than the skill of
running a larger organization.

| # | Question | What to assess | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Does the CEO know what to do?** | **Strategy** (the story and the strategy are the same thing — the proper output of strategy work is the *story*: why this company should exist, why people should join, buy, invest) and **decision-making** (the speed *and* quality of decisions). Interpret broadly: personnel, financing, product, goal-sizing, marketing. | Sets the wrong direction, or cannot articulate a story that gives every employee context. |
| 2 | **Can the CEO get the company to do what she knows?** | The skill of building a well-run organization: organizational design, performance management, incentive structure, and the communication architecture that drives every employee. | This is where most CEOs "fail to scale." Very few earn an A here. |
| 3 | **Did the CEO achieve results against an appropriate set of objectives?** | Results — but first verify the *objectives* were correct. Strong board-managers can "succeed" by setting objectives artificially low; CEOs who ignore board management can "fail" by setting them too high. Early on, the true size of the opportunity is unknown, so objectives mislead. | Judging results without first checking the objectives were set correctly. |

## Notes on the Ones/Twos lens

The source splits CEOs into **Ones** (happiest setting direction — knowing what
to do) and **Twos** (happiest making the company perform — getting it done).
Most CEOs are Ones, since the org hierarchy exists for decision-making
efficiency. Use questions 1 and 2 to locate where a CEO or candidate sits and
where they will need complementary support (e.g. a One CEO typically staffs Twos
and Functional Ones around them).
