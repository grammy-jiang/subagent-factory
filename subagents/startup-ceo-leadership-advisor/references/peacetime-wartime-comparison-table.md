# Peacetime vs. wartime CEO comparison

Peacetime and wartime require radically different management styles. **Peacetime**
exists when a company has a large advantage in a strong, growing market and the
job is to *expand* that advantage. **Wartime** exists when an existential threat —
a competitor, a market shift, a macro shock — could destroy the company, and the
job is to *survive* it. The mode is set by the company's situation, not the CEO's
preference. Most management writing describes peacetime; the contrast below maps
the dimensions where wartime inverts the standard advice.

| Dimension | Peacetime CEO | Wartime CEO |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | Expand the market; grow the existing advantage | Survive a threat that could bankrupt the company |
| Buy-in | Strives for broad-based buy-in | Neither indulges consensus building nor tolerates disagreement |
| Goals | Sets big, hairy, audacious goals | Too busy fighting the threat for grand goal-setting |
| Training | Trains employees for satisfaction and career development | Trains employees to survive the fight |
| Rules / portfolio | Can afford rules like "exit any business not #1 or #2" | Often has no #1 or #2 business; cannot afford that luxury |
| Protocol | Follows established management protocol | Breaks protocol when survival demands it |
| Public conduct | Never embarrasses an employee publicly | Will violate that norm under pressure (e.g. Grove's "you are wasting my time") |
| Deviation tolerance | Tolerates deviation from the plan in service of creativity | Demands strict alignment and urgency |

## How to use this table

- **First, classify the situation, not the person.** Is the dominant force a
  growth opportunity (peace) or an existential threat (war)? Calibrate every
  recommendation to that mode.
- **A mode is temporary.** Companies move between peace and war; the same CEO may
  need both. Mastery means knowing the management rules *and* knowing when to
  follow them and when to violate them — a CEO strong in one mode is not
  automatically strong in the other.
- **Do not apply wartime tactics in peacetime or vice versa.** Wartime
  intensity in a peacetime market burns the team out; peacetime consensus in a
  wartime crisis is too slow to survive.
