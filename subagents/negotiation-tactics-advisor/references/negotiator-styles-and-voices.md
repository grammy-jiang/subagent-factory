# Negotiator styles and voices

Negotiators tend toward one of three style types. Each reads the same move
differently, so the technique sequence must adapt to the counterpart's style
(PRIN-010, CL047).

## Style taxonomy

| Style | Core orientation | Tells | What they need | Risks / cautions |
|-------|------------------|-------|----------------|------------------|
| Analyst | Methodical, data-driven, deliberate | Hates surprises; needs time to process; treats silence as thinking, not discomfort | Data, preparation, and room to reflect; no ambush | Reads pressure as bad faith; rushing them backfires (CL048) |
| Accommodator | Relationship above outcome | Agrees readily, warm, sociable | Genuine rapport — but probe past easy agreement | Makes empty promises to keep the peace → risk of non-delivery (CL049) |
| Assertive | Time equals money; wants to be heard first | Direct, blunt, talks before listening | To feel heard before any redirection | Will not absorb your point until theirs is acknowledged (CL050) |

## Adaptation checklist

**Analyst**

- Bring data and a prepared case; avoid surprises.
- Let silence sit — they are thinking, not stalling.
- Give them time to process; do not force a same-meeting decision.

**Accommodator**

- Build the relationship, but do not mistake easy agreement for commitment.
- Probe for genuine commitment beneath the readiness to agree (test for a
  "That's right", not a "You're right").
- Confirm concrete follow-through to guard against empty promises.

**Assertive**

- Validate their point explicitly *before* any redirection.
- Use direct mirroring — they respond to it well.
- Let them be heard first; only then introduce your position.

## Voice

Across all styles, a calm, downward-inflecting "late-night FM DJ" voice de-escalates;
a direct, assertive voice is reserved for the Assertive counterpart who expects it.
Match tone to style rather than defaulting to one register.
