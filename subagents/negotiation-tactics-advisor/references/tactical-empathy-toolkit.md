# Tactical empathy toolkit

The core listening-and-acknowledgement techniques of FBI-derived tactical-empathy
negotiation. Tactical empathy — deliberately understanding and articulating the
counterpart's perspective and feelings — is the foundational skill; because people
run primarily on fast emotional (System 1) thinking, acknowledging emotion outweighs
logical argument (PRIN-001, CL001–CL002). Every row below is a way to do that.

## Technique table

| Technique | What it is | How to deliver | Effect | Principle / Claim |
|-----------|------------|----------------|--------|-------------------|
| Mirror | Repeat the last 1–3 critical words the counterpart said | As a question, rising inflection; then hold silence ≥ 4 seconds | Triggers elaboration and more disclosure; builds rapport via implicit agreement | PRIN-004 / CL007–CL009 |
| Label | Name the counterpart's emotion as an observation | Opener "It seems like… / It sounds like… / It looks like…", then go silent | Negative emotion named → intensity drops; positive emotion named → amplified | PRIN-002 / CL011–CL014 |
| Accusation audit | Voice the counterpart's likely negative assumptions first | Label framing, before any number or term | Defuses objections pre-emptively; counterpart reassures rather than attacks | PRIN-003 / CL015–CL016, CL030 |
| Elicit "No" | Frame openers so a "No" still opens the conversation | e.g. "Is now a bad time to talk?" instead of "Do you have a few minutes?" | "No" gives safety, security, control → more engagement; it is the start, not the end | PRIN-005 / CL017–CL019 |
| Reach "That's right" | Combine a label with a summary paraphrase of the counterpart's key concerns | Summarise their position, then pause | Marks the moment they feel fully heard — the genuine breakthrough signal | PRIN-006 / CL023–CL024 |

## Label openers

Use observational framing, never "I" framing:

- "It seems like…"
- "It sounds like…"
- "It looks like…"

Deliver, then stop talking — silence does the work (CL014).

## The three Yeses, and what to listen for

| Affirmation | What it really means | Use it? |
|-------------|----------------------|---------|
| Counterfeit Yes | Said to escape pressure or end the exchange | No — false signal |
| Confirmation Yes | Agreement in principle, no commitment to act | Weak — not durable |
| Commitment Yes | A genuine, binding promise to act | The real goal |
| "That's right" | Felt understanding — the breakthrough | The marker to pursue |
| "You're right" | Appeasement to end the conversation; a dead end | Treat as a warning sign |

Sources: CL022, CL023, CL025.

## "No" reframe checklist

- Treat "No" as the starting point of substantive discussion, not the close (CL018).
- Design openers so a "No" answer still opens the conversation (CL019).
- A counterpart who can safely say "No" feels in control and engages more (CL017).
