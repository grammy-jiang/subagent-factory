# Ackerman bargaining and anchoring

## Purpose

Structure a series of monetary offers so the counterpart is walked toward your
target while believing they have squeezed you to a hard limit. The Ackerman model
sets the offer schedule; emotional anchoring (an accusation audit before the first
number) prepares the counterpart to receive it (PRIN-008; PRIN-003, CL030).

## When to use

- Any negotiation involving a sequence of monetary offers where you are the
  offering party — salary, purchasing, deal structuring.

Do **not** apply the full schedule when the counterpart is making all the offers and
you are purely reacting, or in a single-offer, take-it-or-leave-it situation, where a
staged concession sequence signals weakness rather than calculated precision.

## Procedure

1. **Set your target first.** Decide the number you actually want before any offer
   leaves your mouth — the whole schedule is computed backward from it.
2. **Anchor the emotion before the number.** Run an accusation audit naming the
   negatives the counterpart is likely to feel about the figure ("This is going to
   sound low…") so the number lands against a prepared emotional state (CL030).
   See `skills/labeling-and-accusation-audit`.
3. **Open at 65% of target.** This extreme anchor sets the range and gives room to
   move (CL052).
4. **Climb in shrinking increments — 85%, 95%, 100%.** Each raise is smaller than
   the last. The diminishing steps signal you are nearing a firm ceiling, not that
   you have unlimited room (CL052).
5. **Make the final offer a precise, non-round number.** Not 100,000 but 98,750 —
   a calculated-looking figure reads as your true limit rather than a convenient
   round one (CL053).
6. **Attach a small nonmonetary concession to the last offer.** Add an item of
   little direct cost to you. It signals no money remains and produces a
   "you got everything I have" closure (CL054).
7. **Use calibrated questions between rounds.** When the counterpart pushes, answer
   with "How am I supposed to do that?" rather than simply conceding (see
   `skills/calibrated-questions-and-illusion-of-control`).

## Inputs

- Your target price and your walk-away limit.
- The counterpart's likely emotional reaction to the first number.
- A low-cost nonmonetary item available to concede at the close.

## Output

- A concrete four-step offer schedule (65 / 85 / 95 / 100 percent of target) with
  the actual figures filled in.
- A precise non-round final number plus the nonmonetary concession to pair with it.
- A short accusation-audit line to deliver before the first offer.
