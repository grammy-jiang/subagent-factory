# Black Swan and leverage discovery

## Purpose

Find the hidden information and the form of leverage that decide a negotiation before
the tactics do. **Black Swans** are unknown unknowns — facts one party holds that the
other does not even know to ask about — which, once surfaced, can instantly transform
leverage and the set of possible outcomes (PRIN-009, CL055). Alongside the hunt for
Black Swans, assess which of the three forms of leverage you actually hold (CL056).

## When to use

- During preparation and throughout any negotiation where the counterpart's full
  constraints, interests, or authority are unknown.
- When the counterpart's behaviour looks inexplicable or unreasonable.
- When you need to understand your real leverage position (compare mode).

Do **not** keep probing when leverage and the counterpart's constraints are already
fully mapped and no plausible hidden information remains.

## Procedure

1. **Assume a Black Swan exists.** Treat unknown unknowns as present by default and
   actively probe for them rather than waiting for them to surface (CL055). Use
   open calibrated questions, unguarded moments, and face-to-face cues to draw them
   out.
2. **Diagnose apparent irrationality before reacting.** When the counterpart seems
   irrational, run the diagnostic: are they **ill-informed**, **secretly
   constrained**, or **operating under hidden interests**? "Crazy" is almost never
   the real answer — each of the three points at a Black Swan to find (CL060).
3. **Inventory your three leverage types** (CL056):
   - **Positive** — your ability to give the counterpart something they want.
   - **Negative** — your ability to cause them loss or harm if no deal is reached.
   - **Normative** — the counterpart's own stated standards and norms.
4. **Apply normative leverage by exposing inconsistency.** Show where the
   counterpart's current position contradicts a norm or standard they themselves
   profess. The gap between their stated values and their behaviour creates social
   pressure to align (CL057).
5. **Re-evaluate leverage as Black Swans surface.** Each newly revealed fact can
   shift which leverage type is strongest; reassess after every disclosure.

## Inputs

- What is known and, explicitly, what is unknown about the counterpart's situation,
  desires, fears, and constraints.
- The counterpart's stated standards, public commitments, or professed norms.
- Any behaviour that currently reads as irrational or inexplicable.

## Output

- A list of candidate Black Swans to probe for, with the question or moment that
  could surface each.
- A leverage assessment across positive, negative, and normative types, naming which
  you hold and how strong it is.
- For an apparently irrational counterpart, the ill-informed / constrained / hidden-
  interest diagnosis and what it implies.
