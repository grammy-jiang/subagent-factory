# Calibrated questions and the illusion of control

## Purpose

Direct a counterpart's thinking toward their constraints, priorities, and hidden
interests by asking open **How** and **What** questions. These give the counterpart
the illusion of control — they feel in charge because they are doing the talking —
while the question quietly steers them toward the information or the work you need
(PRIN-007, CL039). The same family of questions also lets you decline a demand
without direct refusal.

## When to use

- You are gathering information about the counterpart's constraints, authority, or
  real interests.
- You need to say no to a demand while keeping the relationship intact and the
  counterpart engaged.
- You are drafting a question script for any mode (advise, review, compare).

Do **not** force an open question when a direct closed question is the right tool —
when you need to confirm a binary, logistical fact carrying no emotional charge.
With an Assertive-style counterpart, validate their point explicitly first; open
questions land badly before they feel heard (see `negotiator-styles-and-voices`).

## Procedure

1. **Convert every Why into a What or How.** "Why" reads as accusatory in nearly
   every language and triggers defensiveness (CL040). Recast it: "Why did you do
   that?" → "What led to that decision?" / "How did that come about?".
2. **Open the question with How or What.** These force the counterpart to think and
   to reveal constraints and priorities rather than answer yes/no. Examples:
   - "How am I supposed to do that?"
   - "What about this is important to you?"
   - "What's the biggest challenge you face here?"
   - "How can we solve this together?"
3. **Use "How am I supposed to do that?" as a face-saving No.** It declines a demand
   by handing the problem back to the counterpart, making them engage with your
   constraints instead of you refusing outright (CL041). It signals you cannot
   comply without sounding like a flat rejection.
4. **Stay quiet after asking.** The question only works if the counterpart fills the
   space with thinking and information. Resist answering your own question.
5. **Chain questions toward implementation.** Successive calibrated questions can
   walk the counterpart into solving your problem for you, because they keep the
   sense of control while their answers narrow the options.

## Inputs

- The information you still need from the counterpart (constraints, authority,
  priorities, interests).
- Any demand you need to decline without confrontation.
- The counterpart's style, if known, so you can sequence validation before open
  questions where required.

## Output

- A short set of calibrated How/What questions tailored to the situation, each with
  the constraint or interest it is designed to surface.
- Where a refusal is needed, a "How am I supposed to do that?"-style calibrated No.
- Any Why questions in a draft script flagged and rewritten as What/How.
