# Presentation-Design Principles Index

Package-wide index of every promoted principle, grouped by the skill that owns it. Each entry restates the principle's operative core; the full statement lives in `../principles/principles.yaml`.

## Assertion-Evidence Slide Structure

Skill: `assertion-evidence-slide-structure`

- **P014** — Apply the assertion-evidence structure as a pair of substitutions.
- **P015** — Follow the headline rules concretely.
- **P021** — Recognise the ease of bulleted lists as their real danger.
- **P044** — Judge a headline type by which one leads the speaker to prepare a more focused talk with fewer words overall and which better re-orients a listener who missed the transition onto the slide.
- **P045** — Reject the bulleted list on three grounds at once.
- **P046** — Present the evidence before the assertion in three situations.
- **P047** — Treat the comprehension advantage of assertion-evidence slides as measured rather than asserted.
- **P069** — Never project an assertion with no visual evidence at all, even though showing evidence before the assertion is legitimate.
- **P070** — Expect the structure's largest effect on preparation rather than delivery.
- **P071** — Expect crafting the sentence-assertion headline to be the hardest part of the work.
- **P077** — Diagnose a failed persuasive slide by its missing assertion.
- **P096** — Expect assertion-evidence slides to improve delivery in three specific ways.

## Slide Density And Signal-To-Noise

Skill: `slide-density-and-signal-to-noise`

- **P012** — Reduce an overloaded slide by asking what can be removed without changing the meaning and where the content can be split.
- **P018** — Keep the word count low enough to serve only as a mnemonic the presenter is comfortable delivering.
- **P019** — Strip every phrase and decorative image that does not connect directly to the spoken content.
- **P025** — Measure a slide against projected words per minute rather than against a per-slide word rule.
- **P026** — Give the presenter structure without a script.
- **P033** — Keep the balance between what is shown and what is said.
- **P073** — Split content across slides and reveal the pieces in sequence whenever secondary or technical detail competes with the slide's single main message, keeping on the slide only what is addressed.
- **P080** — Design slides for the audience rather than as the speaker's notes.
- **P081** — Treat TED-style simplicity as engineered rather than effortless.
- **P084** — Hold any grouping on a slide to four items or fewer.
- **P097** — Reveal content progressively and hide each element until it is referred.
- **P101** — Keep the balance between saying and showing.

## Visual Evidence, Analogies And Graphics

Skill: `visual-evidence-analogies-and-graphics`

- **P001** — Start persuasion by recognising exactly what is being asserted and by fixing the main assertion the data supports before deciding how to graph or present it.
- **P002** — Replace a bulleted list with the visual form of the relationship it actually holds.
- **P034** — Choose the type of visual evidence from the assertion it must support.
- **P042** — Anchor any concept the audience does not know in a specific physical example or analogy rather than an abstract or mathematical treatment.
- **P078** — Use analogies to convey how something works, how large it is, or how likely an unfamiliar event is.
- **P091** — Build a chain of evidenced sub-assertions when the main assertion sits too far up the mountain for one graph or dataset to reach, fitting each sub-assertion's evidence to its kind of claim.
- **P100** — Use animation to control the order in which a slide is taken.
- **P102** — Put a short reference listing.
- **P103** — Use Prezi's zoomable single canvas for explaining a whole system divided into parts.

## Typography, Colour And Slide Layout

Skill: `typography-colour-and-slide-layout`

- **P004** — Open blank space between a slide's elements.
- **P007** — Force a large minimum type size.
- **P008** — Keep logos secondary rather than putting one on every slide.
- **P011** — Verify the colour palette by projecting it in the room rather than judging it on the authoring screen.
- **P017** — Treat the presentation software's defaults as an unvalidated inheritance rather than guidance.
- **P023** — Set layout to guide the eye.
- **P049** — Boldface slide type, especially for a larger room, and avoid italics and underlining.
- **P098** — Choose the typeface by reading speed rather than tradition.
- **P099** — Fix the background colour before the rest of the palette, choosing dark or light from the formality of the event and the venue size.

## Story Structure And The Big Idea

Skill: `story-structure-and-the-big-idea`

- **P005** — Do not open presentation software during idea generation; collect and create content first, keep generating along a theme until possibilities are exhausted, and expect the genuinely clever ideas.
- **P035** — Settle the story before opening the slide software.
- **P041** — Use stories for two specific jobs.
- **P059** — Land the ending on a higher plane than the beginning.
- **P060** — Write the big idea — the single controlling message — as a complete sentence that articulates the presenter's unique point of view and conveys what is at stake; a topic is not a big idea, and using.
- **P064** — Maintain a personal catalogue of stories indexed by emotion, recalling them not only by life chronology but by people, places and things, sketching the scene to surface dormant memories and recording.
- **P087** — Open by stating what is.
- **P116** — Make the call to adventure an explicit, memorable big idea conveying what could be, delivered at the moment the audience first sees the stark contrast with what is; the turning point must not be.
- **P118** — Make quantity the objective of ideation.

## Audience Analysis And Persona Design

Skill: `audience-analysis-and-persona-design`

- **P013** — Abandon the goal of satisfying a mixed audience throughout the whole talk.
- **P056** — Build the presentation around what the audience needs and will do rather than around the presenter's agenda.
- **P057** — Never assume the audience has kept up with the presenter's field.
- **P058** — Cast the audience as the hero and the presenter as the mentor.
- **P061** — Answer the seven audience questions and build one persona per distinct audience segment before any slide is built, keeping the persona slide at the front of the deck as private working context.
- **P063** — Check a talk against three traits rather than one.
- **P075** — Test each detail of a known audience's talk against two questions.
- **P114** — Discharge the mentor's two duties.
- **P119** — Calibrate to the audience's tolerance in both directions.
- **P120** — Locate content on the head-heart-gut-groin spectrum and correct in whichever direction the presenter defaults.

## Persuasion — Ethos, Pathos And Logos

Skill: `persuasion-ethos-pathos-and-logos`

- **P006** — Account for all three Aristotelian appeals.
- **P030** — Pair facts with emotional appeal rather than choosing between them.
- **P038** — Assess the audience's prior bias first.
- **P040** — Engineer contrast against the audience's own environment and expectations.
- **P068** — Use the persuasive power of presentations to build up rather than deceive.
- **P092** — Build the character appeal deliberately.
- **P113** — Treat the presentation as a signal passing through sender, transmission, reception and receiver, where distortion can enter at any step; minimize credibility, semantic, experiential and bias noise at.
- **P115** — Build common ground from shared experiences and shared goals, and reveal qualifications humbly as evidence of a similar journey completed.
- **P117** — Describe a reward that makes the action worthwhile and make the payoff obvious.

## Talk Organisation, Transitions And Emphasis

Skill: `talk-organisation-transitions-and-emphasis`

- **P037** — Emphasise deliberately.
- **P039** — Design against the three specific ways audiences get lost.
- **P043** — Signal every transition through at least one of three channels.
- **P067** — Identify the message for every scene before the talk whether or not it will appear on the slide.
- **P076** — Map the talk explicitly.
- **P082** — Deliver satisfying depth inside a deliberately broad talk rather than avoiding breadth.
- **P083** — Design structure on four levers, any one of which can fail a talk.
- **P086** — Select, sort, and rank the details.
- **P089** — Do not enter the middle of a talk until the audience understands why the subject matters.
- **P090** — Make the map memorable by integrating the divisions into a single image that also supplies the background, or, failing that, by anchoring each topic with a representative image.

## Opening, Closing And Framing Slides

Skill: `opening-closing-and-framing-slides`

- **P022** — Run a closing sequence that gives the audience time to compose questions.
- **P024** — Make the final projected slide summarise the talk's most important takeaway.
- **P032** — Include title, outline, and conclusion slides only where they earn their place.
- **P050** — Make the title slide orient rather than announce.
- **P085** — Spend more than twenty or thirty seconds on the title slide and answer at least one of the audience's opening questions before the first slide change.
- **P112** — Plant a handful of succinct, repeatable sound bites so the audience can recall and transfer the message, coordinating key phrases word-for-word with the press release and preparing at least one.

## Rehearsal And Extemporaneous Delivery

Skill: `rehearsal-and-extemporaneous-delivery`

- **P016** — Accept that no single delivery style is correct.
- **P020** — Walk through the whole presentation beforehand even in a mumble.
- **P052** — Practise until the talk can be given with no notes by memorising its organisation.
- **P054** — Keep memorising the actual words the exception rather than the standard.
- **P066** — Account for the audience and the room when judging a delivery.
- **P072** — Let the visual evidence alone trigger what to say.
- **P079** — Require three things of the speaker in a strong presentation.
- **P094** — Answer the risk of groping for a word with repeated practice rather than with a script.
- **P095** — Reject memorisation at presentation length.
- **P105** — Treat reading a speech as costly by default.
- **P106** — Take charge of the room as part of delivery.
- **P107** — Direct the audience's attention with the eyes.
- **P108** — Hold composure through anything mid-talk.
- **P110** — Replace the question of how to handle nervousness with the question of how to achieve confidence, whose answer is passion combined with preparation.
- **P111** — Plan several deliberate delivery-mode changes within an hour.

## Questions, Challenge And Composure

Skill: `questions-challenge-and-composure`

- **P029** — Be transparent so the audience can see past the presenter to the idea.
- **P055** — Meet a challenge with one of three tested responses.
- **P093** — Concede publicly when a challenger is right.
- **P109** — Handle a question through an ordered sequence.

## Format Choice And Preparation Planning

Skill: `format-choice-and-preparation-planning`

- **P009** — When the content really is a document, change the format rather than the slides.
- **P010** — Judge a presentation by whether the audience looks enlightened, moved to act, or willing to change behaviour, and get there by reviewing the content through their eyes, showing them something they.
- **P027** — Reserve the Lessig style.
- **P031** — Weigh a presentation against a document on five advantages.
- **P036** — Critique a talk from four separate perspectives.
- **P048** — Plan for slow adoption of a better slide structure.
- **P051** — Use pecha kucha — 20 slides at 20 seconds each on automatic advance — where there is no time to teach a sophisticated structure.
- **P062** — Brief an illustrator with the story to be told rather than with what to draw, give them time for research, ideation, and revision.
- **P065** — Scale preparation time to the stakes and schedule it.
- **P074** — Refuse to treat slides as an extension of the presenter's persona.
- **P088** — Constrain the length hard.

## Equipment, Venue And Contingency

Skill: `equipment-venue-and-contingency`

- **P003** — Blank the screen deliberately with the black or white key, or with an inserted black slide when the keyboard is out of reach.
- **P028** — Never attempt a live demonstration without rehearsing it on the actual equipment.
- **P053** — Imagine the worst compound case once the structure and slides are set and devise a plan for equipment failure.
- **P104** — Remove the dependencies that fail in an unfamiliar room.
