# Opening, Closing And Framing Slides

## Purpose

This skill designs the slides that frame the talk. A title slide orients rather than announces, and the closing sequence decides whether questions arrive at all, while the final slide stays up longest — displayed throughout the questions it invited. Use it to judge which framing slides earn their place and to design the opening and closing around them.

## When to use

- The title slide announces rather than orients, or the opening is spent reading names and
  affiliations before the first slide change (P050, P085).
- Title, outline, or conclusion slides are being added or removed, and whether each earns its
  place in this talk's length is at issue (P032).
- The talk ends abruptly, or the last projected slide is a Thank You slide, a Questions slide, or
  blank while questions run (P022, P024).
- The presentation will be covered by press, filmed, or reacted to publicly, so quotable,
  transferable phrasing must be prepared in advance (P112).

## Procedure

1. Make the title slide orient the audience rather than announce the talk, giving it a relevant
   image so the speaker has several possible entry points — scope, importance, or history — into
   the same talk for different audiences, because a default title-names-institutions slide turns
   the opening into a hurried obligation and leaves the audience disoriented the moment it
   changes (P050).
2. When the talk uses a title slide, hold it for more than twenty or thirty seconds and answer at
   least one of the audience's opening questions before the first slide change, using the key
   image on that slide as the entry point, because reading off title, name, affiliation, date,
   and logos and then switching leaves those questions unanswered while the audience is still
   adjusting to the speaker (P085).
3. Include a title, outline, or conclusion slide only where it earns its place in this talk,
   since each carries organisation rather than content and the shorter the talk the less it is
   needed, weighing whether a research-conference talk needs an outline slide at all or whether a
   blank opening slide would serve better when the audience already holds a concrete image of the
   topic (P032).
4. Run a closing sequence that gives the audience time to compose questions rather than ending
   abruptly: open with a headline beginning "In closing" or "In summary" so they start as the
   slide appears, follow with a strong final sentence, pause for applause with a thank-you to cue
   it if none comes, and only then animate in the word Questions, because an abrupt ending
   produces silence that reads as though nobody understood or cared (P022).
5. Make the final projected slide — the one displayed longest while questions are taken —
   summarise the talk's most important takeaway so the audience has content from which to
   fashion questions, since an empty slide is worse than a blank screen, a Thank You slide wastes
   the screen when voice and expression convey the thanks better, and a Questions slide tells the
   audience what they already know (P024).
6. When the presentation will be covered by press, filmed, or open to public reaction, plant a
   handful of succinct, repeatable sound bites so the audience can recall and transfer the
   message, coordinating key phrases word-for-word with the press release and preparing at least
   one fifteen- to thirty-second message salient enough that a reporter will obviously feature it
   (P112).

## Inputs

- The opening and closing slides as they stand, the talk's length and setting, and whether
  questions, press, or recording follow.
- The reasoning offered for the design or decision under review: what the presentation is meant
  to achieve, what is on the slides now, and any claim about why the current form works.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The title slide carries only the title, author names, affiliations, date, and institutional
  logos with no image, so the opening reads as a hurried roll call rather than an entry point
  into the talk (P050).
- The talk moves off the title slide within a few seconds of starting, before scope, importance,
  or approach has registered, so none of the audience's opening questions have been addressed
  when the first change lands (P085).
- An outline or section-heading slide, or a conclusion slide that only restates the outline,
  appears in a short, single-line-of-reasoning talk out of habit rather than because it adds
  organisation the audience needs (P032).
- The talk stops on the last content slide, or jumps straight to a Questions slide, with no
  closing headline, summarising sentence, or pause, so the ending lands as sudden silence (P022).
- The slide left on screen during Q&A is a bare Thank You slide, a Questions slide, or a blank or
  black screen, giving the audience nothing to prompt their questions from (P024).
- No memorable, quotable phrase appears anywhere in the deck, notes, or press materials, so a
  reporter or a member of the audience who shares a reaction has nothing coordinated to repeat
  (P112).
