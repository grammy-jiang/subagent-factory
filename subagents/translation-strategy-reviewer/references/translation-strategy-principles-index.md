# Translation Strategy Principles Index

Package-wide index of every promoted principle, grouped by the skill that owns it. Each entry restates the principle's operative core; the full statement lives in `../principles/principles.yaml`.

## Domestication, Foreignization And The Ethics Of Translation

Skill: `domestication-foreignization-and-ethics`

- **P002** — Recognize that translating identity-marked discourse such as 'camp' is target-culture-dependent.
- **P005** — Keep the ethics of the foreign in view.
- **P014** — Treat translation as identity-forming and politically inscriptive.
- **P020** — Orient a translation on the domestication-foreignization axis, where domestication is an ethnocentric, fluent, invisible reduction of the foreign to receiving values that moves the author toward the.
- **P060** — Recognize that the domestic inscription creates a heterogeneous domestic community of interest around a translated text.
- **P061** — Recognize that translating a foreign work into the dominant translation discourse of the target culture is a canonizing gesture that can invert the source's actual significance.
- **P086** — Reject the ethical ideal that an interpreter be a neutral 'pane of glass' relaying ideas without modification.
- **P105** — Recognize that the theorists' strategy orientations align on a single target-oriented versus source-oriented axis.
- **P106** — Translate the ancients and distant sources so as to foreground their exotic, distant character and make them intelligible as foreign rather than modernizing or accommodating them to the present, and.
- **P120** — Understand that an ethics which would preserve the foreignness of the foreign text can only be practiced in domestic terms.
- **P121** — Recognize that the remainder not only inscribes a domestic set of differences but supplies the loss of the foreign-language differences that constituted the text, displacing its historical dimension.
- **P122** — Use a close translation with a targeted remainder to restore a source text's own stylistic and intertextual origins that a free, domesticating translation obscures.
- **P123** — Understand that to translate is to invent for a foreign text new readerships and an imagined community, in Anderson's sense.
- **P139** — Frame translation, with Berman, as the trial of the foreign in a double sense.
- **P140** — Distinguish Foucault's two kinds of translation that map to the non-literary/literary split.

## Ideology, Power And Postcolonial Translation

Skill: `ideology-power-and-postcolonial-translation`

- **P001** — Locate translation within the literary system's two controls.
- **P004** — Read theorists' own agendas critically and detect ideological manipulation in the target text, whether from the translator or from commissioner, editor, or institutional pressure and especially in.
- **P016** — Ground a feminist practice of translation in the double bind rather than the double standard, replacing the oedipal family model of struggle with a model of collaboration that is at once cooperative.
- **P021** — Preserve, with Spivak, the rhetoricity of the language of Third World literatures.
- **P022** — Recognize that a colonial writer's or translator's dilemma is sharpened by diglossia rather than bilingualism.
- **P039** — Recognize that intimacy with the medium reveals a historically and gender-specific tone a distant reading flattens.
- **P048** — Attend to power in cross-cultural translation.
- **P079** — See through the gendered metaphorics of fidelity.
- **P080** — Understand that when a culture's referential language is a foreign one felt to be alienating, translation becomes a re-territorializing operation that does not create a new language but supplants the.
- **P081** — See a linguistic community as a market of symbolic commodities whose vernacular and referential languages carry use-value and exchange-value governed by power relations.
- **P082** — Resist wholesale translation into a hegemonic language that betrays the democratic ideal into the law of the strongest by rendering all Third World literature into a homogenizing 'with-it translatese'.
- **P116** — Follow Spivak in holding that it is not bodies of meaning that are transferred in translation.
- **P128** — Ground socio-political, ethical, or power-focused critique in detailed linguistic analysis of forms and functions before drawing macro-level conclusions.

## Skopos, Function And The Translation Brief

Skill: `skopos-function-and-the-translation-brief`

- **P003** — Treat translation as purpose-driven human interaction that guides cooperation across cultural barriers.
- **P015** — Define target-text function pragmatically from purpose, audience, initiator, translator, and situation; do not infer it from the source text alone.
- **P038** — Make the translation commission an explicit instruction stating both the goal and the conditions for attaining it.
- **P050** — Apply function plus loyalty.
- **P051** — Recognize that most texts are hybrids carrying several coexisting functions, that the target function may legitimately differ from the source function, and that the method depends on far more than.
- **P053** — Let the commissioner's constraints set the macro-level characteristics and overall strategy while the translator decides the micro-level procedures, conditioning translator's notes on genre and skopos.
- **P058** — Understand why mere trans-coding will not produce a serviceable translatum.
- **P073** — Do not let a fulfilled skopos excuse micro-level neglect.
- **P075** — Ground a reflective translation commentary in the translation problem exploration space of purpose or skopos, method, and readership to establish the target norms, capturing them in a translation.
- **P112** — Frame translation, with Vermeer, as a variety of purposeful translational action whose technical aim is the skopos, negotiated with the commissioning client.
- **P145** — Let the purpose or skopos determine the translation.
- **P146** — Apply functionality plus loyalty, treating functionality as the most important criterion but not a licence for anything.
- **P149** — Take Holz-Manttari's translatorial action seriously.
- **P150** — Let the skopos, not the source text or the source's text variety, determine the appropriate text variety of the translatum.

## Text Type And Translation Method

Skill: `text-type-and-translation-method`

- **P026** — Choose translation tactics from the message type, communicative purpose, and intended audience rather than from a single universal method.
- **P054** — Govern translation choices by Nida's three factors.
- **P055** — Describe a natural translation more by what it avoids than by what it states.
- **P071** — Classify document type by communicative purpose before deciding content, structure, and rhetorical strategy.
- **P078** — Apply Reiss's governing thesis that the text type determines the general method of translating while the text variety demands the target language's structural and stylistic conventions.
- **P101** — Recognize a text's type and genre conventions as a prerequisite to translating it.
- **P107** — Use Reiss's core typology, derived from Buhler's three language functions.
- **P108** — When a text mixes types, let the dominant type's translation mode govern all its elements and resolve conflicts by a type-specific priority.
