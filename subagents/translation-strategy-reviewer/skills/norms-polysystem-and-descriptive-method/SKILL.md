# Norms, Polysystem And Descriptive Method

## Purpose

This skill reviews descriptive, norm-oriented, and polysystem claims about translation behaviour. It reconstructs translational norms from patterned behaviour and textual products rather than reading them off or accepting persuasion-biased extratextual statements at face value, treats norms as a graded, historically mobile continuum, judges literary translations as facts of the target system by acceptability rather than adequacy, and lets the polysystem's central-or-peripheral position govern the reading of practice.

## When to use

- A 'norm' claim is being asserted rather than reconstructed from recurrent regularities of behaviour.
- Extratextual statements by translators or critics are being taken at face value as evidence of method.
- A translation is judged for 'adequacy' to the source when acceptability in the receiving system is the descriptive question.
- The polysystem position (central vs peripheral) or the graded, mobile force of a norm needs situating.

## Procedure

1. Before using extratextual norm statements systematically, explicate them precisely, contextualize their cultural function, compare them with one another, and test them against behavior (P027).
2. Weigh translators' self-accounts as evidence of method and stance while allowing for bias and their intuitive voice-led vocabulary, recognizing that much of the labour behind a translation is invisible and that a translation is a (P032).
3. Study translational norms with their two complicating features in mind (P037).
4. Separate preliminary norms from operational norms before testing how selection, directness, and textual decision patterns relate (P042).
5. Treat being a translator as playing a socially allotted role whose prerequisite is acquiring the norms for what is suitable and for manoeuvring among competing constraints (P057).
6. Evaluate translation and translator expertise as culturally situated role performance, not only as bilingual linguistic production (P063).
7. Treat norms as graded and historically mobile constraints whose force may shift, rather than as fixed all-or-nothing rules (P064).
8. Treat translation as more than words on the page, being the product of a process involving human and institutional agents in specific sociocultural, geographical, and historical conditions, and research it through Chesterman's four (P074).
9. Treat literary translations as facts of the target system governed by the norms of a polysystem (P077).
10. Represent norm strength through distributions of group approval, distinguishing basic norms, tendencies, tolerated behavior, and diagnostically symptomatic devices (P087).
11. Apply Chesterman's norms alongside Toury's (P104).
12. Let the polysystem position govern the reading of translation practice, not just status (P110).
13. Reconstruct norms rather than read them off (P111).
14. Treat every translation genre, mode, society, and period as potentially norm-governed while studying the local conditions that shape the norms (P125).
15. Analyze nonconforming translation behavior as possible deviance or innovation without treating it as automatic disproof of an active norm (P126).
16. Account for translators, criticism, ideology, theory, and training institutions as possible influences on norm change while keeping their causal role hedged unless evidence shows more (P127).

## Inputs

- The translation(s) or translation-studies analysis and its declared corpus, norms, and method.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
