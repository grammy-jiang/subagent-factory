# Relevance, Hermeneutics And Literary Strategy

## Purpose

This skill reviews the interpretive, relevance-theoretic, and literary grounding of a strategy. It reduces translation guidance to the search for optimal relevance under the cognitive environment each rule assumes, reads the hermeneutic motion (aggression and incorporation) and the good-utopian acceptance that no perfect translation exists, critiques a literary translation by its concrete textual features and Berman's deforming tendencies, matches a literary text's relationship to its conventions rather than the author's intentions, and treats a translation as a set of strengths open to improvement rather than simply good or bad.

## When to use

- A translation guideline is being applied as an absolute rather than as relevance-relative to a particular readership.
- A literary translation is judged by an abstract fidelity score instead of its concrete features and deforming tendencies.
- The impossibility or the interpretive, appropriative nature of translation (the hermeneutic motion) is denied or over-claimed.
- A translation is graded simply good or bad rather than as strengths and weaknesses open to improvement.

## Procedure

1. Critique a translation by analyzing its concrete textual features (P006).
2. Screen a literary translation for Berman's twelve deforming tendencies (P008).
3. Frame translating, with Levy, both teleologically as communication and pragmatically as a decision process (P056).
4. Adopt the 'good utopian' stance toward an impossible task (P076).
5. Accept that there is no perfect translation, not because a definite set of desiderata cannot all be met but because there is no definite set of desiderata (P083).
6. Treat poetic-aesthetic sources and foregrounding according to whether form is detachable from meaning and whether foregrounding serves expression itself or communicative effect (P089).
7. Rely on the truth-functional core of logic (P099).
8. Recognize the middle two moves of the hermeneutic motion (P109).
9. Read the 'shapelessness' and apparent 'bad writing' of great prose (P113).
10. Reduce all translation principles to a single one (P115).
11. Recognize that literary conventions, unlike linguistic ones, do not invoke the Gricean mechanism, so the form of a novel or sonnet carries no message and there are no 'literary intentions' to read off analogous to literal intentions (P117).
12. Distinguish the two activities 'literary translation' names (P118).
13. Avoid solving ambiguity or historical distance with phrasing that becomes technical, legalistic, anachronistic, or artificially archaic (P124).
14. Treat functional equivalence as a matter of intercultural understanding (P130).
15. Evaluate a translation as a set of strengths and weaknesses open to improvement rather than as simply good or bad, and treat translation strategies as options observed in real practice, not preconceived correct solutions to apply (P131).

## Inputs

- The translation and the interpretive, relevance, or literary-strategy claim at stake.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to critique a translation by analyzing its concrete textual features (P006).
- The analysis fails to screen a literary translation for Berman's twelve deforming tendencies (P008).
- The analysis fails to frame translating, with Levy, both teleologically as communication and pragmatically as a decision process (P056).
- The analysis fails to adopt the 'good utopian' stance toward an impossible task (P076).
- The analysis fails to accept that there is no perfect translation, not because a definite set of desiderata cannot all be met but because there is no definite set of desiderata (P083).
- The analysis fails to treat poetic-aesthetic sources and foregrounding according to whether form is detachable from meaning and whether foregrounding serves expression itself or communicative (P089).
- The analysis fails to rely on the truth-functional core of logic (P099).
