# Technical Translation, Usability And Design

## Purpose

This skill reviews scientific and technical translation as the production of a usable target document. It lets target-user needs, tasks, and cognitive demands govern; treats layout, typography, white space, and graphics as communicative usability factors, not decoration; measures usability by effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction; and holds safety-critical and regulated content to the standardized severity hierarchy, the official set phrases, and country-of-sale documentation regulation.

## When to use

- A technical document is judged as a surface replica rather than a usable target text serving user tasks.
- Safety-critical, warning, or regulated content needs the correct severity hierarchy, official hazard phrases, or country-of-sale regulation applied.
- User-guide structure, information quantity, or visual design is being set without regard to audience tasks and cognition.
- Screenshots, graphics, or layout carry cultural connotations or must match the localized interface.

## Procedure

1. Design visual presentation around human perception (P009).
2. Treat appearance, layout, typography, white space, and format as communicative usability factors, not decoration (P010).
3. Prime and teach new material by linking it to related concepts, existing schemes, examples, habits, and visible reinforcement (P011).
4. Make user guides task-centered teaching interfaces rather than repositories of system information (P012).
5. Ground technical translation in target-audience communicative service, Skopos purpose, and reader cognitive demands (P025).
6. Use graphics deliberately and control translated screenshots against the final localized interface (P033).
7. Be honest, comprehensive enough, accurate, and ethically adequate at the point of need (P034).
8. Evaluate usability for specified users, goals, and contexts through effectiveness, efficiency, and satisfaction (P035).
9. For products sold in the EU, ensure all technical documentation is translated into the country-of-sale language(s), treat the translated documentation as an original target-language document subject to target-language (P036).
10. Provide enough information for the current user purpose, avoiding both overload and under-specification (P049).
11. Choose user-guide structure from product nature, audience background, and user tasks while avoiding information overload (P072).
12. Translate an abstract, together with its title, as a self-contained text (P100).
13. Translate warning and advisory information with particular care (P102).
14. Respect that design (font, size, colour, white space, graphics) drives usability and that clear prose alone does not guarantee comprehension; a translator can improve usability even without altering layout, changing graphics only when (P133).
15. Treat user guides as structured instructional documents in simple language supported by graphics whose usability rests on broadly universal human cognition but also carries culture-specific features and rarely targets a single (P135).
16. Always use the standardized international Hazard, Risk (P143).

## Inputs

- The technical source and translation, the target users and their tasks, and any safety or regulatory constraint.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to design visual presentation around human perception (P009).
- The analysis fails to treat appearance, layout, typography, white space, and format as communicative usability factors, not decoration (P010).
- The analysis fails to prime and teach new material by linking it to related concepts, existing schemes, examples, habits, and visible reinforcement (P011).
- The analysis fails to make user guides task-centered teaching interfaces rather than repositories of system information (P012).
- The analysis fails to ground technical translation in target-audience communicative service, Skopos purpose, and reader cognitive demands (P025).
- The analysis fails to use graphics deliberately and control translated screenshots against the final localized interface (P033).
- The analysis fails to be honest, comprehensive enough, accurate, and ethically adequate at the point of need (P034).
