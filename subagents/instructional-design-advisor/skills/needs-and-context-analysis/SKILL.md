# Needs And Context Analysis

## Purpose

This skill runs the front end: it begins performance improvement without presuming instruction and commits to needs assessment and design only for verified skill gaps, using proportionate analysis to separate capability deficits from accountability, incentive, tool, process, and culture causes. It traces a voiced problem to an organisational outcome and quantifies the desired-versus-actual gap with critical distance, multiple perspectives, and contrary evidence; operationalises vague goals into observable success behaviours confirmed by several knowledgeable contributors; triangulates unclear goal steps through self-performance, competent performers, workplace evidence, and authoritative procedures; uncovers the thought processes an expert actually uses by objective methods rather than self-report; describes learners, learning context, performance context, and tools from actual conditions; splits goals whose subperformances differ in structure; tests adoption fit against organisational goals and total cost; and gives a qualified content expert explicit structured review criteria rather than an unbounded opinion.

## When to use

- A training request arrives before anyone has established that instruction is the right response.
- A goal, its steps, or its success indicators are too vague to design or assess against.
- Learners, delivery technology, workplace conditions, or support arrangements must be established from actual conditions rather than assumed.
- Subject-matter accuracy or goal validity needs a content expert's review under explicit criteria.

## Procedure

1. For complex tasks, capture and teach the expert's actual thought process, not just the task's visible outputs, and use objective elicitation methods to surface it, since experts are typically unaware of their own reasoning (P050).
2. For individualized technology-mediated instruction serving a varied audience, verify technology, access, reliability, context support, and instructor responsibilities against actual conditions, triangulate learner characteristics, and design authentic motivation that fits heterogeneous participation (P065).
3. When evaluating existing instruction for a new organization or audience, test its adoption fit against current organizational goals and job needs, participant characteristics, and the full acquisition, implementation, maintenance, facility, and equipment demands (P070).
4. When a goal is stated in internal-state terms (for example awareness, appreciation, or understanding) or allows materially different interpretations, operationalize it by eliciting observable success behaviors from several knowledgeable contributors, selecting the best indicators, and checking that together they preserve the goal's original intent (P075).
5. Split a broad goal into separate units wherever its subperformances have different structures, add prerequisite instruction when demonstrated entry performance is weak despite prior exposure, and describe learners, learning context, performance context, and tools as distinct elements (P079).
6. When a clear goal is still hard to decompose or admits several plausible performance strategies, draft the goal assessment and triangulate the required actions, cognition, and decisions using self-performance, multiple competent performers, workplace evidence, job analysis, and authoritative procedures (P095).
7. When initiating an instructional-design effort, ground the behaviorally expressed goal in a demonstrated need, then analyze the goal, prerequisites, learners, and use environments before drafting condition-and-criterion performance objectives (P102).
8. When responding to an organizational performance problem or opportunity, begin without presuming instruction is the answer, and run a front-end analysis proportionate to the stakes to distinguish a genuine capability deficit from accountability, incentive, tool, process, or culture causes (P148).
9. Conduct front-end performance analysis without presuming training is needed, and commit resources to needs assessment and instructional design only once a skill gap is verified (P187).
10. To frame a measurable performance need, trace the voiced problem back to an organizational outcome, quantify the desired-versus-actual result and job-performance gaps, separate controllable from uncontrollable factors, and document the causal evidence and candidate solutions (P191).
11. When a performance gap has multiple plausible contributors, diagnose it with critical distance, multiple perspectives, direct evidence, and deliberately sought contrary evidence, building a measurable chain from organizational outcomes through job standards to controllable causes (P192).
12. When the evaluator cannot independently judge unfamiliar subject matter, give a qualified content expert the validated goals and skill frameworks as explicit review standards, converted into structured coverage criteria, rather than asking for an unbounded opinion (P193).

## Inputs

- The stated problem or training request, the organisational outcome behind it, what is known about the performers and their setting, and any analysis already done.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

## Anti-patterns to flag

The following is a curated, highest-impact subset; the full set of 12 grounding principles is covered in ## Procedure above.

- Training content mirrors the expert's self-reported description of the task rather than an objective elicitation of the actual thought process used, missing reasoning the expert isn't consciously aware of (P050).
- A goal stays worded as an internal state (for example "understand" or "appreciate") with no observable success behaviors elicited, or the indicators come from a single contributor instead of several, with no check against the goal's original intent (P075).
- A performance decomposition relies on a single source, such as self-performance alone, instead of triangulating self-performance, multiple competent performers, workplace evidence, job analysis, and authoritative procedures (P095).
- Condition-and-criterion performance objectives are drafted before the goal, prerequisites, learners, and use environment have been analyzed, or the goal itself is never traced back to a demonstrated need (P102).
- Design work starts by assuming instruction is the fix, without a front-end analysis proportionate to the problem that would separate a real capability deficit from accountability, incentive, tool, process, or culture causes (P148).
- A stated performance problem is treated as self-evidently real without tracing it to an organizational outcome, quantifying the desired-versus-actual gap, separating controllable factors, or documenting the causal evidence (P191).
- A content expert's subject-matter review is requested as an open-ended opinion instead of being scoped to validated goals and skill frameworks converted into structured coverage criteria (P193).
