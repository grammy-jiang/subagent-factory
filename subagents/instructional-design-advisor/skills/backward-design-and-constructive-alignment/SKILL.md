# Backward Design And Constructive Alignment

## Purpose

This skill governs the order and the alignment of a design: desired results first, then the evidence that would show they were achieved, then the teaching and learning that gets learners there. It keeps the unit as the working design focus while course, programme, and graduate purposes are designed backward from outcomes; keeps outcomes few, integrated, and stated as what a learner will newly do and how well; prioritises threshold and core content deliberately against stakeholder accretion; makes sure learners know what the learning eventually requires them to do; and treats design as iterative, with earlier units rethought in light of later ones and every assessment criterion traceable to a course outcome and a programme specification.

## When to use

- A course, unit, lesson, or programme is being designed or redesigned and the order of design decisions is at issue.
- Outcomes must be written, pruned, or derived from graduate and professional purposes rather than from topics and available content.
- An existing design must be audited for alignment between stated outcomes, taught activity, and assessed performance.
- Content is accreting from stakeholders and important outcomes must be protected by explicit prioritisation.

## Procedure

1. Keep the unit as the design focus (P006).
2. Ensure students understand what the learning eventually requires them to do (P010).
3. Understand backward design as called backward only because many teachers begin with the means, and follow its order (P013).
4. Plan instruction deliberately at nested levels (P019).
5. Expect the design process to be always iterative, with designers revisiting what they are after, how to assess it, and how to teach it, and rethinking earlier units in light of later designs and results (P041).
6. Adapt constructive alignment to local context while preserving its core relationship (P047).
7. Derive course and programme outcomes from graduate purposes, embedding both foundational and higher-order capabilities in authentic disciplinary or professional contexts (P074).
8. Develop complex graduate capabilities through disciplinary practice, repeated programme experiences, institutional ethos, and integrated evidence (P086).
9. Make evaluation part of the aligned course system (P095).
10. When an activity stands in for learning, state the intended result and success criteria, connect them repeatedly to the activity, and show representative evidence (P096).
11. Keep programme and course outcomes few, integrated, relevant, and manageable; each must state what learners will newly do and how well, while grouping compatible topics under common performances (P107).
12. Prioritise content deliberately (P116).
13. Implement alignment as one agenda (P153).
14. Write course-specific goals as concrete statements of what students will be able to do after the course, with at least one goal under each taxonomy category the course claims, rather than leaving goals in generic form (P160).

## Inputs

- The course, unit, or programme under design; its stated outcomes, assessments, and activities; and the graduate or professional purposes it serves.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.
