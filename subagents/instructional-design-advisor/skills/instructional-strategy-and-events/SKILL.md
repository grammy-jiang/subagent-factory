# Instructional Strategy And Events

## Purpose

This skill selects and sequences what actually happens in instruction, derived from the goal, the analyses, the objectives, and the assessments rather than from habit. It treats the nine events as a design checklist rather than a mandatory script, prescribes a method together with the situation it fits, and sequences like a zoom lens — an epitome of the whole, then elaboration of one part, then synthesis back to the whole. It sequences by prerequisite and manageable progression with entry checks and pretests, and matches technique to outcome type: concepts through varied instances and diagnostic non-instances with irrelevant shortcut cues removed, intellectual skills through activated prerequisites and progressive transfer cases, verbal information through organised prior-knowledge links, attitudes through credible models and delivered consequences, motor skills through part and whole practice. It scaffolds generative and complex performance from supported to independent work, fades prompts so contiguity is established with the intended cue, keeps every practice and test instance new, uses spaced retrieval rather than re-presentation, makes training conditions resemble retrieval conditions, builds strategy flexibility across multiple valid methods, and budgets time across the whole sequence.

## When to use

- The activities, examples, practice, and sequence of a unit or course must be chosen or reviewed.
- Different outcome types — concepts, intellectual skills, verbal information, attitudes, motor skills — need matching techniques.
- Learners must be scaffolded from supported work to independent production, or prerequisites are unmet.
- A recommended technique needs the situation, conditions, and time budget that make it the right one stated alongside it.

## Procedure

1. Add prerequisite instruction for demonstrated deficits, move broadly mastered capabilities into entry checks, and branch learners to only the teaching or review their profiles warrant (P005).
2. Recognize that rules for teaching mental operations are conditional rather than absolute (P011).
3. Prompt each example with attention-focusing information linking its specific attributes to the labels the definition uses, then fade that prompting in the later stages (P019).
4. Have the component concepts and rules recalled just before the new learning (P024).
5. Plan instruction deliberately at nested levels (P025).
6. Require a defined concept to be demonstrated rather than stated (P027).
7. Apply the sequencing principle proper to each outcome type (P028).
8. Sequence like a zoom lens (P029).
9. Introduce a concept only after learners have experienced the phenomenon it names, and open the section with a question arising from what they have just observed, so the definition labels something already seen and the new content is (P032).
10. Replace an abstract statement of objectives with a concrete demonstration of the terminal capability the learner will acquire (P035).
11. Employ repetition as spaced occasions for the learner to recall what was learned rather than as mere re-presentation (P036).
12. Teach a concrete concept by presenting instances that vary widely in their non-relevant characteristics together with negative instances, so correct performance is shown by both selecting members and rejecting non-members, and ensure (P037).
13. Treat the nine events of instruction as a design checklist to be considered for any desired learning, not a mandatory script (P039).
14. Practise against the stimulus named in the objective (P051).
15. Make every practice and test instance new (P052).
16. Delineate the distinctive features of every stimulus presented (P053).
17. Sequence simple-to-complex within a hierarchy, teaching subordinate paths before the superordinate ones that contain them, while recognizing that the order between independent hierarchies is not prescribed and several sequences are (P054).
18. Build deliberate recall of previously learned content into the lesson (P059).
19. Follow the design procedure appropriate to each learner characteristic (P060).
20. Sequence lessons by prerequisites and manageable progression, then use focused entry checks, authentic whole-task and lesson pretests, embedded feedback assessments, and component-plus-integrated posttests to route instruction (P063).
21. Teach intellectual skills through activated prerequisites, explicit prior-to-new links, diagnostic examples and nonexamples, application steps and quality criteria, progressive transfer cases, authentic application practice (P074).
22. Scaffold generative performance from transforming supplied examples to independent creation, support execution with concise criteria, and show multiple representative solutions when several answers are valid (P086).
23. Derive instructional strategy from the goal, analyses, objectives, and assessments, covering motivation, presentation, examples, active practice, feedback, assessment, transfer, and delivery constraints (P117).
24. Evaluate instructional strategy across preinstruction, presentation, participation, assessment, follow-through, motivation, and operational management, adapting criteria to outcomes and learners (P118).
25. Prescribe methods together with the situations they fit (P122).
26. Apply encoding specificity and transfer-appropriate interaction (P123).
27. Build fluency from conceptual relationships and progressively faded representations, prioritizing meaning before formal labels while preserving labels needed for later communication (P143).
28. Use current motivation, outcome-type, and strategy principles as explicit design-analysis criteria, recording both strengths and weaknesses for defensible synthesis (P146).
29. Teach classification with coherent learner-relevant examples, diagnostic nonexamples, removal of irrelevant shortcut cues, and rule-based explanations for every judgment (P147).
30. Teach and assess multiattribute concepts with closely matched examples and nonexamples, immediate error review, and new but strategy-parallel items across pretest, practice, and posttest (P150).
31. Budget time for motivation, readiness checks, instruction, practice, integration, and assessment, combining individualized delivery with prepared human support where learner readiness varies (P180).
32. Develop strategy flexibility by having learners apply, explain, and compare multiple valid methods, and diagnose actual method use rather than inferring it from answers or speed alone (P181).
33. Scaffold a complex procedure from reduced common material to independent projects, modeling both actions and decisions, monitoring guided practice, and remediating before release (P185).
34. Teach verbal information in a relevant, prior-knowledge-linked organization and rehearse meaningful recall cues, using logical mnemonics only when meaningful elaboration is insufficient (P189).
35. Influence attitudes by making desired and undesired choices and consequences explicit, using relatable credible models, and ensuring feedback delivers the promised consequence directly or vicariously (P190).

## Inputs

- The objectives and assessments already fixed, the learners' entry performance, and the activities, examples, and practice currently planned.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- Overlooking P005: Add prerequisite instruction for demonstrated deficits, move broadly mastered capabilities into entry checks, and branch learners to only.
- Overlooking P011: Recognize that rules for teaching mental operations are conditional rather than absolute.
- Overlooking P019: Prompt each example with attention-focusing information linking its specific attributes to the labels the definition uses, then fade that prompting.
- Overlooking P024: Have the component concepts and rules recalled just before the new learning.
- Overlooking P025: Plan instruction deliberately at nested levels.
- Overlooking P027: Require a defined concept to be demonstrated rather than stated.
- Overlooking P028: Apply the sequencing principle proper to each outcome type.
