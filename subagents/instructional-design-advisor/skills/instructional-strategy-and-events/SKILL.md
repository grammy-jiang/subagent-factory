# Instructional Strategy And Events

## Purpose

This skill selects and sequences what actually happens in instruction, derived from the goal, the analyses, the objectives, and the assessments rather than from habit. It covers motivation, presentation, examples, active practice, feedback, assessment, and transfer; sequences by prerequisite and manageable progression with entry checks and pretests; chooses the strategy by the information-processing stage it serves; and matches technique to outcome type — concepts through varied instances and negative instances, intellectual skills through activated prerequisites and progressive transfer cases, verbal information through organised prior-knowledge links, attitudes through modelled choices and consequences, motor skills through demonstration and physical practice. It scaffolds generative and complex performance from supported to independent work, uses spaced retrieval rather than re-presentation, makes training conditions resemble retrieval conditions, and budgets time across the whole sequence.

## When to use

- The activities, examples, practice, and sequence of a unit or course must be chosen or reviewed.
- Different outcome types — concepts, intellectual skills, verbal information, attitudes, motor skills — need matching techniques.
- Learners must be scaffolded from supported work to independent production, or prerequisites are unmet.
- Practice, repetition, and time budgets need setting against criticality and transfer conditions.

## Procedure

1. Add prerequisite instruction for demonstrated deficits, move broadly mastered capabilities into entry checks, and branch learners to only the teaching or review their profiles warrant (P008).
2. Employ repetition as spaced occasions for the learner to recall what was learned rather than as mere re-presentation (P029).
3. Teach a concrete concept by presenting instances that vary widely in their non-relevant characteristics together with negative instances, so correct performance is shown by both selecting members and rejecting non-members, and ensure (P030).
4. Choose the strategy by the information-processing stage it supports (P037).
5. Sequence lessons by prerequisites and manageable progression, then use focused entry checks, authentic whole-task and lesson pretests, embedded feedback assessments, and component-plus-integrated posttests to route instruction (P046).
6. Teach intellectual skills through activated prerequisites, explicit prior-to-new links, diagnostic examples and nonexamples, application steps and quality criteria, progressive transfer cases, authentic application practice (P057).
7. Scaffold generative performance from transforming supplied examples to independent creation, support execution with concise criteria, and show multiple representative solutions when several answers are valid (P072).
8. Teach categories through their defining common structure and useful disciplinary applications; when a misconception is systematic, diagnose and repair the underlying categorization rather than adding another definition (P101).
9. Derive instructional strategy from the goal, analyses, objectives, and assessments, covering motivation, presentation, examples, active practice, feedback, assessment, transfer, and delivery constraints (P102).
10. Evaluate instructional strategy across preinstruction, presentation, participation, assessment, follow-through, motivation, and operational management, adapting criteria to outcomes and learners (P103).
11. Make training conditions resemble the conditions of retrieval (P106).
12. Teach creativity through sufficient disciplinary knowledge, open-ended creation, iterative performance and reflection, exploratory consequences, peer feedback, and a climate safe for unusual hypotheses (P119).
13. Build fluency from conceptual relationships and progressively faded representations, prioritizing meaning before formal labels while preserving labels needed for later communication (P125).
14. Teach classification with coherent learner-relevant examples, diagnostic nonexamples, removal of irrelevant shortcut cues, and rule-based explanations for every judgment (P129).
15. Teach and assess multiattribute concepts with closely matched examples and nonexamples, immediate error review, and new but strategy-parallel items across pretest, practice, and posttest (P132).
16. Provide access to intellectually substantial material through conceptual bridges, paraphrase, examples, performance, and structured comparison rather than lowering expectations solely from prior attainment (P144).
17. For written or oral creation, interpret the full product demand, introduce purpose, audience, content, organization, and criteria early, and explicitly teach any rubric learners cannot yet use (P149).
18. Teach research as distinct judgments and procedures (P158).
19. Budget time for motivation, readiness checks, instruction, practice, integration, and assessment, combining individualized delivery with prepared human support where learner readiness varies (P161).
20. Scaffold a complex procedure from reduced common material to independent projects, modeling both actions and decisions, monitoring guided practice, and remediating before release (P166).
21. Gate drafting and creation on verified adequate, relevant, and accessible source material, then deliberately shift from enabling research procedures to the culminating product (P167).
22. Teach verbal information in a relevant, prior-knowledge-linked organization and rehearse meaningful recall cues, using logical mnemonics only when meaningful elaboration is insufficient (P170).
23. Influence attitudes by making desired and undesired choices and consequences explicit, using relatable credible models, and ensuring feedback delivers the promised consequence directly or vicariously (P171).
24. Teach motor and psychomotor skills through visual demonstration of the movement sequence and its timing, part-to-whole integration for complex performance, and repeated physical practice with immediate corrective feedback, so learners (P172).

## Inputs

- The objectives and assessments already fixed, the learners' entry performance, and the activities, examples, and practice currently planned.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- Overlooking P008: Add prerequisite instruction for demonstrated deficits, move broadly mastered capabilities into entry checks, and branch learners to only.
- Overlooking P029: Employ repetition as spaced occasions for the learner to recall what was learned rather than as mere re-presentation.
- Overlooking P030: Teach a concrete concept by presenting instances that vary widely in their non-relevant characteristics together with negative instances, so correct.
- Overlooking P037: Choose the strategy by the information-processing stage it supports.
- Overlooking P046: Sequence lessons by prerequisites and manageable progression, then use focused entry checks, authentic whole-task and lesson pretests, embedded.
- Overlooking P057: Teach intellectual skills through activated prerequisites, explicit prior-to-new links, diagnostic examples and nonexamples, application steps.
- Overlooking P072: Scaffold generative performance from transforming supplied examples to independent creation, support execution with concise criteria, and show.
