# Multimedia And E-Learning Design

## Purpose

This skill applies the evidence on how people learn from words and pictures. It prefers spoken narration to concurrent on-screen prose when animation and words must be processed together, refuses to duplicate narration with on-screen sentences merely to add a delivery mode, strips irrelevant words, decorative images, and background sound, places printed words next to the graphic part they describe, and eliminates split attention wherever the interface creates it. It signals what should drive selective perception, supplies or activates an organising structure, pretrains component names and behaviour for novices, sets practice quantity from criticality and learner time cost, treats high behavioural engagement as no evidence of learning, declines to design around learning styles, chooses pedagogy before technology, and states boundary conditions whenever it invokes one of these principles.

## When to use

- E-learning, slides, animation, video, or any words-plus-graphics material is being designed or reviewed.
- A medium or technology is being chosen, or is being adopted ahead of the pedagogy.
- A design appeals to learning styles, engagement, or added delivery modes as justification.
- Learners must split attention between text, graphic, question, and feedback.

## Procedure

1. When animation and explanatory words must be processed together, prefer spoken narration to concurrent on-screen prose so verbal and pictorial processing are distributed across channels (P001).
2. Do not duplicate narrated animation with concurrent on-screen sentences merely to add a delivery mode or satisfy presumed learning-style preferences (P004).
3. Treat the high behavioral engagement of games and simulations as no evidence of learning; the design problem is securing the psychological activity (P005).
4. Choose pedagogy before technology (P009).
5. Signal the features that should drive selective perception (P020).
6. Provide or activate an organizing structure or mental model for the new knowledge (P025).
7. Do not design around learning styles (P043).
8. Keep multimedia explanations coherent by removing irrelevant words, decorative images or video, and background sounds or music, even when those additions seem interesting or entertaining (P078).
9. Design explanations to help learners construct coherent causal models by selecting relevant words and images, organizing each representation, and integrating those representations with one another and with prior knowledge (P134).
10. Set practice quantity from task criticality and the automaticity requirement, weighing learner time (P137).
11. For novices learning an unfamiliar system, provide self-paced pretraining on each essential component's name, location, and behavior before presenting the causal interaction of the whole system (P138).
12. Contiguity principle 1 (P139).
13. Eliminate split attention wherever it appears in the interface (P140).
14. Apply these principles as guidance consistent with how the mind works rather than as unbending rules, and state the boundary conditions whenever you invoke one (P141).

## Inputs

- The instructional material or interface under review, its learners' expertise, and the medium, pacing, and control the design assumes.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.
