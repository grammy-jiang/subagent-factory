# Assessment Design And Authentic Tasks

## Purpose

This skill designs and reviews the evidence side of a course: authentic tasks that require judgment on unstructured problems in a realistic context, explicit public criteria applied before judgment, rubric standards written as descriptions of performance rather than bare numbers, and forward-looking tasks that put learners where the knowledge is actually used rather than repeating a taught protocol with varied parameters. It expects evidence of understanding to be less direct than an objective test score, separates formative from summative purpose, separates task grades from outcome achievement, prefers criterion-referenced standards over forced distributions and false percentage precision, builds assessment as an accumulating scrapbook of varied evidence rather than one snapshot, and designs examinations, portfolios, rubrics, reassessment, and the whole assessment programme as one proportionate evidence system.

## When to use

- An assessment task, rubric, examination, portfolio, or whole assessment programme is being designed or reviewed.
- Grades, standards, or pass marks must be set, calibrated, or defended.
- A design must show it is assessing understanding rather than recall, coverage, or test-taking skill.
- External or high-stakes assessment constrains the design and must be reconciled with local outcomes.

## Procedure

1. Match the assessment procedure to the kind of learning (P012).
2. Anchor assessment in authentic tasks (P016).
3. Require explicit criteria for evaluation, using internal consistency for checking and external standards for critique, and identify strengths and weaknesses before judgment (P017).
4. Write each standard on a rubric scale as a description of what performance at that level looks like rather than as a bare numeric label, and derive the criteria from the verbs in the learning goal (P018).
5. Protect criterion-based outcome judgement from administrative constraints (P021).
6. Accept that beyond foundational knowledge and application the available assessment procedures are valid but less consistently reliable, and separate what is assessed for feedback from what is graded (P044).
7. Distinguish exercising from forward-looking assessment (P047).
8. Structure a reflective portfolio with explicit prompts (P048).
9. Expect evidence of understanding to be less direct and more complicated than objective-test evidence (P067).
10. Distinguish task grades from outcome achievement (P081).
11. Maintain an alignment matrix from subordinate skills to condition-response-criterion objectives and parallel assessments, using authentic integrated products and explicit component rubrics for complex performance (P084).
12. Separate formative and summative purposes, label them unmistakably, and keep diagnostic practice safe enough for candid errors while using its evidence to change both learner and teacher action (P089).
13. Design assessment with outcomes and learning activities, using direct or authentic performance and a varied programme of tasks only where each contributes distinct valid evidence (P097).
14. Use a standards model for attainment (P098).
15. Set pass and mastery standards from minimum acceptable qualitative performance, make ordered rubrics public, calibrate assessors on whole examples, and allow bounded reassessment when mastery rather than selection is the goal (P110).
16. Assess creativity in the disciplinary work through grounded originality, ambition, experiment, critical inquiry, contextual appropriateness, and effective realisation while keeping criteria open to unanticipated forms (P112).
17. Design portfolios around strategic evidence selection, early guidance, bounded workload, open but relevant artefacts, and holistic outcome-profile judgement rather than item averaging (P114).
18. If an examination is retained, design it for integrated understanding, prepare learners for efficient resource use, and supplement convergent questions with routes for relevant divergent evidence (P127).
19. For consequential assessment, independently align objectives, taught substance, and assessed substance on both knowledge and process, while using only limited format practice to remove irrelevant unfamiliarity (P128).
20. Keep internal traceability in design records while presenting learner-facing aligned assessments without unnecessary codes, and use whole-task and subordinate-skill pretests to support fair branching (P129).
21. Manage external assessment as a high-stakes constraint by mapping its underlying knowledge and processes against local objectives, teaching the substantive learning, limiting format rehearsal, and retaining unit-specific criteria (P132).
22. Design the assessment programme as an evidence system (P167).
23. Build assessment as a scrapbook rather than a single snapshot (P199).

## Inputs

- The assessment tasks, criteria, rubrics, and grading scheme under discussion, together with the outcomes they are meant to provide evidence for.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

- Overlooking P016: Anchor assessment in authentic tasks.
- Overlooking P017: Require explicit criteria for evaluation, using internal consistency for checking and external standards for critique, and identify strengths.
- Overlooking P018: Write each standard on a rubric scale as a description of what performance at that level looks like rather than as a bare numeric label, and derive.
- Overlooking P021: Protect criterion-based outcome judgement from administrative constraints.
- Overlooking P044: Accept that beyond foundational knowledge and application the available assessment procedures are valid but less consistently reliable, and separate.
- Overlooking P047: Distinguish exercising from forward-looking assessment.
