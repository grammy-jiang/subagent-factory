# Assessment Design And Authentic Tasks

## Purpose

This skill designs and reviews the evidence side of a course: authentic tasks that require judgment on unstructured problems in a realistic context, explicit public criteria applied before judgment, validity and reliability questions asked plainly, and forward-looking tasks that put learners where the knowledge is actually used rather than asking whether topics were covered. It separates formative from summative purpose, separates task grades from outcome achievement, prefers criterion-referenced standards over norm-referenced curves, builds assessment as an accumulating scrapbook of varied evidence rather than one snapshot, and designs examinations, portfolios, rubrics, reassessment, and the whole assessment programme as one proportionate evidence system.

## When to use

- An assessment task, rubric, examination, portfolio, or whole assessment programme is being designed or reviewed.
- Grades, standards, or pass marks must be set, calibrated, or defended.
- A design must show it is assessing understanding rather than recall, coverage, or test-taking skill.
- External or high-stakes assessment constrains the design and must be reconciled with local outcomes.

## Procedure

1. Anchor assessment in authentic tasks (P014).
2. Require explicit criteria for evaluation, using internal consistency for checking and external standards for critique, and identify strengths and weaknesses before judgment (P015).
3. Write assessment tasks forward-looking rather than backward-looking (P016).
4. Ask of validity whether the test measures what it purports to, whether results correlate with other valid results, whether the sample represents what was taught, and whether results predict future success (P018).
5. Use the objective to set up the assessment situation directly (P026).
6. Accept that beyond foundational knowledge and application the available assessment procedures are valid but less consistently reliable, and separate what is assessed for feedback from what is graded (P032).
7. Distinguish exercising from forward-looking assessment (P035).
8. Structure a reflective portfolio with explicit prompts (P036).
9. Prefer criterion-referenced grading against predefined standards using performances and portfolios, with continual assessment of instruction, over norm-referenced grading on a curve judged by end-of-course ratings (P039).
10. Distinguish task grades from outcome achievement (P065).
11. Maintain an alignment matrix from subordinate skills to condition-response-criterion objectives and parallel assessments, using authentic integrated products and explicit component rubrics for complex performance (P069).
12. Expect evidence of understanding to be less direct and more complicated than objective-test evidence (P071).
13. Separate formative and summative purposes, label them unmistakably, and keep diagnostic practice safe enough for candid errors while using its evidence to change both learner and teacher action (P075).
14. Design assessment with outcomes and learning activities, using direct or authentic performance and a varied programme of tasks only where each contributes distinct valid evidence (P082).
15. Use a standards model for attainment (P083).
16. Set pass and mastery standards from minimum acceptable qualitative performance, make ordered rubrics public, calibrate assessors on whole examples, and allow bounded reassessment when mastery rather than selection is the goal (P094).
17. Assess creativity in the disciplinary work through grounded originality, ambition, experiment, critical inquiry, contextual appropriateness, and effective realisation while keeping criteria open to unanticipated forms (P097).
18. Design portfolios around strategic evidence selection, early guidance, bounded workload, open but relevant artefacts, and holistic outcome-profile judgement rather than item averaging (P099).
19. If an examination is retained, design it for integrated understanding, prepare learners for efficient resource use, and supplement convergent questions with routes for relevant divergent evidence (P109).
20. For consequential assessment, independently align objectives, taught substance, and assessed substance on both knowledge and process, while using only limited format practice to remove irrelevant unfamiliarity (P110).
21. Keep internal traceability in design records while presenting learner-facing aligned assessments without unnecessary codes, and use whole-task and subordinate-skill pretests to support fair branching (P111).
22. Manage external assessment as a high-stakes constraint by mapping its underlying knowledge and processes against local objectives, teaching the substantive learning, limiting format rehearsal, and retaining unit-specific criteria (P114).
23. Build assessment as a scrapbook rather than a single snapshot (P136).
24. Before grading learner choice among project options, calibrate options for cognitive demand, workload, required knowledge, support, and scoring, and explain those differences before selection (P145).
25. Design the assessment programme as an evidence system (P148).

## Inputs

- The assessment tasks, criteria, rubrics, and grading scheme under discussion, together with the outcomes they are meant to provide evidence for.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- Overlooking P014: Anchor assessment in authentic tasks.
- Overlooking P015: Require explicit criteria for evaluation, using internal consistency for checking and external standards for critique, and identify strengths.
- Overlooking P016: Write assessment tasks forward-looking rather than backward-looking.
- Overlooking P018: Ask of validity whether the test measures what it purports to, whether results correlate with other valid results, whether the sample represents what.
- Overlooking P026: Use the objective to set up the assessment situation directly.
- Overlooking P032: Accept that beyond foundational knowledge and application the available assessment procedures are valid but less consistently reliable, and separate.
- Overlooking P035: Distinguish exercising from forward-looking assessment.
