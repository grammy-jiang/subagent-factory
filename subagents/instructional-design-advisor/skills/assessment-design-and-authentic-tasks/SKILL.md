# Assessment Design And Authentic Tasks

## Purpose

This skill designs and reviews the evidence side of a course: authentic tasks that require judgment on unstructured problems in a realistic context, explicit public criteria applied before judgment, rubric standards written as descriptions of performance rather than bare numbers, and forward-looking tasks that put learners where the knowledge is actually used rather than repeating a taught protocol with varied parameters. It expects evidence of understanding to be less direct than an objective test score, separates formative from summative purpose, separates task grades from outcome achievement, prefers criterion-referenced standards over forced distributions and false percentage precision, builds assessment as an accumulating scrapbook of varied evidence rather than one snapshot, and designs examinations, portfolios, rubrics, reassessment, and the whole assessment programme as one proportionate evidence system.

## When to use

- An assessment task, rubric, examination, portfolio, or whole assessment programme is being designed or reviewed.
- Grades, standards, or pass marks must be set, calibrated, or defended.
- A design must show it is assessing understanding rather than recall, coverage, or test-taking skill.
- External or high-stakes assessment constrains the design and must be reconciled with local outcomes.

## Procedure

1. Select the assessment method to fit the type of learning goal: reflections, portfolios, and problem-based performance for learning-how-to-learn; attitude, interest, or value questionnaires for caring; before-and-after confidence instruments and teammate reports for the human dimension; concept maps, interdisciplinary cases, and capstones for integration; and simulations, demonstrations, team projects, and case studies scored against clear criteria for application (P012).
2. Ground assessment in authentic tasks: realistic problems that demand judgment and innovation on an unstructured situation, require the student to actually perform the subject, reproduce a real context's constraints, purposes, and audiences, and call on a whole repertoire of skill rather than isolated pieces, since such tasks give daily work its direction, coherence, and motivation (P016).
3. Insist on explicit evaluation criteria, check internal consistency and critique against external standards, and name a performance's strengths and weaknesses before rendering a judgment (P017).
4. Phrase every rubric level as a description of the performance it represents rather than a bare number, and derive the criteria from the verbs inside the learning goal, so that an application goal naming design, construct, and assess produces three criteria clusters, each with its own multi-point standards (P018).
5. Shield criterion-based judgement of outcomes from administrative pressure: keep relative selection separate from achievement, rely on valid supervised performances wherever invigilation is required, and convert qualitative judgements into mandated numbers only after the substantive judgement is made (P021).
6. Recognise that, beyond foundational knowledge and application, the remaining assessment procedures are valid yet less consistently reliable, and keep what is assessed for feedback separate from what is graded, because caring and human-dimension goals can be valuable without being fit to anchor a course grade (P044).
7. Separate exercising from forward-looking assessment: repeating a taught procedure with varied numbers builds useful early practice and familiarity but does not demonstrate readiness to act, and learners find realistic tasks more motivating than repetitive drill (P047).
8. Build reflective portfolios around explicit prompts asking what was learned about the content, how that learning connects to the student's personal, social, and work life, and what the student learned about their own learning process, or use one prompt per taxonomy category (P048).
9. Treat evidence of understanding as less direct and more complicated to read than an objective-test score, since a correct answer can come from rote recall, test-taking skill, or luck, so probe the reasoning behind answers and the meaning the learner attaches to results rather than counting the percentage correct (P067).
10. Keep task grades distinct from outcome achievement: aggregate valid evidence by both the task and the outcome, retain task-level feedback, report outcome profiles, and use weak outcome patterns to redesign activities and programme sequencing (P081).
11. Keep an alignment matrix running from subordinate skills through condition-response-criterion objectives to parallel assessments, using authentic integrated products together with explicit component rubrics wherever the performance is complex (P084).
12. Keep formative and summative purposes separate and unmistakably labelled, keep diagnostic practice safe enough for learners to risk candid errors, and use its evidence to change both learner and teacher action (P089).
13. Design assessment alongside the outcomes and learning activities it serves, reaching for direct or authentic performance and adding a varied set of tasks only where each one contributes evidence the others cannot (P097).
14. Adopt a standards model for attainment: judge multidimensional performance holistically against public criteria, avoid the false precision of percentages and forced grade distributions, and let every learner who meets a standard receive the grade tied to it (P098).
15. Base pass and mastery standards on the minimum acceptable qualitative performance, publish the ordered rubrics, calibrate assessors against whole worked examples, and permit bounded reassessment wherever the goal is mastery rather than ranked selection (P110).
16. Judge creativity within the discipline's own work by weighing grounded originality, ambition, experimentation, critical inquiry, contextual appropriateness, and effective realisation, while leaving the criteria open to forms nobody anticipated (P112).
17. Design portfolios around a strategic selection of evidence, early guidance to the learner, a bounded workload, artefacts that are open yet relevant, and a holistic judgement of the outcome profile rather than an average of item scores (P114).
18. If an examination survives the design, build it for integrated understanding, prepare learners to use their resources efficiently under exam conditions, and add routes for relevant divergent evidence alongside the convergent questions (P127).
19. For any assessment with real consequences, independently align the objectives, what was taught, and what is assessed, on both knowledge and process, and use only a limited amount of format practice to strip away irrelevant unfamiliarity (P128).
20. Keep the internal traceability codes in the design records but strip them from the learner-facing assessment, and use whole-task and subordinate-skill pretests to support fair branching between paths (P129).
21. Treat an externally mandated assessment as a high-stakes constraint: map its underlying knowledge and processes against the local objectives, teach the substantive learning it demands, limit rehearsal of its format, and keep the unit's own criteria in place (P132).
22. Plan the whole assessment programme as an evidence system: use synoptic tasks wherever several outcomes integrate, complementary tasks wherever a single sample would be insufficient, and weight and scale workload in proportion to each piece's educational importance (P167).
23. Treat assessment as an accumulating scrapbook rather than a single snapshot, gathering evidence along the way through varied methods and formats, since understanding develops through ongoing inquiry and rethinking rather than arriving in one end-of-instruction moment (P199).

## Inputs

- The assessment tasks, criteria, rubrics, and grading scheme under discussion, together with the outcomes they are meant to provide evidence for.
- The reasoning offered for the design decision under review: the intended learning, the learners and setting, the evidence already gathered, and any claim of alignment, effectiveness, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on instructional design; it does not build the course, materials, or item bank for the caller, teach or grade learners, or certify a programme or its subject-matter content.

## Anti-patterns to flag

The following is a curated, highest-impact subset of failure modes; the full set of 23 principles is covered in [Procedure](#procedure).

- The assessment tasks are worksheet-style repetition of taught examples with different numbers, offered as evidence the learner is ready to perform in a real context (P047).
- A rubric shows only numeric point values or a single overall score, with no description of what performance at each level actually looks like (P018).
- Grading is dominated by a single end-of-course exam or project, with no accumulated evidence gathered along the way (P199).
- Non-content goals such as caring, attitudes, or the human dimension are folded into the course grade despite no reliable procedure existing to judge them (P044).
- Tasks stay confined to classroom-only formats such as short answer or objective test, even where the outcome calls for judgment on an unstructured, real-world problem (P016).
- A portfolio's grade is computed by averaging individual item scores rather than by a holistic judgement of the outcome profile the portfolio represents (P114).
- Formative, low-stakes practice and summative, high-stakes grading are run in the same event without clear labelling, discouraging learners from risking the candid errors diagnostic work needs (P089).
- Percentage scores or a forced grade distribution are imposed on a criterion-referenced judgement, so a learner who meets the standard can still be denied its grade (P098).
- An externally mandated test drives the unit's teaching and assessment wholesale, displacing the unit's own objectives and criteria instead of being mapped against them (P132).
- A creativity or originality outcome is scored against a fixed checklist that cannot recognise a valid but unanticipated form of response (P112).
- Open-ended work is scored directly without first identifying its strengths and weaknesses against explicit criteria (P017).
