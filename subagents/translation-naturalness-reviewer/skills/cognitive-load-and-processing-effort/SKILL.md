# Cognitive Load And Processing Effort

## Purpose

This skill reviews a translation for the mental effort it imposes on the reader. It treats translation as inferential communication that should be optimally relevant — adequate contextual effects for the least effort — and checks working-memory burden, attention switching, given-new progression, and whether the reader is helped to frame the problem, so that 'unnaturalness' is diagnosed as gratuitous processing effort rather than blamed on the source.

## When to use

- A rendering reads as effortful or 'unnatural' and the cause may be gratuitous processing effort, not the source.
- Working-memory burden or attention switching is high where chunking, recognition cues, or given-new order would ease it.
- The reader lacks the declarative background needed to act, or a maxim is flouted without a recoverable implicature.
- Context and intended interpretation are not made easy to follow for the target reader.

## Procedure

1. Treat translation as inferential communication, making the target optimally relevant so the receiver derives adequate contextual effects without unnecessary effort by supplying communicative clues, and decide whether and how to (P005).
2. Reduce working-memory burden through chunking, short retention gaps, familiar groupings, relevant visuals, and recognition-based cues (P010).
3. Minimize target-reader processing effort by making context, intended interpretation, and given-new progression easy to follow (P017).
4. Minimize attention switching because reading the guide and doing the task draw on the same limited cognitive capacity (P021).
5. Help users frame problems correctly, surface misconceptions, and provide enough declarative background before expecting fluent action (P022).
6. Support both reading-to-learn and reading-to-do, including sequential reading and random lookup (P028).
7. Use the Co-operative Principle and its four maxims, Quantity, Quality, Relevance, and Manner, as orientation points rather than strict rules (P046).
8. Shape translated documentation around reader cognition, including perception, memory, attention, learning, and problem solving (P047).
9. Examine anything that would violate the target reader's expectations and adjust it to avoid wrong implicatures (P066).
10. Choose delivery medium according to how users actually read and use the documentation (P089).
11. Treat translation as an interpretive cognitive process (P100).

## Inputs

- The source and target texts and the reading task, with attention to the effort the target imposes.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to treat translation as inferential communication, making the target optimally relevant so the receiver derives adequate contextual effects without unnecessary effort by (P005).
- The analysis fails to reduce working-memory burden through chunking, short retention gaps, familiar groupings, relevant visuals, and recognition-based cues (P010).
- The analysis fails to minimize target-reader processing effort by making context, intended interpretation, and given-new progression easy to follow (P017).
- The analysis fails to minimize attention switching because reading the guide and doing the task draw on the same limited cognitive capacity (P021).
- The analysis fails to help users frame problems correctly, surface misconceptions, and provide enough declarative background before expecting fluent action (P022).
- The analysis fails to support both reading-to-learn and reading-to-do, including sequential reading and random lookup (P028).
- The analysis fails to use the Co-operative Principle and its four maxims, Quantity, Quality, Relevance, and Manner, as orientation points rather than strict rules (P046).
