# Audience, Brief And Reader Fit

## Purpose

This skill reviews whether a translation is driven by who will read and use the text and by an explicit brief, rather than by source-text features alone. It checks that the target audience, purpose (information vs publication), medium, and terminology are established up front, that the reader's assumed background knowledge is supplied deliberately, and that vocabulary, explanation, and detail are calibrated to the audience's decoding capacity, expertise, and seniority — since the same source can legitimately yield different translations for different readers.

## When to use

- A translation lacks an explicit brief specifying audience, purpose, medium, and any terminology or style requirements.
- The reader's background knowledge is being assumed rather than assessed and supplied.
- Vocabulary, explanation, or detail is pitched wrong for the audience's literacy, expertise, or seniority.
- The type of translation (gist, information-purposes, publication) is undecided or read off the source instead of the client's need.

## Procedure

1. Base translation decisions on who will read the text, how they will use it, and how it will be distributed, and establish these via a translation brief specifying at minimum the target audience, the intended purpose (P001).
2. Assess and supply the target reader's background knowledge deliberately (P002).
3. Plan documentation around specific readers, their tasks, prior knowledge, problems, and objectives (P013).
4. Accept that the translation brief has no settled form and often forces educated guesses, so ask the client a short standard set of questions (P034).
5. Drive a translation from the brief and source analysis (P035).
6. Calibrate vocabulary, style, explanation, and explicitness to the audience’s decoding capacity, experience, and interests (P058).
7. Decide translation type from source status, use, reception history, purpose, and brief, not from source-text features alone (P060).
8. Determine first what type of translation the client wants (P075).
9. Categorize readers by role (P096).
10. Create a document profile (P107).
11. Set translation quality requirements from the commission purpose and risk level, distinguishing basic understanding or information uses from publication, image, legal, or high-stakes uses (P142).
12. Handle culture-specific references according to the target user need for precision, explanation, or local usability (P143).
13. Account for international readers through audience-specific linguistic and cultural adaptation (P144).
14. Adapt register and emphasis to the target audience (P149).
15. Calibrate language and detail to the audience's education, literacy, work experience, expertise, and seniority (P150).

## Inputs

- The translation, its brief (or its absence), and the intended readers and their use of the text.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to base translation decisions on who will read the text, how they will use it, and how it will be distributed, and establish these via a translation brief specifying at (P001).
- The analysis fails to assess and supply the target reader's background knowledge deliberately (P002).
- The analysis fails to plan documentation around specific readers, their tasks, prior knowledge, problems, and objectives (P013).
- The analysis fails to accept that the translation brief has no settled form and often forces educated guesses, so ask the client a short standard set of questions (P034).
- The analysis fails to drive a translation from the brief and source analysis (P035).
- The analysis fails to calibrate vocabulary, style, explanation, and explicitness to the audience’s decoding capacity, experience, and interests (P058).
- The analysis fails to decide translation type from source status, use, reception history, purpose, and brief, not from source-text features alone (P060).
