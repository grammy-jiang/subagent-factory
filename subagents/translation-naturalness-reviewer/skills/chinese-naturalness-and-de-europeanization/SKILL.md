# Chinese Naturalness And De-Europeanization

## Purpose

This skill reviews a Chinese target for natural idiom against Europeanized (English-interfered) constructions. It preserves concision, flexible syntax, and rhythmic force; prefers concrete subjects and direct verbs over abstract nominalized subjects and weak-verb-plus-abstract-noun packaging; strips redundant plural marking, imported passive and membership structures, and over-explicit causal scaffolding; and distinguishes ordinary prose correction from deliberate, knowing literary innovation.

## When to use

- A Chinese sentence is needlessly complex or stiff because Europeanized wording has displaced concise, flexible native syntax.
- Abstract nominalized subjects, weak-verb-plus-abstract-noun phrases, or pseudo-technical nouns repackage a plain action.
- Imported passives, one-of/as-one-of membership structures, redundant plural marking, or formal because/therefore scaffolding stiffen the text.
- A departure from normal Chinese idiom must be checked as deliberate innovation before it is corrected.

## Procedure

1. For ordinary Chinese prose, preserve concision, flexible syntax, and rhythmic force; resist Europeanized wording when it makes the sentence needlessly complex or stiff (P082).
2. Let coordinate Chinese words or phrases stand by juxtaposition when natural, and choose connectors that match progression, contrast, or adjustment instead of defaulting to English-like and-linking (P083).
3. Recognize that the translator's chief obstacle is often not the source language's difficulty but the dead, conventional register of his own language, and that there is no single target language but a series of period- and (P120).
4. Use established Chinese idioms as live resources, not as decorative substitutes for thought, and do not flatten them into wordy paraphrase when the idiom fits (P131).
5. Prefer concrete subjects, people, and event clauses over abstract nominalized subjects when revising Chinese sentences influenced by English syntax (P132).
6. Replace weak-verb plus abstract-noun constructions with direct Chinese verbs whenever the phrase only repackages a plain action (P133).
7. Do not use pseudo-technical abstract nouns merely to sound objective or elevated; revise to direct adjectives, nouns, or everyday expressions when precision does not improve (P134).
8. Avoid redundant plural marking in Chinese when quantity words, collective nouns, or context already make plurality clear (P135).
9. Avoid imported one-of and as-one-of structures when class membership is obvious; recast the sentence so the main subject is not buried under a heavy membership phrase (P136).
10. Avoid over-explicit causal scaffolding in Chinese; remove formal because/therefore markers when sequence and context already convey the relation, and do not use a because-clause as the subject (P137).
11. Do not force Chinese idiomatic verb phrases into stiff adverbial forms, and delete empty success adverbs when the verb already entails completion (P138).
12. Reduce mechanical modifier-particle chains by deleting needless particles, using parallel or connective phrasing, or restoring verb clauses so modifier hierarchy stays clear (P139).
13. Prefer active or agentive Chinese constructions over imported passive patterns, and when passive meaning is needed choose an idiomatic passive expression suited to the event and register (P140).
14. Distinguish ordinary prose correction from deliberate literary innovation (P141).

## Inputs

- The Chinese target text and the specific constructions suspected of English-interfered Europeanization.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
