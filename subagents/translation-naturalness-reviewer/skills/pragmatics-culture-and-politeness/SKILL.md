# Pragmatics, Culture And Politeness

## Purpose

This skill reviews pragmatic and cultural naturalness. It handles culturally non-matching referents by a deliberate choice (formal term plus note, functional equivalent, borrowed term, or description); warns that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail can destroy the figurative meaning it carries; weighs politeness, taboo, and face against accuracy as culturally variable; and adjusts address, titles, and terms to the target's norms to avoid unintended implicatures.

## When to use

- A culturally non-matching referent needs a deliberate strategy rather than a default gloss.
- Over-clarifying a culture-specific detail risks destroying the indirect, figurative meaning it carries.
- Politeness, taboo, or a face-threatening act must be weighed against accuracy for the target culture.
- Modes of address, titles, or terms of affection need adjusting to the target's norms to avoid wrong implicatures.

## Procedure

1. Beware that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail to help the target reader can destroy the indirect, figurative meaning it carries across a text (P024).
2. Evaluate ELF communication by mutual intelligibility, efficiency, and multilingual multicompetence rather than by native-speaker correctness alone (P026).
3. For culturally nonmatching referents, choose deliberately among formal term plus note, functional equivalent, borrowed term with classifier, and receptor-language description (P031).
4. Weigh politeness and taboo against accuracy (P095).
5. Weight the seriousness of a face-threatening act as a cultural variable set by the social distance and relative power of the speakers (P129).
6. Respect the target reader's sense of what is socially and textually appropriate, the context for Be polite, including conventions like the expected calendar, and adjust modes of address, titles, name combinations, and terms of (P148).

## Inputs

- The translation and the culture-specific reference, politeness, or face concern under review.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
