# Register, Tenor, Mode And Text Type

## Purpose

This skill reviews register and text-type fit using the Hallidayan model — field, tenor, mode — mapped to a text's function in its sociocultural framework. It maps the whole text's predominant function (informative, expressive, operative, audio-medial) to a method, matches register to the receivers' expectations, applies House's covert/overt distinction, and treats the frameworks as a flexible toolkit rather than a rigid, English-oriented one-to-one model.

## When to use

- A text's predominant function (informative, expressive, operative) is not mapped to a translation method.
- The target register (field, tenor, mode) misfits the receivers' expectations or the genre's conventions.
- A covert translation that should read as target-original, or an overt one tied to its source, is mis-oriented.
- Register or genre frameworks are being applied rigidly or imposed uncritically on a non-European language.

## Procedure

1. For covert translation, account for target pragmatics through cultural filters supported by multi-method research on register and genre variation across language pairs (P015).
2. Treat German-English pragmatic differences such as directness, content orientation, self-reference, routine use, and explicit justification as corpus- and study-bound tendencies rather than fixed national traits (P025).
3. Build register analysis from Field, Tenor, and Mode (P039).
4. For children’s picture books, analyze whether lexis, syntax, cohesion, iconic linkage, sound patterning, and repetition support young listeners’ comprehension, drama, and involvement (P040).
5. In children’s books, treat respect for readers and characters, naming, titles, conversational rituals, reassurance, and gentle socialization as core Tenor and genre-function evidence (P041).
6. Work at the level of the whole text and map its predominant function to a method (P051).
7. Use the Hallidayan model to relate lexicogrammatical choices to a text's function in its sociocultural framework, analysing register as field, tenor, and mode mapped to the ideational, interpersonal, and textual metafunctions, and (P052).
8. Assess translation quality by comparing the source and target register profiles (P053).
9. Match the translation to the register expectations of its intended receivers along field, tenor, and mode, deciding tenor by the perceived purpose of the translation, unless the purpose is deliberately to give the reader a flavour of (P063).
10. Use discourse and register analysis as a flexible toolkit rather than a rigid one-to-one model, and heed its cautions (P069).
11. Check register and discourse fit locally (P084).
12. Use Genre to connect individual texts to macro-contextual text classes and combine it with language/text and register to characterize textual function (P086).
13. Use House's covert/overt distinction (P098).
14. Treat text types as prototypes on a continuum without rigid divisions, incorporating the relevant non-linguistic disciplines (P101).
15. Establish the text's variety (P122).

## Inputs

- The source and target texts and their register profile, genre, and text-type function.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
