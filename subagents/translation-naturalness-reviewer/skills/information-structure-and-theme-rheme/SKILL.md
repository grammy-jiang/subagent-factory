# Information Structure And Theme-Rheme

## Purpose

This skill reviews the flow of information across clauses. It checks the theme-rheme and given-new organisation that makes a text read smoothly, requires a discernible thematic method of development, resolves clashes between end-weight and target grammar, and re-signals marked focus by the target's own means (typography, punctuation, or a compensating structure) — reading markedness as meaning that must be motivated, not carried over mechanically.

## When to use

- Information does not progress given-before-new, so sentences read as jerky or back-to-front.
- The target lacks a discernible thematic method of development and feels disjointed.
- End-weight clashes with a target grammatical principle (e.g. subject-before-predicate) and a heavy-subject result stands.
- Marked theme or focus has been transferred wholesale rather than re-signalled by the target's means.

## Procedure

1. Know the marked-theme devices, fronted theme (P032).
2. Attend to a text's information dynamics, treating each clause as a message whose interactional organization drives a clean progression of links (P042).
3. Maintain a discernible thematic method of development in the target text (P043).
4. Order information given-before-new, the hearer-oriented, context-dependent default that aids comprehension and underlies end-focus and end-weight, and resolve clashes between end-weight and a basic target grammatical principle, such (P044).
5. Handle marked information focus by the target's means (P045).
6. Analyse each clause as a theme, what it is about placed in initial position and orienting back and forward, plus a rheme, what is said about it and carrying the key information; theme-rheme is hierarchical and text-based, accounting (P064).
7. Draw on the strategies for resolving tension between syntax and information flow (P065).
8. Read markedness as meaning (P092).
9. Recognise the limits of the Hallidayan initial-position view of theme, its partial circularity and poor fit for verb-initial and topic-prominent languages, and handle topic-prominent languages such as Chinese, Japanese, and Korean (P093).
10. Treat word order as significant beyond grammar (P146).
11. Recognise that word order serves syntactic, semantic, and communicative functions at once and that languages prioritise them differently by how fixed their order is, English leading with syntax and free-order languages following (P147).

## Inputs

- The source and target clauses and the theme-rheme, given-new, or marked-focus pattern in play.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the audience, brief, strategy, and any naturalness or quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to know the marked-theme devices, fronted theme (P032).
- The analysis fails to attend to a text's information dynamics, treating each clause as a message whose interactional organization drives a clean progression of links (P042).
- The analysis fails to maintain a discernible thematic method of development in the target text (P043).
- The analysis fails to order information given-before-new, the hearer-oriented, context-dependent default that aids comprehension and underlies end-focus and end-weight, and resolve clashes (P044).
- The analysis fails to handle marked information focus by the target's means (P045).
- The analysis fails to analyse each clause as a theme, what it is about placed in initial position and orienting back and forward, plus a rheme, what is said about it and carrying the key (P064).
- The analysis fails to draw on the strategies for resolving tension between syntax and information flow (P065).
