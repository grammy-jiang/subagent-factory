# Translation Naturalness Principles Index

Package-wide index of every promoted principle, grouped by the skill that owns it. Each entry restates the principle's operative core; the full statement lives in `../principles/principles.yaml`.

## Audience, Brief And Reader Fit

Skill: `audience-brief-and-reader-fit`

- **P001** — Base translation decisions on who will read the text, how they will use it, and how it will be distributed, and establish these via a translation brief specifying at minimum the target audience, the.
- **P002** — Assess and supply the target reader's background knowledge deliberately.
- **P013** — Plan documentation around specific readers, their tasks, prior knowledge, problems, and objectives.
- **P034** — Accept that the translation brief has no settled form and often forces educated guesses, so ask the client a short standard set of questions.
- **P035** — Drive a translation from the brief and source analysis.
- **P058** — Calibrate vocabulary, style, explanation, and explicitness to the audience’s decoding capacity, experience, and interests.
- **P060** — Decide translation type from source status, use, reception history, purpose, and brief, not from source-text features alone.
- **P075** — Determine first what type of translation the client wants.
- **P096** — Categorize readers by role.
- **P107** — Create a document profile.
- **P142** — Set translation quality requirements from the commission purpose and risk level, distinguishing basic understanding or information uses from publication, image, legal, or high-stakes uses.
- **P143** — Handle culture-specific references according to the target user need for precision, explanation, or local usability.
- **P144** — Account for international readers through audience-specific linguistic and cultural adaptation.
- **P149** — Adapt register and emphasis to the target audience.
- **P150** — Calibrate language and detail to the audience's education, literacy, work experience, expertise, and seniority.

## Technical Translation And Usability

Skill: `technical-translation-usability`

- **P003** — Be a good technical writer in order to be a good technical translator.
- **P008** — Evaluate user guides holistically with representative task-based usability testing; readability formulas are only partial indicators.
- **P016** — Treat technical translation as target-user usability work, not as source-text transfer alone.
- **P018** — Introduce iconic linkage with test materials that contain suitably spaced recurrence, a concise style guide, and translation-memory support.
- **P019** — Match target-language technical conventions and required document form while preserving correctness and usability.
- **P067** — Be able to recognize the specialized file types and software of the client's industry and be comfortable.
- **P072** — Concentrate strategy decisions on target audience needs and native-quality technical communication.
- **P103** — Judge efficiency from the user total task, and keep guide information clear, digestible, and concise.
- **P105** — Keep language clear, simple, and to the point as a core value of technical communication.
- **P109** — Apply Iconic Linkage: when the source expresses the same information several times with slightly different wording, pick one single translation and reuse it throughout.
- **P110** — Optimize for usability.

## Cognitive Load And Processing Effort

Skill: `cognitive-load-and-processing-effort`

- **P005** — Treat translation as inferential communication, making the target optimally relevant so the receiver derives adequate contextual effects without unnecessary effort by supplying communicative clues.
- **P010** — Reduce working-memory burden through chunking, short retention gaps, familiar groupings, relevant visuals, and recognition-based cues.
- **P017** — Minimize target-reader processing effort by making context, intended interpretation, and given-new progression easy to follow.
- **P021** — Minimize attention switching because reading the guide and doing the task draw on the same limited cognitive capacity.
- **P022** — Help users frame problems correctly, surface misconceptions, and provide enough declarative background before expecting fluent action.
- **P028** — Support both reading-to-learn and reading-to-do, including sequential reading and random lookup.
- **P046** — Use the Co-operative Principle and its four maxims, Quantity, Quality, Relevance, and Manner, as orientation points rather than strict rules.
- **P047** — Shape translated documentation around reader cognition, including perception, memory, attention, learning, and problem solving.
- **P066** — Examine anything that would violate the target reader's expectations and adjust it to avoid wrong implicatures.
- **P089** — Choose delivery medium according to how users actually read and use the documentation.
- **P100** — Treat translation as an interpretive cognitive process.

## Documentation Structure And Genre

Skill: `documentation-structure-and-genre`

- **P009** — Write concise, direct, audience-fit user-guide language without ambiguity, obscuring euphemism, unnecessary jargon, or unexplained acronyms.
- **P012** — Build modular, navigable guides with small functional task units, meaningful headings, useful overviews, and reader progress points.
- **P020** — Design user guides to support starting, productivity, troubleshooting, and experience-level differences.
- **P048** — Serve multiple proficiency levels with predictable novice support and efficient reference for experienced users.
- **P076** — For procedural (cookbook-style) manuals use a prerequisites list plus numbered steps and control the information flow so the reader is not overloaded; never combine multiple tasks in a single sentence.
- **P077** — For presentations, resolve the inherent ambiguity of bullet-point slides by requesting the speaker's notes or the full paper, and watch translation length.
- **P078** — Understand why a text is structured as it is.
- **P097** — Make tutorials gentle, supportive, and never confusing, too-advanced, or wrong.
- **P102** — Protect reader safety and prevent product damage before pursuing other instructional goals.
- **P104** — Treat administrator help as a guide-failure signal by directing users to the guide first and counting each escalation.
- **P106** — For reference manuals and any cross-referenced document, treat topics as independent, dipped-into units.

## Usability Testing And Evaluation

Skill: `usability-testing-and-evaluation`

- **P011** — Subject user documentation to legal, standards, accuracy, completeness, safety, readability, layout, and hands-on usability quality assurance.
- **P023** — Build usability from the start using cognetics, explicit goals, and measurable criteria.
- **P029** — Implement usability through a hierarchy of principles, guidelines, local rules, and style guides, while still validating with users.
- **P030** — Establish usability with evaluation rather than design confidence alone.
- **P033** — Screen and consent participants with role-specific profiles, data-collection disclosure, confidentiality, and clear session tracking.
- **P049** — Build guide quality early and improve it iteratively with user involvement and pilot studies.
- **P050** — Triangulate subjective ratings with objective performance and recall measures.
- **P061** — Familiarize participants with context, acclimate them to recording, and capture useful informal comments without letting them drive scoring.
- **P068** — Investigate the translation process with think-aloud protocols triangulated with video observation, interviews and questionnaires, keystroke logging, and eye-tracking.
- **P073** — Prepare users and materials so participants critique the product and guide, not themselves, without being distracted by avoidable text errors.
- **P074** — Use screen logging or recording suited to the application interaction style instead of over-shoulder observation.

## Chinese Naturalness And De-Europeanization

Skill: `chinese-naturalness-and-de-europeanization`

- **P082** — For ordinary Chinese prose, preserve concision, flexible syntax, and rhythmic force; resist Europeanized wording when it makes the sentence needlessly complex or stiff.
- **P083** — Let coordinate Chinese words or phrases stand by juxtaposition when natural, and choose connectors that match progression, contrast, or adjustment instead of defaulting to English-like and-linking.
- **P120** — Recognize that the translator's chief obstacle is often not the source language's difficulty but the dead, conventional register of his own language, and that there is no single target language but a.
- **P131** — Use established Chinese idioms as live resources, not as decorative substitutes for thought, and do not flatten them into wordy paraphrase when the idiom fits.
- **P132** — Prefer concrete subjects, people, and event clauses over abstract nominalized subjects when revising Chinese sentences influenced by English syntax.
- **P133** — Replace weak-verb plus abstract-noun constructions with direct Chinese verbs whenever the phrase only repackages a plain action.
- **P134** — Do not use pseudo-technical abstract nouns merely to sound objective or elevated; revise to direct adjectives, nouns, or everyday expressions when precision does not improve.
- **P135** — Avoid redundant plural marking in Chinese when quantity words, collective nouns, or context already make plurality clear.
- **P136** — Avoid imported one-of and as-one-of structures when class membership is obvious; recast the sentence so the main subject is not buried under a heavy membership phrase.
- **P137** — Avoid over-explicit causal scaffolding in Chinese; remove formal because/therefore markers when sequence and context already convey the relation, and do not use a because-clause as the subject.
- **P138** — Do not force Chinese idiomatic verb phrases into stiff adverbial forms, and delete empty success adverbs when the verb already entails completion.
- **P139** — Reduce mechanical modifier-particle chains by deleting needless particles, using parallel or connective phrasing, or restoring verb clauses so modifier hierarchy stays clear.
- **P140** — Prefer active or agentive Chinese constructions over imported passive patterns, and when passive meaning is needed choose an idiomatic passive expression suited to the event and register.
- **P141** — Distinguish ordinary prose correction from deliberate literary innovation.

## Pragmatics, Culture And Politeness

Skill: `pragmatics-culture-and-politeness`

- **P024** — Beware that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail to help the target reader can destroy the indirect, figurative meaning it carries across a text.
- **P026** — Evaluate ELF communication by mutual intelligibility, efficiency, and multilingual multicompetence rather than by native-speaker correctness alone.
- **P031** — For culturally nonmatching referents, choose deliberately among formal term plus note, functional equivalent, borrowed term with classifier, and receptor-language description.
- **P095** — Weigh politeness and taboo against accuracy.
- **P129** — Weight the seriousness of a face-threatening act as a cultural variable set by the social distance and relative power of the speakers.
- **P148** — Respect the target reader's sense of what is socially and textually appropriate, the context for Be polite, including conventions like the expected calendar, and adjust modes of address, titles, name.

## Professional Practice And Revision

- **P111** — When revising another translator's work, find and fix errors rather than rewrite it as your own.
- **P112** — Never use footnotes in a professional translation to flag confusion or queries.
