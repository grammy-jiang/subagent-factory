# Write clear technical prose

## Purpose

Make a draft clearer for its audience using Google's technical-writing guidance: shorter, more
active sentences, focused paragraphs, parallel lists, and consistent, defined terms. The aim is
prose that reads faster, is easier to maintain, and does not assume the reader shares the
author's expertise.

## When to use

- A draft is dense, passive, or hard to follow and the author wants it tightened.
- A reviewer must point at specific clarity problems and their fixes, not give a vague "make it
  clearer".
- Terms are used inconsistently, or paragraphs and lists are doing too much.

Do not use this skill to decide a page's Diátaxis type — classify first; clarity applies within
any type.

## Procedure

### Sentences

1. **Prefer the active voice** (actor + verb + target) so each sentence names who does what to
   whom; convert passive sentences unless the actor is genuinely unknown. (P011)

2. **Keep sentences short and focused on a single idea.** Split off any subordinate clause that
   branches into a separate idea. Shorter sentences read faster and are easier to maintain.
   (P055, P056)

3. **Choose precise, strong verbs** and reduce weak generic ones (forms of "be", "occur",
   "happen"); treat a generic verb as a smell to fix. Cut filler words and replace bloated verb
   phrases with one precise verb. (P031, P058)

### Paragraphs

4. **Focus each paragraph on a single topic** as an independent unit of logic; cut or move any
   sentence that drifts off it. (P057)

5. **Lead each paragraph with its central point**, because readers focus on (and may read only)
   the opening sentence. (P059)

### Words and terms

6. **Use one unambiguous term per concept** and apply it consistently; never rename a concept
   part-way through. (P054)

7. **Introduce a long name's short form before using it** — spell out an acronym with the
   acronym in parentheses on first use — then use the short form consistently. (P010)

8. **Use a pronoun only when its referent is unambiguous**: introduce the noun first and keep the
   pronoun close to it. (P014)

### Lists and tables

9. **Convert prose to lists where it helps**: a bulleted list for unordered items, a numbered
   list for ordered ones. (P030)

10. **Keep list items parallel** in grammar, logical category, capitalization, and punctuation —
    the first item sets the pattern. (P022)

### Finish

11. **Read the result back against the audience.** If a term, sentence, or paragraph still
    assumes knowledge the reader lacks, simplify or define it.

## Inputs

- **Required:** the draft text and its intended audience.
- **Optional:** a project style guide to defer to on specific conventions.

## Output

A clearer revision, or — in review — a list of specific clarity findings (sentence, paragraph,
term, or list), each with the concrete fix and the principle it serves.
