# Participative Working Groups

## Purpose

Constitute, task, and run the small cross-level working groups that form the standing
participative structure around an incentive payment scheme. These groups are where
productivity problems are identified and solved jointly by people at different levels;
the payment system then rewards the improvement they produce rather than causing it.
The skill also enforces the boundary rule that separates issues fit for team decision
from those that belong to normal negotiation channels, and describes the co-ordinating
subcommittee that keeps the groups functioning.

## When to use

- A management team wants advice on establishing a participative structure of small
  cross-level working groups alongside a payment scheme.
- Leadership needs to know how to constitute, task, and run those groups — including
  how decisions are reached, which problems are in scope, and which issues to exclude.
- An organisation needs to understand how the teams relate to the payment system and
  what ongoing oversight the structure requires.

## Inputs

- The sections, departments, functions, or projects whose work is closely inter-dependent.
- The levels of staff involved (e.g. shop-floor workers, supervisors, managers; or
  white-collar equivalents).
- The productivity problems management wants tackled, and company priorities and future
  plans from which team tasks can be drawn.
- Whether the programme has already reached the point of needing a co-ordinating
  subcommittee (i.e. teams are operating or about to operate).

## Procedure

### Stage 1 — Establish the managers' team first

1. **Form the managers' team as the essential first step.** Because the approach
   requires managers to change how they reach decisions, and because their commitment
   depends on being involved in setting up the system, the managers' team must be
   constituted before any workforce teams.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

2. **Run the management-team programme of work.** Cover four areas with this team:
   - How operating through participative teams changes their decision-making
     responsibilities, and why decisions should be opened for prior discussion with those
     affected.
   - How to cope with proposals arising from the teams: responding promptly, showing that
     things get done, maintaining flexibility, encouraging adequate preparation of proposals,
     and reviewing how much change has resulted from team proposals.
   - Which areas of the company each team will represent, and where to start.
   - Identification of one or preferably two suitable tasks per team — matters requiring
     real work on a one-to-six-month time-scale, with longer projects divided into stages
     where possible. Each manager should take a large part in identifying tasks for teams in
     his areas and should prepare suitable remits with back-up information.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

### Stage 2 — Engage the workforce

3. **Present the proposals to the intended workforce.** The first step is to secure their
   willingness to take part. A well-prepared presentation of the proposals should gain
   support, since the approach gives people influence over decisions affecting their work.
   Ensure trade union representatives understand what the project involves, how it has
   worked elsewhere, and the role they can play; their support will assist acceptance among
   members.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0012]

4. **Invite nominations or volunteers and select joint chairmen.** Ask the workforce to
   nominate or volunteer to form a team for their area (around six people per team) and to
   select one of these as their joint chairman. The management team assigns a supervisor
   as joint chairman and confirms that person's willingness, along with any other
   management or support staff needed for each team.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0012]

5. **Brief all joint chairmen.** Hold discussions with all joint chairmen covering what the
   team should do; its remit and procedures; how to chair a meeting; how to write a report;
   meeting administration and communications; how the company operates (systems,
   procedures, manufacturing methods, economics); and any other issues the management team
   or the joint chairmen identify.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0012]

### Stage 3 — Compose and run the working groups

6. **Compose each group across levels.** Set up groups of six to eight people drawn from
   different organisational levels whose work is closely inter-dependent — from the same
   section, department, function, or project. Apply the same cross-level pattern to
   white-collar sections.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

7. **Use cross-section or temporary teams where work crosses boundaries.** Where
   departmental boundaries may need to change, or a project affects several sections, draw
   a team from different sections. Such a team may be temporary if the project does not
   require a permanent re-alignment; otherwise teams are usually permanent.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

8. **Rotate membership periodically.** Change membership from time to time to give others
   the chance to take part — for example by replacing two members annually.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

9. **Feed the group both kinds of problem.** Have the group meet fairly regularly on two
   kinds of productivity problem: those the group itself puts forward as ways of improving
   productivity, and those management feeds into the group for solution. Ensure both kinds
   are tackled and that decisions reached are then implemented.
   [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

10. **Reach decisions by informed consensus, with the manager retaining authority.** Base
    decisions on full information aimed at consensus or best compromise. The manager retains
    authority and responsibility for the final decision, but the team will have influenced
    it; even members who would have preferred another course will know why this one was
    chosen and will have contributed their ideas to making it work.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

11. **Hold the negotiation boundary.** Keep within the team's remit all issues associated
    with getting the work done more effectively — methods of working, ways of organising
    work, systems for increasing efficiency, changes in technology. Recognise issues on
    which management and the workforce are likely to disagree fundamentally and transfer them
    to normal negotiation channels as soon as possible, or keep them out of team discussions
    from the outset.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

12. **Support managers through the change of style.** For some managers the participative
    approach is a minor adjustment; for others it is a substantially different way of
    reaching decisions that supervisors and managers may find hard to accept. Provide advice
    and encouragement until they can see for themselves how effective this management style
    is.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0010]

### Stage 4 — Establish ongoing oversight

13. **Set up the co-ordinating subcommittee.** Once teams are operating, constitute a small
    group of four or five sufficiently senior people who meet the joint chairmen of each team
    for about an hour each month. The subcommittee discusses how each team is progressing,
    what problems it has encountered, and what it has achieved. Members must be senior enough
    to resolve difficulties, including insisting on action when a team has agreed changes but
    no action has followed.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0015]

### Stage 5 — Link the groups to the payment system

14. **Assign measurement and payment-link design as a team task.** One task for the teams
    can be to determine how departmental or section performance can best be measured and
    related to payment from a performance-related incentive scheme. Devise the simplest
    possible mechanism and links, since the extra pay functions as a reward for the effort
    and improved performance the participating teams have produced — not as the force that
    motivates the improvement.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0016]

15. **Treat incentive pay as reward, not driver.** The driving force for improved efficiency
    must come from good management and the involvement of everyone in a participatory
    programme of performance improvement. Incentive pay facilitates co-operation and rewards
    results; it is not the primary motivator.
    [source: payment-systems-and-20260612115310-h0016]

## Output

A defined set of participative working groups with:
- Cross-level membership drawn from inter-dependent sections, departments, functions, or
  projects (six to eight people per group).
- A problem agenda covering both group-generated and management-supplied productivity
  problems.
- A decision rule: informed consensus with the manager retaining final authority.
- A rotation policy (e.g. two members replaced annually).
- Joint-chairman appointments and briefing records.
- An explicit boundary list distinguishing issues that stay with the team from those that
  go to negotiation channels.
- A co-ordinating subcommittee of four or five senior people meeting joint chairmen monthly.
- A payment-link task assigned to each team, designing simple performance measures and
  reward connections for their section.
