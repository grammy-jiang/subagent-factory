# Pilot and Extension

## Purpose

Put the constituted participative teams into operation as a pilot, then extend the programme
to other areas of the company, while establishing the co-ordination, review, and payment-link
machinery that keeps the participative structure functioning and prevents it drifting after
launch. This is the run-and-sustain stage that follows scheme design and team constitution.

## When to use

- The participative teams have been formed, tasked, and briefed and are ready to begin
  operating.
- A management team wants guidance on the review, co-ordination, and payment-link arrangements
  that are needed to sustain participation beyond the initial pilot period.
- The programme has been running but lacks a structured review cycle or co-ordinating
  subcommittee, and the teams are showing signs of drift or declining momentum.

## Inputs

- Constituted participative teams with their joint chairmen identified.
- A candidate area in which to run the pilot teams first, and the further areas to extend to
  afterwards.
- A volunteer willing to be seconded to a full-time co-ordinator role for one or two years.
- A new performance-related incentive scheme (or a commitment to design one) to which the
  teams can relate their departmental or section performance.

## Procedure

1. **Recruit a full-time co-ordinator.** Once most people in the company understand the
   exercise and the commitment the company is giving it, call for a volunteer to be seconded
   for one or two years as the full-time Participative Team Co-ordinator. This person is
   responsible for ensuring that the teams operate smoothly and are adequately serviced
   throughout the programme.

2. **Run pilot teams, then extend.** Set the teams into operation, starting with the candidate
   pilot area. After the pilot is established, extend the programme to the other areas of the
   company identified in the management team's programme of work.

3. **Hold a six-monthly review.** About six months after the teams begin operating, hold the
   first review as a presentation of relevant information to a group comprising all the joint
   chairmen of existing teams together with members of management and, where relevant,
   trade-union representatives. Conduct this review on a regular basis thereafter to resolve
   difficulties as they arise, answer questions, and provide greater impetus where necessary.

4. **Stand up a co-ordinating subcommittee.** Form a small group of four or five people to
   meet the joint chairmen of each team for about an hour each month. The agenda covers how
   each team is progressing, what problems it has encountered, and what it has achieved.
   Members of the subcommittee must be senior enough to resolve difficulties — for example by
   insisting that action is taken when a team has agreed to changes but nothing has happened.

5. **Link the teams to the payment system.** Give each team the task of determining how its
   departmental or section performance can best be measured and related to pay under the
   performance-related incentive scheme. Devise the simplest possible mechanism and links.

6. **Frame extra pay as reward, not driver.** The additional pay is a reward for the extra
   effort and improved performance that results from the changes the teams make — not the
   motivating force behind improvement. Improvement is driven by good management and by the
   involvement of everyone in a participatory programme of performance improvement; the
   payment scheme facilitates that involvement rather than replacing it.

## Output

A running and sustained participative structure comprising:

- A set of pilot teams that have been extended to other areas of the company.
- A full-time Participative Team Co-ordinator seconded for one to two years.
- A six-monthly review cycle bringing together joint chairmen, management, and (where
  relevant) trade-union representatives.
- A monthly co-ordinating subcommittee of four or five senior people.
- A simplest-possible payment link that rewards (rather than drives) the productivity
  improvement the teams generate.
