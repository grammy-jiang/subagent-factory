# Scheme Subversion and Decay Diagnosis

## Purpose

Diagnose why an incentive payment scheme has not delivered the expected performance
improvement, by checking it against the two principal failure modes the source identifies:
the scheme being modified or subverted by individuals or groups pursuing other objectives,
and the gradual weakening or decay of the intended policy as it is experienced lower down the
organisation. The skill ties each finding to its failure mode and to whether the necessary
operating and control systems were installed, and recommends a periodic review cadence to
guard against continuing drift.

## When to use

- An existing incentive payment scheme has not delivered the expected performance improvement
  and the caller needs a diagnosis of whether weak consultation, missing operating controls,
  or gradual subversion of the scheme is the cause.
- A scheme is being reviewed for the risk of drift before it is extended to additional areas.
- Management wants to establish a scheduled review process that will catch subversion or decay
  before it becomes irreversible.

## Inputs

- An account of the scheme as senior management intended it — the strategic policy or
  objective the scheme was meant to serve.
- An account of how the scheme is actually experienced and operated at shop-floor or
  operational level.
- Information on what operating and control systems were installed alongside the scheme when
  it was introduced.
- Details of any other change interventions (for example, new technology) that accompanied
  the scheme's introduction.
- The extent and form of consultation that took place during scheme design and implementation.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Reconstruct the intended policy

Establish what the scheme was originally meant to achieve: the firm commitment at senior
level, the strategic policy or objective it was designed to serve. Record this as a baseline
for comparison with what happens further down the organisation.

### Step 2 — Test for modification or subversion (Failure Mode 1)

Check whether the strategic policy has been modified or subverted by lower levels of
management pursuing different goals and objectives. Note that this failure mode is
particularly associated with situations where other change interventions have been made
alongside the payment scheme — for example, the introduction of new technology — so examine
the scheme in the context of any such accompanying changes.

### Step 3 — Test for gradual decay (Failure Mode 2)

Check how the policy is seen and experienced by those lower down the organisation. The
effects at shop-floor level of what was originally a firm senior-level commitment are
governed by how the policy is perceived and experienced there, and they can differ greatly
from what was intended. Identify where the intended policy has gradually weakened as it has
passed down the organisation.

### Step 4 — Check for missing operating and control systems

Determine whether adequate operating or control systems were installed when the scheme was
introduced. Their absence can worsen the gradual weakening of the policy and is a primary
reason a scheme fails to make its intended impact. Flag this explicitly wherever decay is
found, because the failure to install such systems is a distinct contributing cause, not
merely a symptom.

### Step 5 — Check the consultation that preceded the scheme

Success is associated with the extent of consultation about design and implementation of a
scheme. Weak or absent consultation is therefore a root cause to record alongside
subversion or decay findings, not a separate matter. Assess how deeply and broadly employees
were consulted across levels and sections before and during the scheme's introduction.

### Step 6 — Assess the review cadence

Determine whether the scheme has been subject to regular review since its introduction.
Progress and achievements should be reviewed on a periodic basis — approximately every six
months in the first instance — to resolve difficulties, answer questions, and provide
greater impetus where necessary. A review of this kind should bring together relevant joint
chairmen or team representatives, management, and, where applicable, trade union
representatives. The absence of such a review process removes the mechanism for catching
and correcting subversion or decay before it becomes entrenched.

### Step 7 — Tie each finding to a corrective recommendation

For each finding, name the failure mode it belongs to and recommend the concrete correction:

- For **modification/subversion**: identify the management level at which the diversion is
  occurring and restore the original strategic intent through the participative structure.
- For **gradual decay**: install or reinstate the operating and control systems that were
  absent or neglected, re-establish the intended policy through involvement and consultation,
  and confirm that a regular review cadence is in place to detect further drift.
- For **weak consultation**: recommend widening participation to cover the levels and sections
  of staff affected, since involvement and consultation during design and implementation are
  a primary means of minimising drift and decay.
- For **absent review**: institute a scheduled six-monthly review that surfaces progress,
  difficulties, and questions across joint chairmen, management, and representative bodies.

## Output

A diagnosis of the underperforming scheme that states:

1. Which failure mode or modes are present — modification or subversion by groups pursuing
   other objectives, gradual decay of the intended policy, or both.
2. Whether the necessary operating and control systems were installed when the scheme was
   introduced.
3. The state of the consultation that preceded the scheme.
4. Whether a periodic review process has been established and, if not, how its absence has
   contributed to the current state.
5. A concrete corrective recommendation tied to each named failure mode.
