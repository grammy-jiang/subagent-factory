# Choosing Advisers Groups And Positions

## Purpose

This skill guides evaluating where to train or work — a graduate adviser, research group,
postdoc position, or industrial or government lab — by weighing actual mentoring quality
over reputation, gathering trainee evidence before committing, scoring postdocs on a defined
field and a path to visible results, and probing how a lab manages priorities, resources,
and internal mobility, so the choice rests on protected access, guidance, credited output,
and mobility rather than name recognition.

## When to use

- A graduate adviser or research group is being chosen and reputation is being
  weighed against actual mentoring quality (P010).
- Evidence about a prospective adviser needs to be gathered from current or
  former trainees before committing (P033).
- A prospective group or faculty role is being researched ahead of an interview
  or application (P011).
- Postdoctoral positions are being compared against one another (P021).
- A position in an industrial or government research laboratory is on the
  table, including how it is run day to day and what it offers as a route back
  toward an academic career (P034, P024).

## Procedure

1. When choosing between advisers, prefer the established adviser only when
   other mentoring qualities are otherwise comparable; when they are not
   comparable, choose the adviser who actually protects access, guidance,
   continuity, and independent recognition, even if that adviser is less
   well known (P010).
2. Before committing to a prospective group, research it directly, arrive with
   informed questions, and be ready to explain a genuine complement between the
   candidate's own goals and the group's actual work; for a faculty role,
   prepare the teaching contribution as part of the same case (P011).
3. When comparing postdoctoral positions, score each against four concrete
   criteria together, not any one alone: a defined field, prompt access to work
   that is already significant, a visible path to a completed result, and
   enough visibility from that result to secure the next role (P021).
4. When weighing a research-laboratory position against an academic track,
   treat building a peer-recognized publication record there as viable only
   while two conditions hold at once: the work stays scientifically significant
   and it stays publicly visible; if either erodes, reconsider the case for a
   later senior academic role built on that record (P024).
5. To evaluate a prospective adviser, gather trainee evidence on five points:
   accessibility, real (not nominal) guidance, support extended to beginners,
   the quality of group interaction, and whether members can articulate the
   group's larger scientific purpose (P033).
6. Before joining a prospective laboratory, investigate how it manages
   priorities, resources, performance, collaboration, and internal mobility,
   since these mechanisms determine both the constraint a poor supervisory fit
   creates and the candidate's ability to escape it from within (P034).

## Inputs

- The adviser, group, position, or laboratory under consideration, what is
  known about how it actually works, and the candidate's career needs.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the goal, the plan or
  practice in place, and any claim of importance, readiness, or soundness made.

## Output

Shape the output to the mode the request calls for:

- **advise** — one recommendation tied to the situation, naming the principle(s) it engages and the residual trade-off or referral.
- **review** — a findings list keyed to area; per finding name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the trade-off or referral, and end with a concrete next step, highest-impact first.
- **plan** — an ordered plan of steps, each tied to its principle and scoped to the horizon.

Whichever mode: this skill advises on research-career and empirical practice; it does not run the study, write the paper or grant, or make the hiring, admission, funding, or tenure decision for the caller.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A candidate picks the more famous adviser by name recognition alone, without
  comparing actual mentoring quality, access, guidance, continuity, or
  independent recognition against a less prominent alternative (P010).
- A candidate goes into a group visit or interview able to recite only the
  lab's publication list, with no informed questions prepared and no
  explanation of how the candidate's own goals complement the group's actual
  work (P011).
- A postdoc offer is accepted or recommended on stipend or location alone,
  with no check on whether the field is well defined, access to significant
  work is prompt, a path to a completed result exists, or the position gives
  enough visibility for the next role (P021).
- A researcher keeps pursuing an academic career from inside a research
  laboratory whose projects have drifted into internal, unpublished, or
  low-visibility work, without noticing that the publication record has
  stopped being peer-recognized (P024).
- An adviser's suitability is judged from the adviser's own account or general
  reputation alone, with no interview of current or former trainees about
  accessibility, real guidance, support for beginners, group interaction, or
  shared understanding of the group's larger purpose (P033).
- A candidate joins a laboratory without asking how it actually manages
  priorities, resources, performance reviews, collaboration, or internal
  mobility, leaving no way to tell in advance whether a poor supervisory fit
  could later be escaped from within (P034).
