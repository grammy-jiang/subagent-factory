# Writing And Publishing Scientific Work

## Purpose

This skill guides writing up and publishing research so its significance is discoverable and its claims are sound. `## When to use` and `## Procedure` carry the specific checks.

## When to use

- A manuscript is being readied for submission and its readiness is in question: premises and logical links need to be exposed, assumptions distinguished from established results, and known weaknesses disclosed rather than smoothed over (P003).
- A paper's title, abstract, or introduction needs to be drafted or checked so a reader who stops at any one of them can still find the question, contribution, and change in understanding (P006).
- A manuscript reports empirical or computational work whose feasibility or correctness a reviewer needs to independently evaluate, and the write-up must decide what methodological detail stays in the main argument versus supplementary material (P008).
- A manuscript needs a demanding local read and, where the author does not command the publication language professionally, language editing before it goes out — or a referee's report needs to be read as a signal about the exposition rather than dismissed (P009).
- A publication strategy is being decided: whether to consolidate a contribution into a small portfolio of strong papers or split it into many (P012).
- A doctoral dissertation, proposal, confirmation review, or defense needs its chapters organized around one explicit thesis rather than a loose set of studies (P013).
- An abstract and introduction are being drafted together and need to cover problem, gap, contribution, evidence, impact, and limitation in a consistent order (P032).
- A result has been found in a narrow or rough form and its broader, still-supported generalization needs to be identified and stated (P046).

## Procedure

1. Before circulating a manuscript for submission, walk it line by line to expose every premise and logical link, mark which claims are established results versus working assumptions, and disclose every known weakness in the argument or data rather than tightening the wording so the claim reads stronger than the evidence supports — this discipline matters most under an early-career time pressure that tempts a rushed release (P003).
2. Make significance discoverable at every entry point a reader might stop at: an accurate title that names the actual finding, a brief results-focused abstract, and an introduction that explains the question, the contribution, and the change in understanding the paper produces, so a reader who reads only the title or abstract still leaves with the paper's point (P006).
3. Decide what methodological detail belongs in the main argument versus supplementary material: publish the detail an independent evaluator needs to reproduce or verify the result, especially when the finding depends on a method the reviewer may not know, and move optional derivations or specialist procedures out of the core narrative so it stays readable (P008).
4. Before submission, obtain demanding local review from colleagues willing to push back, arrange language editing when the author does not command the publication language professionally, and when a referee's report seems to misunderstand a correct result, treat that as a prompt to improve the exposition rather than to argue the referee is simply wrong — unless the misunderstanding in fact exposes a substantive research flaw (P009).
5. When deciding how to place a body of work in the literature, prefer publishing a small portfolio of high-quality, influential papers over slicing a strong contribution into many weak, minimally differentiated publications that inflate the count while diluting impact (P012).
6. Build a doctoral dissertation around one explicit, coherent thesis rather than a loose collection of studies: organize chapters as a logically ordered body of substantial advances, supply the problem, the prior solutions, and the candidate's contribution and evidence for each, and exclude work that is unrelated to the thesis or merely incremental (P013).
7. Draft the abstract as a self-contained account that states the problem, the gap it addresses, the contribution, the evidence, the impact, and any material limitation — then expand those same points, in the same order, in the introduction, adding the background and motivation the abstract had no room for (P032).
8. Once a result is found, first clean up and explain its underlying logic, then ask how broadly it actually generalizes and recast it into the broadest form the evidence genuinely supports, without silently hiding the scope or limitations that bound where it applies (P046).

## Inputs

- The manuscript, dissertation, or publication plan, its intended venue and audience, and the evidence behind each claim.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the goal, the plan or practice in place, and any claim of importance, readiness, or soundness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on research-career and empirical practice; it does not run the study, write the paper or grant, or make the hiring, admission, funding, or tenure decision for the caller.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A manuscript heads to submission with an unstated assumption presented as an established result, or a known weakness is smoothed out of the text instead of disclosed, so a reader cannot tell where the evidence actually stops (P003).
- A reader who only sees the title or abstract cannot tell what was actually found — the title is generic or process-focused, the abstract buries the result under methods or background, or the introduction opens with broad context instead of the question and contribution (P006).
- A reviewer cannot independently reproduce or check the result because a necessary methodological detail is missing from the paper, or, conversely, the main argument is clogged with derivations and specialist procedure that belonged in supplementary material (P008).
- A manuscript goes out with no demanding local read, obvious language errors survive despite the author not commanding the publication language professionally, or a referee's confusion is dismissed as the referee's error instead of prompting a rewrite of the unclear passage (P009).
- A single strong contribution is split across several thin papers that individually add little, inflating the publication count while diluting the impact any one paper can claim (P012).
- A dissertation reads as a stapled-together set of unrelated or merely incremental studies with no single stated thesis tying the chapters together, or a chapter is included that does not advance the stated thesis (P013).
- The abstract omits the gap, the evidence, the impact, or a material limitation, or the introduction reorders or drops points the abstract raised instead of expanding them in the same sequence (P032).
- A result is reported only in its original narrow form though the underlying logic clearly supports a broader claim, or the write-up generalizes the result beyond what the evidence supports without flagging the extended scope or its limitations (P046).
