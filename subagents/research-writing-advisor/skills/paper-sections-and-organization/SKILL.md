# Paper Sections And Organization

## Purpose

This skill guides the structure of a research paper and how each IMRaD section does its job. It checks that the introduction defines a concrete problem and funnels to a question calibrated to the audience and venue, that Methods are reproducibly complete, that Results are ordered top-down by interpretive dependency, that the Discussion reconnects to the goal and bounds its conclusions and limitations, and that the abstract, title, and conclusions are differentiated from the body rather than duplicating it. It builds each section from its rhetorical functions and checks the form against authentic target-journal examples.

## When to use

- An introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, abstract, conclusions, or title needs writing or reviewing for its section-specific job.
- A paper's overall organization must be built around the argument and the field's expected structure.
- Background, openings, or limitations need calibrating to the audience, venue, and section function.
- A reusable section model is being built from rhetorical functions and verified against field examples.

## Procedure

1. Make the introduction define a concrete problem and narrow to a question (P003).
2. Recast rather than copy overlapping Abstract content (P004).
3. Whether you separate or combine Results and Discussion, ground the choice in making the reader's job easy and letting readers distinguish what you found from what you think; distinguish data, inference, and interpretation, and combine (P005).
4. Organize a Results section top-down by orienting readers and giving an evidence-faithful overall pattern before individual findings, repeating an overview only when a new result set requires a distinct analytical frame (P025).
5. Calibrate background to audience and section function (P030).
6. Tailor the opening to the intended audience and venue, reframing the same core message with the characters and concerns that engage a specific journal or funding agency (P031).
7. Conclude the core interpretive argument with a reliable takeaway that connects findings to the original question and existing knowledge without exceeding supported populations, conditions, or outcomes (P035).
8. Distinguish reporting an observed trend from interpreting it through a model, and keep model-based or subjective interpretation out of standalone Results when the venue expects separation (P046).
9. Use active title wording, searchable nouns, and evidence-backed concrete differentiators; verify uncertain phrasing in a scholarly corpus and replace bare self-praise such as 'novel' with the feature that is new (P048).
10. Choose a Methods opening from target-journal precedent, report completed work in past tense, use present for established methods, and select active or passive voice by focus without turning a research report into a directive protocol (P049).
11. Make Methods reproducibly complete by reporting applicable subjects, setting, design, assumptions, variables, treatment, precautions, equipment, collection, analysis, criteria, advances, and outcome-sensitive quantities, while using (P050).
12. For a tool or apparatus Abstract, emphasize the capability, benefits, performance, and validation; for a problem-solution science Abstract, move from gap through solution and comparison to practical implication with context normally (P060).
13. Build a reusable section model from rhetorical functions, verify it against authentic field examples, learn the language that realizes each function, and use it as a scaffold rather than a permanent constraint (P061).
14. When a limitation affects interpretation, discuss it in the body of the Discussion (P062).
15. Structure Discussion around aim support, comparison, interpretations, limitations, validity, contribution, transfer, implications, and future research; follow applicable reporting standards and open by reconnecting to the goal (P076).
16. Bound any external generalization with evidence and context, and close Discussion with supported takeaways, extensions, improvements, exclusions, implications, recommendations, or future work that complete the interpretive argument (P087).
17. Use the body to define concepts and hypotheses, supply accessible prerequisite background, develop reasoning, present central evidence, and establish the detailed conclusions promised earlier (P091).
18. For a broader audience or a diverse review panel, use a quick two-step opening that first engages the audience's interest and then narrows to your specific focus, keeping it to two steps and delaying your true central characters when (P094).
19. Order findings by interpretive dependency and mark a contrasting, surprising, or especially important result set through paragraph structure and an accurate early relational cue (P095).
20. Use declarative, question, playful, or two-part titles only after checking recent venue practice, and ensure the form delivers a robust answer or distinct specification without sensationalism or repetition (P099).
21. After establishing the research space, identify the paper's purpose, focus, and organization, then preview only enough method and findings to define the contribution and connect those moves into a continuous progression (P101).
22. At the Discussion opening, recall only the background, methodological features, and principal findings needed to launch interpretation, without introducing overdue method detail or repeating Results at length (P106).
23. Use limitations to bound present conclusions and motivate specific, answerable future projects, balancing retained evidence without allowing positive findings to erase a material constraint (P110).
24. Differentiate Conclusions from the Abstract through informed synthesis and added implications, revise the two together for redundancy, and place paper-wide synthesis in Conclusions rather than duplicating it at the Discussion end (P117).
25. Replace assignment echoes, generic definitions, and inflated universal openings with community-specific context; use a fact, anecdote, or permitted quotation only when it anticipates key concepts and illuminates the problem (P119).
26. Report provenance and spatial configuration precisely enough to identify consequential materials, equipment, and component relationships, using figures to complement rather than replace essential prose (P127).
27. When using a prior method, name its source and characterize the present procedure accurately as reproduced, lightly adapted, or substantially changed, elevating only consequential modifications as contributions (P142).
28. Use the flexible Discussion model (P158).
29. Build the introduction body as a funnel from the opening's large problem to the challenge's specific question, framing the knowledge gap (P159).

## Inputs

- The paper or section under discussion, its intended venue and audience, and the field's structural conventions.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the goal, the audience and venue, the draft or plan in place, and any claim of clarity, rigor, or readiness made.

## Output

Per finding: name the gap and the principle it engages, give the correction, state the residual trade-off or the referral to make, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill advises on research writing and presentation; it does not write the paper, section, slides, or talk for the caller, guarantee acceptance or publication, or rule on the domain-science correctness of the research.
