# Research-Writing Principles Index

Package-wide index of every promoted principle, grouped by the skill that owns it. Each entry restates the principle's operative core; the full statement lives in `../principles/principles.yaml`.

## Research Argument And Contribution

Skill: `research-argument-and-contribution`

- **P010** — Treat research design and substantive writing as one iterative activity.
- **P022** — Organize the finished paper around its developed claim and reasons, not discovery history, source sequence, or prompt wording; discard material that does not serve the final argument and conclude.
- **P032** — Turn an exploratory topic into tractable work by stating the broad problem, defining concrete near-term outcomes, grounding the work in a motivating example, and using a meaningful surrogate when.
- **P040** — Estimate a claim's significance by the important beliefs or practices it would revise, the puzzle it resolves, the inquiry it opens.
- **P043** — Position the contribution by stating whether it confirms, contradicts, extends, reframes, or methodologically changes prior work, focusing on established comparators and adding new literature only.
- **P058** — Generate contributions through creative agreement by strengthening evidence, testing assumptions, or extending scope, and through creative disagreement by testing classifications, histories, causes.
- **P065** — State the challenge as a clear, concrete, explicit question describing the knowledge you hope to gain, not as objectives, and never leave it implicit.
- **P071** — Balance confidence and humility, develop the courage to ask new questions and reach for understanding rather than replicating an old story in a new system or with new technology, and find.
- **P075** — Make the inferential bridge explicit.
- **P082** — Audit each major reason as a section, balance it with sufficient relevant evidence, restore missing subreasons, qualify overstatement, address important alternatives collegially, and expose warrants.
- **P090** — Stress-test whether the problem exists and matters, whether the proposed solution fits it and beats alternatives at acceptable cost, and whether every item of evidence is fit, accurate, current.
- **P100** — Judge a research contribution on both originality and validity, recognizing that a valuable new question, synthesis, framework, or rigorous reevaluation can be original even when no wholly new.
- **P109** — Name the actual type of research gap and bound novelty to the documented knowledge search rather than using stronger deficiency or priority language than the literature supports.
- **P116** — Claim applied significance only when the conceptual answer plausibly enables the proposed action; otherwise emphasize the demonstrated conceptual value and label practical use as a possibility.
- **P125** — Prefer grant opportunities where established scholarship creates a credible competitive advantage, and assess the proposal against likely field leaders rather than against an absolute threshold.
- **P130** — Support a practical proposal with evidence about feasibility, implementation burden, proportional cost, unintended harms, and alternatives; if research establishes only understanding, label.
- **P153** — Develop significance from the start by stating the topic, the specific unknown, and the more important understanding the answer enables, then test and refine that account from the audience's.
- **P157** — Calibrate warrant exposition to the audience.
- **P164** — Treat problem discovery as substantive research.

## Paper Sections And Organization

Skill: `paper-sections-and-organization`

- **P003** — Make the introduction define a concrete problem and narrow to a question.
- **P004** — Recast rather than copy overlapping Abstract content.
- **P005** — Whether you separate or combine Results and Discussion, ground the choice in making the reader's job easy and letting readers distinguish what you found from what you think; distinguish data.
- **P025** — Organize a Results section top-down by orienting readers and giving an evidence-faithful overall pattern before individual findings, repeating an overview only when a new result set requires.
- **P030** — Calibrate background to audience and section function.
- **P031** — Tailor the opening to the intended audience and venue, reframing the same core message with the characters and concerns that engage a specific journal or funding agency.
- **P035** — Conclude the core interpretive argument with a reliable takeaway that connects findings to the original question and existing knowledge without exceeding supported populations, conditions.
- **P046** — Distinguish reporting an observed trend from interpreting it through a model, and keep model-based or subjective interpretation out of standalone Results when the venue expects separation.
- **P048** — Use active title wording, searchable nouns, and evidence-backed concrete differentiators; verify uncertain phrasing in a scholarly corpus and replace bare self-praise such as 'novel' with the feature.
- **P049** — Choose a Methods opening from target-journal precedent, report completed work in past tense, use present for established methods, and select active or passive voice by focus without turning.
- **P050** — Make Methods reproducibly complete by reporting applicable subjects, setting, design, assumptions, variables, treatment, precautions, equipment, collection, analysis, criteria, advances.
- **P060** — For a tool or apparatus Abstract, emphasize the capability, benefits, performance, and validation; for a problem-solution science Abstract, move from gap through solution and comparison to practical.
- **P061** — Build a reusable section model from rhetorical functions, verify it against authentic field examples, learn the language that realizes each function, and use it as a scaffold rather than a permanent.
- **P062** — When a limitation affects interpretation, discuss it in the body of the Discussion.
- **P076** — Structure Discussion around aim support, comparison, interpretations, limitations, validity, contribution, transfer, implications, and future research; follow applicable reporting standards and open.
- **P087** — Bound any external generalization with evidence and context, and close Discussion with supported takeaways, extensions, improvements, exclusions, implications, recommendations, or future work.
- **P091** — Use the body to define concepts and hypotheses, supply accessible prerequisite background, develop reasoning, present central evidence, and establish the detailed conclusions promised earlier.
- **P094** — For a broader audience or a diverse review panel, use a quick two-step opening that first engages the audience's interest and then narrows to your specific focus, keeping it to two steps and delaying.
- **P095** — Order findings by interpretive dependency and mark a contrasting, surprising, or especially important result set through paragraph structure and an accurate early relational cue.
- **P099** — Use declarative, question, playful, or two-part titles only after checking recent venue practice, and ensure the form delivers a robust answer or distinct specification without sensationalism.
- **P101** — After establishing the research space, identify the paper's purpose, focus, and organization, then preview only enough method and findings to define the contribution and connect those moves.
- **P106** — At the Discussion opening, recall only the background, methodological features, and principal findings needed to launch interpretation, without introducing overdue method detail or repeating Results.
- **P110** — Use limitations to bound present conclusions and motivate specific, answerable future projects, balancing retained evidence without allowing positive findings to erase a material constraint.
- **P117** — Differentiate Conclusions from the Abstract through informed synthesis and added implications, revise the two together for redundancy, and place paper-wide synthesis in Conclusions rather.
- **P119** — Replace assignment echoes, generic definitions, and inflated universal openings with community-specific context; use a fact, anecdote, or permitted quotation only when it anticipates key concepts.
- **P127** — Report provenance and spatial configuration precisely enough to identify consequential materials, equipment, and component relationships, using figures to complement rather than replace essential.
- **P142** — When using a prior method, name its source and characterize the present procedure accurately as reproduced, lightly adapted, or substantially changed, elevating only consequential modifications.
- **P158** — Use the flexible Discussion model.
- **P159** — Build the introduction body as a funnel from the opening's large problem to the challenge's specific question, framing the knowledge gap.

## Narrative Structure And Paragraphs

Skill: `narrative-structure-and-paragraphs`

- **P002** — Choose between point-first.
- **P017** — Do not distract in the resolution.
- **P053** — Create unexpectedness through a framed knowledge gap.
- **P068** — Give a contribution or focus shift a new paragraph, and use paragraph length as a unity diagnostic so multi-stage blocks become visually navigable without splitting one coherent idea arbitrarily.
- **P111** — Make the paragraph the unit of composition, a complete short story with a coherent structure that fits the larger work; treat the grade-school topic-sentence model as a simplified lead/development.
- **P112** — Do not write a weak resolution that only asserts importance.

## Clarity And Sentence Style

Skill: `clarity-and-sentence-style`

- **P001** — Build on the reader's schemas.
- **P006** — Distinguish jargon (a term for a schema the reader lacks or that has a plain-language equivalent) from a necessary technical term, use the terms that work for your audience, control whether a term.
- **P008** — Prefer the active voice as a matter of course because it is clear, direct, and visual; use the passive deliberately only to make the acted-on the subject or to honestly avoid naming the actor.
- **P011** — When a sentence's point is unclear, rewrite it into the active voice and decide which story it tells; read your writing aloud to catch what the eye skips; package each sentence into compact OCAR.
- **P020** — Translate broad implications into accessible, specific statements naming the action, population, unaffected factor, source of risk, consequence, and preventive response while preserving technical.
- **P021** — Treat many clause-separating commas or chains of descriptions as a reorganization signal.
- **P037** — During revision, diagnose nominalized actions as possible sources of vague verbs, extra phrasing, and hidden actors, then restore specific verbs when meaning and useful concepts are preserved.
- **P038** — Take simple ideas seriously because grasping is not understanding and simple is not easy; avoid needless complication, and note that simple ideas compose into complex theories while a simple change.
- **P041** — Prefer short familiar wording without sacrificing exact technical meaning, replace broad labels with the measured property, and repeat the same technical term when synonym variation could imply.
- **P047** — Counter author familiarity by testing every sentence from the reader's perspective for purpose, motivation, necessity, and avoidable complexity.
- **P051** — Keep relative and participial clauses only for compact, unambiguous additions; otherwise create a new finite sentence with a precise demonstrative noun phrase or repeated subject, ordered.
- **P055** — Match emphasis to evidential importance.
- **P056** — Put the action in your verbs and place them early, avoiding verb and adjective nominalizations and recovering any action a nominalization has pushed out of position, while using a nominalization.
- **P064** — Design research prose for low-friction evaluation.
- **P070** — Be concrete: prefer specific, definite language, ground mid-level abstractions in widely understood schemas or the concrete details that explain them, and link each abstract concept to a concrete.
- **P077** — Beware the curse of knowledge, where knowing too much and writing too little overcondenses ideas so connections obvious to you are invisible to the reader; write and fix any unit by identifying who.
- **P096** — Cut redundancies and obvious words that state the well-known or implied, and write with strong nouns and verbs rather than decorating weak words.
- **P115** — Be professional without being pedantic.
- **P121** — Follow familiar conventions when they reduce reader effort and optimize prose for clarity, correctness, directness, and interest, departing only for a communicative benefit that exceeds.
- **P146** — Make a hard sentence tractable by starting from a concept the reader already knows and placing an unfamiliar term in the low-emphasis middle if they know it, dropping it if they do not, or moving it.
- **P151** — Use an isolated short sentence sparingly to give a pivotal qualification or conclusion extra weight, with its connection to surrounding prose clearly signaled.

## Academic English For Non-Native Writers

Skill: `academic-english-for-non-native-writers`

- **P033** — Choose tense as a rhetorical signal.
- **P079** — Apply journal-aware scientific form to articles and numbers.
- **P080** — Take responsibility as the author for submitting correct English.
- **P085** — Determine noun countability from the intended contextual meaning, give singular count nouns appropriate determiners, and select indefinite or definite reference according to reader identifiability.
- **P102** — Keep grammatically required title articles, use them to distinguish new from specific referents and partial from exhaustive coverage, choose 'a/an' by sound, and check field usage when convention.
- **P126** — Distinguish author-planned future work from community recommendations through commitment-appropriate modals, and formulate recommendations with a grammatical that-clause and base verb.
- **P137** — Choose Discussion voice from journal practice but use first-person possessives, named authors, citations, or study labels wherever passive wording would blur attribution.
- **P169** — Signal evidence ownership at the sentence start and combine tense, named authors, citations, and study-display references; then state the substantive relationship when moving between prior.

## Figures, Tables And Data Display

Skill: `figures-tables-and-data-display`

- **P028** — Interpret quantities against an explicit relevant baseline, retain exact values when readers need them, and ensure the framing supports rather than rhetorically inflates the eventual conclusion.
- **P059** — Before reporting an indirectly calculated value, provide or cite the necessary theory, assumptions, derivation, and valid baseline, and connect distributed displays so readers can trace evidence.
- **P063** — Put units in axes and columns, expand compact labels naturally in prose or captions, explain representative values when scale may be misread, and update every embedded label after terminology changes.
- **P084** — Use figures for intrinsically spatial data-structure relationships and language-neutral mathematical notation for broad audiences, while interpreting every figure and retaining code syntax when.
- **P103** — Show a result's importance through concrete contrast, magnitude, or pattern; make displays evidence for prose claims, report only key values, and give each legend enough information to stand alone.
- **P118** — Prefer familiar basic graphics unless a more complex field-appropriate form reveals relationships that simpler forms cannot, and let the argument rather than software defaults determine design.
- **P124** — Give each figure and table a number, a skimmable caption that identifies major elements and parameters, and nearby main-text introduction and interpretation; omit visuals the argument never uses.
- **P152** — Present statistics against the right denominator, such as population size, rather than raw totals.

## Literature And Source Use

Skill: `literature-and-source-use`

- **P007** — Map candidate literature by paper type, venue, citation relationships, authors, institutions, dates, and meaningful topic groups, while treating citation counts and search rank as prominence-biased.
- **P012** — Introduce complex evidence with the reason or interpretation readers should draw, and provide complete accurate citations and source lists so they can audit evidence and assess the research base.
- **P014** — Organize literature by a chronology, theme, or method that reveals progress, achievements, limits, gap, and response; within each unit synthesize studies and choose author- or finding-prominent.
- **P016** — Do not closely imitate source prose, figures, or paper design; a citation does not authorize copied wording, so quote a precisely marked permitted span or produce an independently written attributed.
- **P026** — Paraphrase by first understanding and citing the attributable idea, then restructuring its grammar and order while preserving technical terms, qualifiers, meaningful sequence, and original epistemic.
- **P029** — Build the literature review continuously as a topic-organized critical synthesis of contributions, limits, relationships, and open questions, preserving displaced early material so reading is not.
- **P074** — Search literature iteratively through complementary paths, using discoveries to refine terminology and criteria, separating broad collection from critical evaluation, and repeating the search.
- **P078** — Use libraries, librarians, expert reference works, literature reviews, and abstracts to map a field, refine search parameters, find access routes, and select material for close reading.
- **P086** — Criticize earlier work only when relevant, explain why its original interpretation was reasonable, ground the revision in stronger evidence or known limits, and seek conditional reconciliation before.
- **P122** — Use a structured comparison matrix for directly relevant studies to expose differences in problems, methods, results, strengths, limitations, bias, implications, and the new study's extension.
- **P138** — Quote only when exact wording is the object of attention, mark and cite it correctly, and otherwise demonstrate understanding through paraphrase rather than filling the paper with quotations.
- **P140** — Assess both legal rights and academic ethics when owning or reusing intellectual creations because copyright, patents, publication agreements, and plagiarism standards protect different interests.
- **P156** — Read an important rival first for its strongest coherent meaning and again for critical response; summarize it accurately, inspect its support, and verify every consequential representation before.

## Evidence Integrity And Claims

Skill: `evidence-integrity-and-claims`

- **P015** — Build credibility through an unbroken chain from prior work to conclusions.
- **P018** — Support a novel algorithm with the literature, stated limitations, credible implementation, appropriate data, sufficient tests, and an objective explanation that lets skeptical readers judge novelty.
- **P034** — Test alternative explanations, interpret negative results as possible failures of test or implementation as well as hypothesis, and sanity-check outputs against invariants, totals, boundaries.
- **P036** — Use proofs, models, simulations, and real experiments as complementary but bounded evidence.
- **P042** — Audit conventional research-language formulations for concealed evidential defects rather than allowing euphemism to hide missing support, failed work, selective samples, unreadable evidence, or poor.
- **P067** — Hedge with precision rather than vagueness.
- **P069** — Grant authorship only for significant intellectual contribution to conception, execution, or interpretation; directed coding, proofreading, funding, management, reward, or favor does not qualify.
- **P081** — Present weaknesses, bias, and methodological limits as visibly as strengths, incorporate them into alternative explanations, keep achievement claims proportional, and hedge only claims whose.
- **P092** — Replace inflated scientific language with operational definitions and scrutinize work that evades evidence, ignores contrary advances, fails to develop under new data, or reports implausibly perfect.
- **P093** — Choose significance thresholds for context, interpret significance as evidence against the null rather than certainty, and interpret nonsignificance as inconclusive rather than proof of the null.
- **P104** — Scope conclusions to tested cases and factors, avoid untested rankings or informal equivalence, and interpret quantitative differences only according to the metric's measurement scale.
- **P105** — Let the intended measurement determine experimental software scope, building the simplest probe that preserves realistic behavior rather than an unnecessary complete system.
- **P120** — Keep conventional research reports focused on evidence-bearing material, omitting routine false starts unless a failure itself yields an important lesson or the genre explicitly calls for reflection.
- **P133** — Maintain an immutable dated record of methods, versions, inputs, parameters, outputs, interpretations, decisions, and failures, and share code and data when rights and constraints permit.
- **P136** — Signal a supported achievement explicitly and explain its value, using emphatic language rarely and only at strength accepted by the discipline and justified by the evidence.
- **P147** — Report credible negative and unexpected results explicitly, distinguishing informative absence from invalid data caused by procedural failure.
- **P150** — Treat every method as good when done well.
- **P168** — Make the evidence chain auditable by identifying collectors and methods, using near-primary sources, citing completely, and accepting responsibility for every selection and transformation between.
- **P172** — Challenge a favored hypothesis with plausibility checks and increasingly severe attempts at disproof, checking early contradictions but abandoning or reformulating the claim when evidence shows it.

## Revision, Editing And Peer Review

Skill: `revision-editing-and-peer-review`

- **P027** — Test the manuscript's message before drafting by explaining it to a non-expert and presenting methods and results to knowledgeable colleagues who can expose omissions, alternatives, anomalies.
- **P054** — Run a final consistency audit across components, ordering, attribution, terminology, names, abbreviations, capitalization, spelling, tense, typography, units, precision, visuals, algorithms.
- **P066** — Beat the curse of knowledge.
- **P083** — Make a review's recommendation explicit and evidence-based, cite specific prior work for novelty objections, identify strengths and actionable fixes, and keep the decision provisional until.
- **P107** — Turn reviews into discrete action points, decide for each whether to change, justify resistance, or ask the editor to resolve a tradeoff, and document every response in a professional, easily scanned.
- **P123** — Treat foundational omissions and pervasive consistency errors as triage signals.
- **P132** — Express criticism specifically, politely, and with examples where useful, and expose substantive concerns to authors rather than hiding new objections in confidential comments.
- **P135** — Read an editorial decision promptly, identify whether a revision path is open, and when it is, revise and resubmit promptly unless the required changes are genuinely impossible or unacceptable.
- **P139** — Edit from the reader's perspective, consolidate duplication, remove obsolete content, and after every move recheck both locations for definitions, context, intelligibility, and argumentative.
- **P145** — After gaining distance from a draft, skim its high-level frame and paraphrase the argument, then ask an outside reader to do the same as a test of organizational intelligibility.
- **P149** — Audit Methods for reproducibility, selective citation, action density, natural prose, reader-oriented sequence, clear references, effective layout, grammatical purpose and consequence, number style.
- **P171** — Calibrate evaluation to learner experience, treat mechanical early use of principles as normal, credit a well-established problem even without a solution, and give information-gathering work.

## Writing Productivity And Habits

Skill: `writing-productivity-and-habits`

- **P024** — Improve by deliberate practice and peer critique.
- **P039** — Start drafting early enough for reconception, follow required field structures when they exist, and treat an early introduction as a replaceable working frame rather than a fixed commitment.
- **P108** — Outline the article's argument before prose generation and keep the finished paper within the target journal's normal length, favoring brevity only while necessary evidence and reasoning remain.
- **P131** — Bring one substantive chapter to a coherent completed state before moving among fragments, and write the final preface and introductory chapter after the book's actual argument has emerged.
- **P143** — Treat the first draft as only a first draft and delete ruthlessly, moving cut passages into a separate document so deletion is painless.
- **P154** — Allocate recurring writing periods inside the normal workweek now rather than waiting for large open blocks, deadline pressure, or the next career milestone, and protect the resulting personal-time.

## Note-Taking And Thinking

Skill: `note-taking-and-thinking`

- **P019** — Compose each permanent note in relation to notes already in the slip-box, reviewing captured notes daily and asking how each contradicts, corrects, supports, adds to, or combines with what you have.
- **P052** — Change behaviour by strategically building new habits rather than by willpower or by breaking old ones.
- **P072** — Do complex thinking in writing rather than in the head.
- **P129** — Deepen familiarity to become more open to genuinely new ideas.
- **P155** — Keep an open mind by seeking dis-confirming facts and asking what a text excludes; the brain defaults to confirmation and the easily-available, so design the workflow to gather by relevance rather.
- **P160** — Connect every new note to related notes as you add it, filing it behind a related note and adding links that can point anywhere.
- **P162** — Aim for a critical mass of high-quality, well-handled notes.
- **P165** — Aim ultimately at transformation, becoming a different thinker by questioning your own routines, captured in a simple core.
- **P166** — Understand that the slip-box trains long-term learning as much as it aids writing.

## Slide And Visual Design

Skill: `slide-and-visual-design`

- **P009** — Do not put your spoken words on slides.
- **P013** — Design slides to reveal, not to carry words.
- **P023** — Maximize the signal-to-noise ratio to reduce cognitive strain.
- **P045** — Use presentation features only when they improve learning or delivery, prioritizing legibility, simplicity, and relevance over available effects.
- **P114** — Secure legal rights to all photos, video, music, and fonts.
- **P141** — Before designing, clarify the audience, context, purpose, and desired action, and be able to state plainly what your point is and why it matters to this audience.
- **P144** — Do not use slides as crutches.

## Presenting And Public Speaking

Skill: `presenting-and-public-speaking`

- **P044** — When detailed theory is inaccessible in a talk, explain its relation to the field, project value, and implications, while discussing relevant limitations and open problems candidly.
- **P057** — For notes, prefer unobtrusive support.
- **P073** — Before building your idea, make clear what it is not, ruling out plausible alternatives so the audience can close in on your meaning; a well-built explanation, made personally relevant and opened.
- **P088** — Convey a talk's hierarchical structure through the linear medium of speech.
- **P089** — Tell a story from the stage with four essentials.
- **P097** — The speaker's number-one mission is to rebuild an idea.
- **P098** — Avoid overrunning: rehearse to confirm the talk fits, watch the clock against a halfway mark, and plan a talk of no more than about 90 percent of your allotted time.
- **P113** — Build understanding as a hierarchy.
- **P128** — When a future vision could unsettle an audience, make clear why it is worth pursuing and frame it around human values rather than clever technology alone.
- **P134** — Design a technical presentation around one clear message and explain algorithms so their operation remains visible; slides and visual cleverness are supporting means, not the purpose.
- **P148** — Protect the whole audience from monopolizing interruptions, and during questions restate each issue, answer everyone briefly and respectfully, admit unknowns, and defer complex discussion when.
- **P161** — A great talk is both scripted and improvisational, like jazz.
- **P163** — You have truly memorized a talk only when you can deliver it while doing another cognitively demanding task and at double speed.
- **P167** — A compelling image, video, or object is a strong opening hook.
- **P170** — Choose format innovations judiciously.
