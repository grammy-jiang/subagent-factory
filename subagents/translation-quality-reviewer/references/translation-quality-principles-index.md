# Translation Quality Principles Index

Package-wide index of every promoted principle, grouped by the skill that owns it. Each entry restates the principle's operative core; the full statement lives in `../principles/principles.yaml`.

## Translation Universals And The Third Code

Skill: `translation-universals-and-the-third-code`

- **P001** — When analysing translated text, probe for the recurring candidate universals.
- **P079** — Operationalise explicitation, simplification, and normalisation as distinct measurable constructs instead of treating them as interchangeable labels.
- **P084** — Keep universals and norms distinct.
- **P114** — Treat distinctive translated-language profiles non-evaluatively unless evidence shows actual incompetence or error.
- **P115** — Scope proposed translation universals as tendencies and test whether language-pair or culture-specific explanations fit better.
- **P121** — To isolate a translation universal, assemble a corpus of texts translated into one target language from a variety of source languages, keep the patterns that recur across source languages and that.
- **P139** — Distinguish the third code from translationese.
- **P143** — Generalise simplification hypotheses only after testing across genres, language pairs, and interpreted texts rather than relying on early or single-design evidence.

## Corpus Design And Methodology

Skill: `corpus-design-and-methodology`

- **P003** — Choose the corpus type by the question.
- **P012** — Treat the primary purpose of corpus work in translation studies as explaining what translation is.
- **P050** — Check comparability, metadata, copyright clearance, and alignment requirements before attributing corpus differences to translation status or using parallel-corpus evidence.
- **P068** — Use carefully designed, contextualized parallel and comparable corpora to generalize and verify equivalence claims beyond individual cases.
- **P077** — Use explicitly designed machine-readable corpora together with suitable software tools; corpus evidence is only useful when it can be searched, counted, displayed, and analysed.
- **P078** — Match the corpus measure to the research question and control for its limitations before comparing texts.
- **P097** — Use genre-level corpus comparison to relate an individual text pair to norms and options in that genre and language pair.
- **P107** — Use corpora as one tool within a broader evidence set that also includes introspection, observation, textual analysis, and ethnographic analysis.
- **P108** — Combine qualitative and quantitative inquiry by using corpus statistics as prompts for recontextualized interpretation rather than as final answers.
- **P112** — Frame corpus-based translation studies around explicit hypotheses, varied data, descriptive categories, and a methodology that can support both inductive and deductive questions.
- **P118** — Use corpus metadata to separate regional variation, dialect, source-language interference, translator-mother-tongue effects, institutional profile, team structure, target language, and source-text.
- **P122** — Ground corpus-based translation and interpreting advice in empirical corpus evidence, using quantitative patterns together with qualitative interpretation rather than treating either as sufficient.
- **P135** — Normalize corpus frequencies when subcorpora differ in size, and do not infer translation influence from frequency alone when item functions differ.
- **P140** — Use large computerised corpora to open questions that small-scale studies cannot settle.
- **P141** — Treat corpus research as an empirical check on intuition; prefer natural-discourse evidence when claims about language patterning are disputed or assumed.

## Overt/Covert Translation And Functional Equivalence

Skill: `overt-covert-translation-and-equivalence`

- **P005** — For overt translation, aim at second-level access to the source function, preserve the source as intact as possible, and balance necessary explanation against replacement of source-culture markedness.
- **P006** — Build the quality comparison from a detailed source-text profile that specifies the function to be sought in the target text.
- **P007** — For covert translation, account for target pragmatics through cultural filters supported by multi-method research on register and genre variation across language pairs.
- **P008** — When evaluating covert translation, check whether titles, advertising, signs, or other target texts reveal shifts between explicit content, interpersonal address, directness, and implicitness.
- **P011** — Classify unjustified cultural-filter changes as covert versions, and classify added special-audience or special-purpose renderings as overt versions rather than ordinary translations.
- **P030** — Treat equivalence as approximate value across differences, assessed at text level and prioritized case by case across denotative, connotative, pragmatic, text-normative, and formal-aesthetic demands.
- **P031** — Define text function as use in a particular situational and cultural context, not as a direct projection from broad language-function typologies.
- **P038** — Assess meaning preservation across semantic, pragmatic, and textual dimensions rather than reducing equivalence to referential content.
- **P059** — Require any purpose-oriented TQA method to define textual function explicitly, identify source-text type, compare translation methods or shifts, and evaluate adequacy against text-specific features.
- **P062** — Decide translation type from source status, use, reception history, purpose, and brief, not from source-text features alone.
- **P063** — Analyze translation as frame and discourse-world movement, then choose criticism criteria according to whether the sought function is overt or covert.
- **P064** — For covert translation, permit language/text and register manipulation only when it preserves target-frame genre and individual textual function.
- **P093** — Classify a case as covert translation when source and target addressees have comparable contemporary needs and the target can function as an original in its own culture.
- **P106** — Revise overt/covert expectations when current corpus evidence shows reduced cultural filtering, dominant English source-norm takeover, or emerging overt-like hybrid translation types.
- **P127** — Classify mismatches as covert functional errors when dimensional profile differences alter function despite acceptable grammar or denotation.
- **P131** — Keep source-target comparison in the assessment even when target-audience appropriateness or translation purpose influences whether the product is a translation or version.
- **P133** — Treat functional equivalence as a matter of intercultural understanding.
- **P136** — Account for different cognitive demands in overt and covert translation.
- **P138** — Use the overt/covert cline and translation/version distinction to state which linkage and textual operation are being evaluated.

## Register: Field, Tenor And Mode Analysis

Skill: `register-field-tenor-mode-analysis`

- **P010** — In Tenor analysis of mission statements, inspect missionizing stance, optimism, inclusive pronouns, persuasive positivity, grammatical metaphor, omitted agents, and accessible versus specialist.
- **P013** — Break context into manageable situational dimensions and inspect medium, participation, social role, and social attitude through linguistic evidence.
- **P040** — Use linguistic cues such as spoken-mode absence, passivization, pronouns, requests, modality, and questions to diagnose medium, participation, and social-role effects.
- **P041** — Inspect pronouns, agency, passives, non-human subjects, syntactic markedness, information order, and text sequencing as evidence of interpersonal strategy.
- **P042** — Build register analysis from Field, Tenor, and Mode.
- **P060** — Treat text function as realized through situational dimensions and their syntactic, lexical, and textual correlates.
- **P069** — Use the updated Field/Tenor/Mode split to reduce category overlap.
- **P070** — Assess mission statements as both exposition and exhortation, with special attention to corporate values and indirect requests for employee alignment.
- **P071** — In Mode analysis of mission statements, distinguish oral-rhetorical writtenness from plain information delivery and evaluate repetition, parallelism, rhetorical macrostructure, and summative closure.
- **P072** — When evaluating translated mission statements, check for shifts in positivity, emotional engagement, indirectness, rhetorical cohesion, perlocutionary effect, and genre interpretation.
- **P073** — Flag lexical and process shifts that weaken persuasive function, including negative shadow meanings, false friends, added material-action focus, specialist terminology, and loss of colloquial.
- **P074** — Flag Tenor shifts that turn positive missionizing into conditions, effort, discipline, direct request, exhortation, or pressure on addressee behaviour.
- **P075** — Keep the revised model source-target comparative, with Participation under Tenor, process types under Field, textual aspects under Mode, connectivity covering coherence and cohesion, and corpus.
- **P116** — When adapting register-analysis tools to translation, analyse co-occurring feature clusters against an appropriate baseline rather than isolated feature counts.
- **P129** — Use formal-style and province markers such as complex noun phrases, complete clauses, formal lexis, technical terminology, explicit alternatives, dating, figurative absence, and cohesion to.
- **P130** — Synthesize function by stating how marked dimensions contribute to ideational and interpersonal components, recognizing that one device may serve both.

## Error Analysis And Evaluation Discipline

Skill: `error-analysis-and-evaluation-discipline`

- **P002** — Do not use translationese indicators as a direct proxy for translation quality; quality judgments need separate evidence for meaning preservation and pragmatic acceptability.
- **P021** — Use micro-, macro-, and superstructural levels to judge error seriousness only as part of a specified, text-context-sensitive procedure.
- **P035** — Preserve the strength of the evidence.
- **P036** — Reject global impressionistic or contradictory assessment criteria unless they are converted into explicit, operationally checkable textual and contextual tests.
- **P037** — Do not accept target-only, reception-only, or response-only evaluations as sufficient when they cannot explain the source-translation relation or distinguish translation from adaptation.
- **P048** — Separate linguistic analysis from social judgement.
- **P049** — Account for social, political, ethical, publishing, marketing, reader, and purpose factors without letting them displace linguistic-textual analysis.
- **P061** — Allow analyst judgement only as argued, evidence-constrained hypotheses, acknowledging that equivalence and TQA retain non-absolute and subjective elements.
- **P086** — Treat usage norms, reader-response data, market response, and judge samples as insufficient unless their empirical basis and operational procedure are explicit.
- **P087** — Reject analytic category sets that overlap, lack theoretical grounding, obscure text-context relations, or make target-language evidence inaccessible to evaluators.
- **P090** — Classify overt errors separately as denotative omissions, additions, substitutions, wrong selections or combinations, ungrammaticality, and questionable acceptability.
- **P091** — State the assumptions and exceptions behind dimensional error judgements, including cultural comparability, intertranslatability, and whether the target has an added special function.
- **P094** — Weight errors by source-profile priorities, evaluation objective, and functional component, treating denotative mismatches as especially serious when ideational function is central.
- **P095** — Measure revised-model quality by analogous source and target profile/function analysis while distinguishing dimensional from non-dimensional mismatches.
- **P109** — Let a controlled TQA model supply qualitative categories before quantification, especially to reduce stereotype-producing corpus interpretations.
- **P125** — Separate obligatory system-driven shifts from optional translator choices before assigning evaluative responsibility for a translation difference.
- **P128** — Weight errors by their effect on the individual text’s ideational and interpersonal functional match rather than by a universal error hierarchy.

## Cognition, Pragmatics And Contrastive Evidence

Skill: `cognition-pragmatics-and-contrastive-evidence`

- **P004** — Recognize that expert translators may use either conceptual processing or more direct transcoding, so process assumptions should be evidence-based rather than presumed.
- **P009** — Use cognitive translation research to inform TQA only where it clarifies comprehension, transfer, problem solving, decision making, reconstitution, and bilingual pragmatics.
- **P015** — Treat verbal reports, behavioural process measures, and neuroimaging as limited evidence that cannot by itself establish underlying cognition or translation quality.
- **P024** — Treat German-English pragmatic differences such as directness, content orientation, self-reference, routine use, and explicit justification as corpus- and study-bound tendencies rather than fixed.
- **P088** — Analyze translations as discourse utterances whose illocutionary force depends on context, not as isolated sentence correspondences.
- **P099** — Triangulate contrastive pragmatic evidence using elicited tasks, retrospective data, meta-pragmatic judgements, naturalistic data, and varied authority/familiarity relations.
- **P124** — Integrate social-contextual and cognitive perspectives as complements, while keeping the assessment anchored in the textual evidence available for the source and target texts.
- **P132** — Analyze discourse openings, closings, supportive strategies, gambits, requests, complaints, directness, politeness, roles, rights, and obligations when diagnosing pragmatic differences.

## Cultural Filtering, Ideology And Globalization

Skill: `cultural-filtering-ideology-and-globalization`

- **P022** — Treat preservation of factual content as insufficient when target choices weaken addressee involvement, flattery, reassurance, indirectness, responsibility management, hedging, or interpersonal force.
- **P026** — Evaluate ELF communication by mutual intelligibility, efficiency, and multilingual multicompetence rather than by native-speaker correctness alone.
- **P032** — Keep cultural-filter judgements current with language-pair developments because empirical evidence reduces arbitrariness but communicative norms continue to change.
- **P045** — Account for translation’s double transmission and rupture by analyzing how texts are recontextualized across time, space, indexical orders, and language-cultural barriers.
- **P046** — Do not treat ELF as a threat that makes translation obsolete; check the complementary roles of ELF, native languages, translation, and localization in the domain.
- **P047** — Avoid permanent cultural-filter generalizations; reassess each case against current communicative norms, globalization, internal diversification, and corpus evidence.
- **P066** — Reduce stereotyping risk by testing contrastive findings against larger samples, bicultural expert interpretation, and multicultural comparison.
- **P067** — Under globalization, test whether transported forms retain source function or acquire new local value through orders of indexicality and local uptake.
- **P100** — Frame cultural explanation around who makes culture relevant to whom, for what purpose, where, and in which context.
- **P101** — Analyze globalized discourse across lexical, semantic, pragmatic-discourse, socio-semiotic, micro-process, and macro-process levels.
- **P102** — Evaluate borrowings and English imports by their contextual function, including whether they threaten local resources, facilitate communication, develop new functions, or signal identity.
- **P103** — Treat globalized text production as negotiation and accommodation within hybrid cultures rather than as one-way transfer.
- **P105** — Watch for under-researched English influence at syntactic, pragmatic, discourse, and textualization levels because these changes can be harder to detect than lexical borrowing.
- **P123** — Ground socio-political, ethical, or power-focused critique in detailed linguistic analysis of forms and functions before drawing macro-level conclusions.
- **P137** — Treat cultural filtering as quality-relevant when target-culture directness, explicitness, and content orientation submerge rhetorical strategies central to source function.
- **P145** — Include socio-cultural context in causal explanations of translational patterns instead of relying on linguistic counts alone.

## Descriptive Studies And Translational Norms

Skill: `descriptive-studies-and-translational-norms`

- **P016** — Explain translated-text features through target-culture norms, conventions, and historical systems rather than reducing them to source-text derivation.
- **P018** — Treat translated text as an object of study in its own right.
- **P025** — Treat language, knowledge, norms, culture, translator cognition, and social constraints as interrelated, situated phenomena when explaining translation choices.
- **P034** — Adopt a target-orientation.
- **P053** — Ground translation theory empirically.
- **P054** — Require a descriptive branch with an explicit, reproducible methodology.
- **P055** — Before selecting features or evidence, classify the study target as a general universal, a mediator-specific style, or a source-text response, then choose measurements that match that target.
- **P076** — Investigate operational norms through the corpus at two levels.
- **P080** — Attribute a pattern to an individual translator only after testing author style, source-language preferences, and sociolect norms as alternative explanations.
- **P083** — Identify translational norms descriptively and only from a corpus.
- **P085** — Keep translation’s double orientation visible by checking both fidelity to the prior source event and fit with target-language genre and reception expectations.
- **P111** — Model translation as a mediated communicative event.
- **P117** — Account for target-system norms and source-text status when interpreting normalisation.
- **P142** — Interpret additions and omissions as functional translation strategies only after checking clarity, coherence, aesthetic intent, complexity, and whether omitted meaning is vital.

## Russian Corpus And Interpreting Research

Skill: `russian-corpus-and-interpreting-research`

- **P019** — For Russian interpreting research, treat corpus availability as a design constraint.
- **P020** — Prioritize corpus construction and study designs for underexplored Russian varieties, especially constrained communication, amateur translation, post-edited translation, second-language translation.
- **P029** — When surveying Russian translation and interpreting corpora, include the Russian research tradition rather than assuming the field is represented only by better-known English, French, German, or.
- **P051** — When recommending Russian interpreting data sources, describe UN Web TV, SIREN, and COINCOUT with their acquisition, transcription, size, domain, and access limitations.
- **P052** — Recommend supervised or unsupervised machine learning for Russian translation/interpreting only when the corpus is large, feature-rich, and metadata-suitable, and frame the result as pattern.
- **P056** — For complex variation in translation or interpreting, discourage explanation from isolated frequencies or concordances and recommend multifactorial modelling when multiple explanatory variables are.
- **P057** — In mode or producer comparisons, operationalize linguistic markers clearly and pair descriptive indicators with significance testing and effect sizes before interpreting group differences.
- **P058** — For Polish-Russian or Russian-Polish multifactorial studies, require metadata-rich corpora that record factors such as translation direction, delivery mode, and text variety before making explanatory.
- **P146** — When evaluating universals or strategies, check patterns across lexical, syntactic, and discourse levels and avoid strong Russian interpreting generalizations until independent corpora replicate them.
- **P147** — Encourage Russian translationese studies to move beyond newspapers by using larger corpora and feature sets spanning lexical, morphological, syntactic, and genre-sensitive evidence.
- **P148** — For advanced computational or process-oriented Russian corpus research, encourage interdisciplinary collaboration across linguistic, literary, computational, NLP, and psychological expertise.

## Genre, Children's Literature And Accessibility

Skill: `genre-childrens-literature-and-accessibility`

- **P014** — Discover genre from everyday lingua-cultural practice, expect translation to remain in the source genre, and route null target-genre realizations to version or non-verbal alternatives when covert.
- **P023** — Flag translations that normalize imaginative agency, over-simplify capable-reader syntax, or alter address and action descriptions in ways that infantilize characters or impose hierarchy.
- **P028** — Evaluate claims about stage suitability, speakability, or accessibility by comparing translated texts with corpus features that match the claimed function.
- **P043** — For children’s picture books, analyze whether lexis, syntax, cohesion, iconic linkage, sound patterning, and repetition support young listeners’ comprehension, drama, and involvement.
- **P044** — In children’s books, treat respect for readers and characters, naming, titles, conversational rituals, reassurance, and gentle socialization as core Tenor and genre-function evidence.
- **P065** — Evaluate title, ending, added material, and framing changes as functional shifts when they move a children’s text away from its source atmosphere or role relationships.
- **P092** — Treat poetic-aesthetic sources and foregrounding according to whether form is detachable from meaning and whether foregrounding serves expression itself or communicative effect.
- **P096** — Use Genre to connect individual texts to macro-contextual text classes and combine it with language/text and register to characterize textual function.
- **P098** — Do not assume children require domestication; consider overt access to foreignness or the original voice when covert adaptation substantially changes source function.
- **P110** — Do not treat local cohesion as sufficient when translation loses source macrostructural parallelism, rhetorical guidance, oral-rhetorical effect, or summative closure.
- **P126** — Use argumentation structure as a useful comparison lens only when it fits the source genre, and do not let it displace other linguistic or micro-textual features.
- **P144** — Test accessibility or audience-fit claims with quantifiable textual features and reformulate specialised content when the target audience requires it.

## Chinese Prose And Europeanization

Skill: `chinese-prose-and-europeanization`

- **P119** — For ordinary Chinese prose, preserve concision, flexible syntax, and rhythmic force; resist Europeanized wording when it makes the sentence needlessly complex or stiff.
- **P120** — Let coordinate Chinese words or phrases stand by juxtaposition when natural, and choose connectors that match progression, contrast, or adjustment instead of defaulting to English-like and-linking.
- **P149** — Use established Chinese idioms as live resources, not as decorative substitutes for thought, and do not flatten them into wordy paraphrase when the idiom fits.
- **P150** — Prefer concrete subjects, people, and event clauses over abstract nominalized subjects when revising Chinese sentences influenced by English syntax.

## Applied Corpus Tools And Textual Devices

Skill: `applied-corpus-tools-and-textual-devices`

- **P017** — Analyse simplification and conventionalisation through observable shifts such as title simplification, clearer syntax, shorter units, regularised punctuation, reduced repetition, and conventional.
- **P027** — Use target-reader need, lexical availability, and meaning criticality to choose among superordinates, approximation, common synonyms, circumlocution, paraphrase, glossing, or loan-word retention.
- **P033** — Use corpus comparison to examine addressee involvement, perspective, subjectivity, information organization, interactionality, and source-language shining-through.
- **P039** — Include textual devices in the analysis by tracking theme dynamics, clausal linkage, iconic linkage, deictic dependence, and overall text organization.
- **P081** — Use corpus-based tools in Bible translation to describe existing translations, explain their systemic conditions, and support quality, accessibility, and skopos-specific decisions.
- **P082** — Use keyword, concordance, and collocation analysis to investigate cultural or ideological meanings in translated texts.
- **P089** — Analyze style and province as functional evidence by checking shared background, implicitness, formality, institutional convention, domain activity, topic, and production details.
- **P104** — Allow non-mother-tongue translation only with appropriate competence and controls such as qualified native-language revision or stylistic advice when target-native expectations matter.
- **P113** — Connect fine-grained textual measurements with cultural, ideological, pedagogical, and technological context when interpreting corpus findings.
- **P134** — Set translation quality requirements from the commission purpose and risk level, distinguishing basic understanding or information uses from publication, image, legal, or high-stakes uses.
