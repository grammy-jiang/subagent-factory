# Literal–Free Strategy History And Retranslation

## Purpose

This skill reviews a translation against the long strategy debate — Dryden's metaphrase/paraphrase/imitation triad, sense-for-sense versus word-for-word, and Schleiermacher's move-the-reader-or-the-author choice — and against retranslation and 'afterlife' dynamics. It checks verse prosody, historical register and archaism, scholarly apparatus, and the reading of a translation against its predecessors.

## When to use

- A rendering needs placing on the metaphrase–paraphrase–imitation scale or the literal–free axis.
- A verse translation must map both prosodic systems rather than transfer a form wholesale.
- Historical distance, archaism, or a scholarly apparatus governs the target register.
- A retranslation is being read in relation to its predecessors and a work's afterlife.

## Procedure

1. Read a translation in relation to the rival versions it reacts to, not in isolation (P030).
2. Apply Borges's evaluative method (P031).
3. Consider experimental and creative strategies for literary and philosophical texts, including Pound's energizing, archaizing, foreignizing style and use of a deliberately non-contemporary idiom to bring out the source's difference at (P034).
4. Default to sense-for-sense translation rather than slavish word-for-word rendering (P036).
5. Prepare a verse translation by first mapping both languages' prosodic systems (P045).
6. Reckon with untranslatability rather than defensively dismiss it (P048).
7. Value a translation for its creative transformation and not fidelity alone (P071).
8. Frame translating, with Levy, both teleologically as communication and pragmatically as a decision process (P077).
9. When no other guidance is given, follow the classic ranked prescriptions for good translation (P142).
10. Recognize that any de-modernizing or anti-anachronism method can itself smuggle in a new anachronism (P149).
11. Use Dryden's triad as a diagnostic scale, placing a rendering on the metaphrase (P157).
12. When you cannot satisfy every criterion, resolve trade-offs by a fixed priority (P158).
13. Expect translations to date while originals endure and invite re-translation (P171).
14. Recognize that the translator's chief obstacle is often not the source language's difficulty but the dead, conventional register of his own language, and that there is no single target language but a series of period- and (P172).
15. Choose the target register by matching proportionate historical distance rather than absolute date (P173).
16. For scholarly translation, prescribe an 'ugly', unliterary but very clear apparatus that prioritizes substance over beauty with copious footnotes, and render the work's full apparatus (P174).
17. Acquire exact information about the source's period, customs, and realia and trace the unacknowledged intertextual borrowings woven into the text (P177).

## Inputs

- The translation, its source's period and form, and any prior versions it reacts to.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to read a translation in relation to the rival versions it reacts to, not in isolation (P030).
- The analysis fails to apply Borges's evaluative method (P031).
- The analysis fails to consider experimental and creative strategies for literary and philosophical texts, including Pound's energizing, archaizing, foreignizing style and use of a (P034).
- The analysis fails to default to sense-for-sense translation rather than slavish word-for-word rendering (P036).
- The analysis fails to prepare a verse translation by first mapping both languages' prosodic systems (P045).
- The analysis fails to reckon with untranslatability rather than defensively dismiss it (P048).
- The analysis fails to value a translation for its creative transformation and not fidelity alone (P071).
