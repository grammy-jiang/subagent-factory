# Hermeneutics And The Limits Of Translatability

## Purpose

This skill reviews the interpretive and philosophical grounding of a translation: Steiner's fourfold hermeneutic motion (trust, aggression, incorporation, restitution), the indeterminacy of translation and inscrutability of reference (Quine), relevance-theoretic interpretive resemblance (Gutt), and untranslatability. It holds that there is no perfect translation and treats each choice as a double interpretation.

## When to use

- A translation's interpretive stance — what it trusts, takes, and must restitute — is under review.
- Claims of full determinacy or perfect equivalence need tempering with indeterminacy and the inscrutability of reference.
- An 'unnatural' rendering is imposing gratuitous processing effort against interpretive resemblance.
- Untranslatability or the impossibility of a definitive translation is being denied or over-claimed.

## Procedure

1. Treat translation as at once interpretation and creation, involving a choice among the source's possible interpretations and a choice among the target's possible expressions of that meaning, and expect the translator in practice to (P003).
2. Model translation as Steiner's fourfold hermeneutic motion (P005).
3. Let the principle of relevance constrain both what a translation conveys (P032).
4. Understand that beyond observation sentences and truth functions translation runs on analytical hypotheses (P056).
5. Read each language as a different equation of statements and silences whose gaps constrain thought (P095).
6. Understand the literalist position and its costs (P096).
7. Take Quine's radical translation (P097).
8. Hold the inscrutability of reference in view (P098).
9. Treat stimulus meaning as an approximation to meaning only for observation sentences (P099).
10. Adopt the 'good utopian' stance toward an impossible task (P117).
11. Accept that a good translation faces a contradictory demand and is therefore always already a double interpretation (P123).
12. Complete the hermeneutic motion with restitution, its ethical crux (P130).
13. Note that merely reversing the hierarchy (P132).
14. Model translation, with Gutt, as interlingual interpretive use (P134).
15. Diagnose most 'unnaturalness' as gratuitous processing effort (P135).
16. Locate the real barriers to literal translation as specific and local rather than deep (P137).
17. Accept that there is no perfect translation, not because a definite set of desiderata cannot all be met but because there is no definite set of desiderata (P138).
18. Treat translation as an interpretive cognitive process (P144).
19. Rely on the truth-functional core of logic (P150).

## Inputs

- The translation and the interpretive or philosophical claim (determinacy, equivalence, translatability) at stake.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to treat translation as at once interpretation and creation, involving a choice among the source's possible interpretations and a choice among the target's possible (P003).
- The analysis fails to model translation as Steiner's fourfold hermeneutic motion (P005).
- The analysis fails to let the principle of relevance constrain both what a translation conveys (P032).
- The analysis fails to understand that beyond observation sentences and truth functions translation runs on analytical hypotheses (P056).
- The analysis fails to read each language as a different equation of statements and silences whose gaps constrain thought (P095).
- The analysis fails to understand the literalist position and its costs (P096).
- The analysis fails to take Quine's radical translation (P097).
