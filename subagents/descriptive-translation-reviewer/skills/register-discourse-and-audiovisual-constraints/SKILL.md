# Register, Discourse And Audiovisual Constraints

## Purpose

This skill reviews translation at the level of register and discourse using the Hallidayan model — field, tenor, mode — and checks cohesion, pragmatic linkage, transitivity and modality shifts, and politeness/face. It gives special attention to audiovisual and subtitling constraints, where space, legibility, and timing force shortening that can re-code interpersonal meaning, not merely omit it.

## When to use

- A translation's register profile (field/tenor/mode), genre, and function need comparing against the source's for a quality judgement.
- Cohesion, an implicit pragmatic link, or a transitivity/modality shift changes meaning.
- A face-threatening act or its irony is being shifted across cultures.
- A subtitle or dubbed line is constrained by on-screen space, timing, and legibility.

## Procedure

1. Analyse an audiovisual text across its signifying codes (P019).
2. Decide whether a translation is overt (P021).
3. Watch that subtitling's habitual preference for brevity does not merely omit politeness markers but can actively re-code the interpersonal dynamics (P052).
4. Use politeness theory to pinpoint how a translation shifts a face-threat and its irony (P053).
5. Use the Hallidayan model to relate lexicogrammatical choices to a text's function in its sociocultural framework, analysing register as field, tenor, and mode mapped to the ideational, interpersonal, and textual metafunctions, and (P064).
6. Assess translation quality by comparing the source and target register profiles (P065).
7. Treat cohesion (Halliday and Hasan's five types of reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, and lexical cohesion) as language-specific, rebuilding the web of cohesive ties in the target rather than copying it and keeping the (P066).
8. Manage pragmatic meaning across cultures by making an implicit link explicit for readers who lack the background (P067).
9. Watch how discourse-level choices shift meaning (P068).
10. Subtitle within the hard space and time limits of about two lines of 38 Roman or 13 to 15 Chinese or Japanese characters and around six seconds, respecting camera cuts and matching duration to the dialogue rhythm, following the (P069).
11. Weight the seriousness of a face-threatening act as a cultural variable set by the social distance and relative power of the speakers (P085).
12. Use discourse and register analysis as a flexible toolkit rather than a rigid one-to-one model, and heed its cautions (P090).
13. Treat subtitling as an overt, vulnerable translation constrained by on-screen space, legibility, and timing that force shortening, and expect it to normalize informal sociolect by increasing cohesion and using conventional grammar and (P091).

## Inputs

- The source and target texts (or AV material) and the register, discourse, or subtitling constraints in play.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
