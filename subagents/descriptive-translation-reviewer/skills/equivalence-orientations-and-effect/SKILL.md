# Equivalence Orientations And Effect

## Purpose

This skill reviews how a translation positions itself on the formal-versus-dynamic equivalence axis and whether the chosen orientation fits the purpose. It checks equivalent-effect claims for the illusion that arises when a text is out of the receptor's space and time, and holds a rendering to the synthesized requirements of sense, spirit, natural form, and equivalent response.

## When to use

- A translation claims formal (source-form) or dynamic (closest-natural) equivalence and the fit of that orientation to the brief needs checking.
- An 'equivalent effect' or 'same response' claim is being made and may be illusory.
- Koller's denotative/connotative/text-normative/pragmatic/formal relations are being chosen among, or naturalness is being assessed.
- A legal or equally-authentic multilingual text needs a technique close to formal equivalence with little room for adjustment.

## Procedure

1. Because equivalent effect can be illusory when a text is out of target space and time, follow Newmark in choosing method by text type (P059).
2. Govern translation choices by Nida's three factors (P074).
3. Describe a natural translation more by what it avoids than by what it states (P076).
4. Resolve the categories the source and target languages obligatorily force (P104).
5. Choose the equivalence orientation by purpose (P105).
6. Escalate through Koller's five equivalence relations by the needs of the communicative situation, trying denotative (P106).
7. Pitch equivalence at the level of the overall impression the message makes, not element-by-element identity (P118).
8. Understand formal equivalence (P124).
9. Hold a good translation to the synthesized requirements (P125).
10. Test a rendering's naturalness against three areas at once (P126).
11. Use Nida's analytical procedure and semantic tools where helpful (P143).
12. Treat meaning as contextual and functional (P159).
13. Check a translation against Nida's four requirements (P160).
14. Keep a literal word-for-word rendering wherever it secures equivalent effect (P161).
15. For legal documents and equally valid multilingual treaties use a technique close to formal equivalence with little room for adjustment, yet ensure each version still produces the same response (P162).
16. Understand dynamic equivalence (P179).
17. Note that the dominant naturalness ideal Nida reports (P180).

## Inputs

- The translation and its stated equivalence orientation, purpose, and target audience.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
