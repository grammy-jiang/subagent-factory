# Descriptive Method And Translational Norms

## Purpose

This skill reviews whether a translation is being described and judged with an explicit, systematic, target-oriented descriptive method rather than by ad-hoc prescription. It checks that translational norms are reconstructed from patterned behaviour at the receiving end — not read off directly — and that findings are contextualized by sector, subsystem position, and historical moment, with norm strength treated as graded and mobile.

## When to use

- A translation or corpus of translations is being analysed and the reviewer wants the method checked for being explicit, systematic, and intersubjectively testable.
- A claim about a 'norm' is being made and needs checking against whether it was reconstructed from recurrent regularities or merely asserted.
- Preliminary, initial, and operational norms are being conflated, or the adequacy–acceptability orientation of a translation needs situating.
- Toury's probabilistic laws (growing standardization, interference) are being invoked to explain regularities and the reasoning needs checking.

## Procedure

1. Expect translated literature to assume a central, innovating position under three conditions (P002).
2. Use Toury's structure of translational norms (P010).
3. Reconstruct norms rather than read them off (P011).
4. Preserve the adequacy-acceptability axis by distinguishing source-norm orientation, target-norm orientation, and mixed compromises (P023).
5. Before using extratextual norm statements systematically, explicate them precisely, contextualize their cultural function, compare them with one another, and test them against behavior (P035).
6. Do descriptive translation studies with an explicit, systematic method so findings are intersubjectively testable, comparable, and replicable, treating translations first as facts of the target culture whose position determines (P039).
7. Study translational norms with their two complicating features in mind (P046).
8. Use Chesterman's S-universals (P054).
9. Separate preliminary norms from operational norms before testing how selection, directness, and textual decision patterns relate (P057).
10. Reconstruct translational norms from patterned behavior at the receiving end, using recurrent regularities in comparable products or practices as primary evidence (P101).
11. Treat norms as graded and historically mobile constraints whose force may shift, rather than as fixed all-or-nothing rules (P102).
12. Contextualize norm findings by social sector, subsystem position, and historical moment, and order features by representativeness instead of treating all observations as equal (P103).
13. Expect Toury's probabilistic laws when reviewing translations (P110).
14. Recognize that coupled-pair mapping is ad hoc, partial, and not fully objective or replicable, so support it with an explicit repertory-of-features checklist, and do not let a target-text-only stance overlook ideological and political (P146).
15. Explain shifts as norm-governed only when their realization is patterned (P151).
16. Map operational norms across textual matrix, distribution, segmentation, and target textual-linguistic selection instead of reducing them to wording choices alone (P152).
17. Move from isolated norm observations to integrated normative structures by relating findings across multiple problem areas (P153).
18. Represent norm strength through distributions of group approval, distinguishing basic norms, tendencies, tolerated behavior, and diagnostically symptomatic devices (P154).
19. Use frequency as evidence for norm status only with subgroup context and methodological caution (P155).
20. Use learned intuition pragmatically for corpus selection and hypothesis formation while actively seeking stronger systematic and statistical methods (P156).
21. Apply Chesterman's norms alongside Toury's (P166).

## Inputs

- The translation(s) or translation-studies analysis under review and its declared method, corpus, and norm claims.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to expect translated literature to assume a central, innovating position under three conditions (P002).
- The analysis fails to use Toury's structure of translational norms (P010).
- The analysis fails to reconstruct norms rather than read them off (P011).
- The analysis fails to preserve the adequacy-acceptability axis by distinguishing source-norm orientation, target-norm orientation, and mixed compromises (P023).
- The analysis fails to before using extratextual norm statements systematically, explicate them precisely, contextualize their cultural function, compare them with one another, and test them (P035).
- The analysis fails to do descriptive translation studies with an explicit, systematic method so findings are intersubjectively testable, comparable, and replicable, treating translations (P039).
- The analysis fails to study translational norms with their two complicating features in mind (P046).
