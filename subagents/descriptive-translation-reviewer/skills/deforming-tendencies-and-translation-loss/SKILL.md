# Deforming Tendencies And Translation Loss

## Purpose

This skill reviews a literary translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis for Berman's negative analytic of deforming tendencies and for the translator's characteristic betrayal, normalization, which flattens deliberate authorial deviation into standard expression. It catches subtractive, paratextual, and typographic losses and the over-clarification that destroys an indirect or figurative meaning. It is a review-only skill: it critiques an existing translation or analysis and does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## When to use

- A literary translation may be flattening the author's deliberate deviations from ordinary usage.
- Paratextual or typographic features (italics, layout, translator's notes) that carried meaning have been dropped or altered.
- A culture-specific detail has been over-clarified or spelled out, and the figurative charge it carried may be lost.
- A translation reads noticeably longer, more polished, or more "elegant" than its source.
- A reviewer must check whether every value of the source was consciously weighed rather than assumed preserved.

## Procedure

1. Screen the translation for Berman's twelve deforming tendencies — rationalization, clarification, expansion, ennoblement, qualitative and quantitative impoverishment, destruction of rhythms, of underlying networks of signification, of linguistic patternings, and of vernacular networks or their exoticization, destruction of expressions and idioms, and effacement of the superimposition of languages — and confirm the translator refused to replace a source idiom with a target equivalent or hunt for a target vernacular for a source vernacular, favouring instead a positive labour on the letter (P014).
2. Check the translation for the subtractive changes that flatten a carefully constructed source text: paratextual and typographic losses, mishandling of the source's own foreign or translated words, morphological simplification that strips a term of its systemic value, semantic-neighbour substitution when a more literal alternative exists, phrase-level changes that set aside a concept or reverse an argument, assimilation to target discourse norms that gives a theoretical text a falsely immediate tenor, and the dropping of repetition, parallelism, or a key term's polysemy that carries argumentative structure (P025).
3. Beware that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail to help the target reader can destroy the indirect, figurative meaning it carries across the text, and confirm that any cohesion shift the translator made was an optional stylistic choice rather than one dictated by the two grammars before treating it as evidence of a translation trend (P026).
4. Catch expansion and the two poles of stylistic distortion and impoverishment — empty added length that muffles the work's voice and slackens its rhythm, ennoblement's false elegance and its popularizing opposite that together annihilate the original's authentic orality, qualitative impoverishment that strips a term of its sonorous or iconic richness, and quantitative impoverishment that flattens a proliferation of unfixed signifiers into fewer signifiers (P081).
5. Catch the effacement of vernacular, idiom, and superimposed languages, and prefer a literal translation attached to the letter of the work over the Platonic figure that separates spirit from letter and embellishes toward a text clearer and purer than the original (P082).
6. Judge whether the translator delimited the central allusions a reader needs to follow the plot or argument, and check that an ambiguous or indirect form was not flattened into a single reading that forecloses the source's interpretive options (P083).
7. Confirm the translator consciously chose which value to preserve and which to sacrifice — since no translation can keep every value of a work at once — and assess even a deliberately heterogeneous, skewing diction as a strategy in its own right rather than an unexamined default (P114).
8. Guard against normalization, the translator's characteristic betrayal that flattens the author's deliberate deviations from ordinary usage into standard expression, while accepting that translation is a structurally utopian, essentially impossible task whose impossibility is not a fault to lay on the individual translator (P115).
9. Emit the findings from this review, ordering them from the most damaging deformation to the least, in the Output format below.

## Inputs

- The literary source and its translation, with attention to the author's marked or deviant usage.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step; order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The review passes over a translation that quietly rationalizes syntax, ennobles diction, or otherwise commits one of Berman's twelve deforming tendencies without naming it (P014).
- A paratextual or typographic loss — dropped italics, an inserted translator's note, a term flattened to its bare gloss — goes unremarked even though it strips the source of systemic value (P025).
- A culture-specific detail has been spelled out for the target reader in a way that erases the figurative or indirect meaning it carried across the text, and the review does not flag it (P026).
- The translation reads noticeably longer or more "elegant" than the source, and the review treats the added length or polish as a neutral stylistic choice rather than expansion or ennoblement (P081).
- A source vernacular, idiom, or superimposed language has been smoothed into one register or swapped for a target "equivalent", and the review accepts it as an unavoidable simplification (P082).
- An ambiguous or allusion-laden passage has been resolved to a single reading or left unglossed with no record of why the translator made that trade-off (P083).
- The review commends a translation for "preserving everything" without asking which value was sacrificed to protect which other value (P114).
- The review flags a deviation from ordinary usage as an error to fix rather than checking whether it is the author's deliberate style being normalized away (P115).
