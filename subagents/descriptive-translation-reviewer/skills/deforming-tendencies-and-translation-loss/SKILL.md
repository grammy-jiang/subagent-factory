# Deforming Tendencies And Translation Loss

## Purpose

This skill screens a literary translation for Berman's negative analytic of deforming tendencies — rationalization, clarification, expansion, ennoblement, and the rest — and for the translator's characteristic betrayal, normalization, which flattens deliberate authorial deviation. It catches subtractive, paratextual, and typographic losses and the over-clarification that destroys an indirect meaning.

## When to use

- A literary translation may be flattening the author's deliberate deviations from ordinary usage.
- Paratextual or typographic features (italics, layout) that carried meaning have been dropped.
- A culture-specific detail has been over-clarified, destroying its figurative charge.
- Every value cannot be preserved at once and the reviewer must check which were consciously chosen.

## Procedure

1. Screen a literary translation for Berman's twelve deforming tendencies (P014).
2. Catch the subtractive changes that flatten a carefully constructed source text (P025).
3. Beware that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail to help the target reader can destroy the indirect, figurative meaning it carries across a text (P026).
4. Catch expansion and the two poles of stylistic distortion and impoverishment (P081).
5. Catch the effacement of vernacular, idiom, and superimposed languages, and prefer literal translation to the Platonic figure (P082).
6. Make the translator the judge of how much of a source's reference and allusion network to explain (P083).
7. Treat every translation choice as selective preservation (P114).
8. Guard against the translator's characteristic betrayal, normalization (P115).

## Inputs

- The literary source and its translation, with attention to the author's marked or deviant usage.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to screen a literary translation for Berman's twelve deforming tendencies (P014).
- The analysis fails to catch the subtractive changes that flatten a carefully constructed source text (P025).
- The analysis fails to beware that over-clarifying a culture-specific detail to help the target reader can destroy the indirect, figurative meaning it carries across a text (P026).
- The analysis fails to catch expansion and the two poles of stylistic distortion and impoverishment (P081).
- The analysis fails to catch the effacement of vernacular, idiom, and superimposed languages, and prefer literal translation to the Platonic figure (P082).
- The analysis fails to make the translator the judge of how much of a source's reference and allusion network to explain (P083).
- The analysis fails to treat every translation choice as selective preservation (P114).
