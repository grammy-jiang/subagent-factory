# Advertising Results Audit

## Purpose

Audit an advertising or marketing spend the way finance audits any other investment: against
a measured selling return. Marketing is an investment to be measured by results, not an
expense to be justified by awareness, impressions, or creative acclaim. Most companies never
measure their return at all; this skill makes the measurement mandatory and exposes spend
that cannot prove it sells.

## When to use

- A campaign or marketing spend produced visibility, impressions, or awareness but no
  measurable selling result, and the caller wants to know why it failed and what to demand.
- A proposed or completed advertising spend must be gated against a selling criterion before
  it proceeds or is judged a success.
- A brand is "successful" or well-known yet sales are flat, and someone must test whether the
  spend is actually driving purchases.

## Procedure

1. **Define what success means in selling terms.** Decide whether the spend is meant to drive
   incremental sales, loyalty, purchase intent, or repeat usage. Reject awareness or
   impressions as the success metric — awareness only gets a brand into the consideration set;
   it does not by itself sell. State the concrete result that would prove or disprove the spend.
2. **Establish a baseline.** Capture pre-spend sales (and the chosen success metric) for the
   relevant product, region, and period, so there is something to measure the result against.
3. **Measure pre- versus post-spend results.** Compare sales before and after the spend, or
   compare a region/segment where the spend ran against a comparable one where it did not, so
   the lift is attributable rather than assumed.
4. **Compute cost per incremental sale.** Divide total spend by the incremental change in
   sales and compare that figure against the company's usual cost-of-sales. A spend whose
   cost per incremental sale is worse than normal cost-of-sales has not earned its place.
5. **Refuse the standard excuses.** "We don't know how," "it can't be done," and "there are
   too many variables" are not exemptions — finance and accounting measure every other part of
   the business the same way. Treat an unmeasured spend as a failed audit until a return figure
   exists.
6. **Diagnose the root cause of a miss.** When the result is weak, trace it to the underlying
   mistake — chasing awareness or creative acclaim instead of selling, the wrong market, ceded
   control of the message, or no clear reason for the customer to buy — rather than blaming
   execution alone. Use the Kmart pattern as a cautionary check: sustained spend with no
   demanded return coincides with collapsing share against disciplined competitors.
7. **Issue a verdict and corrections.** Return a pass/fail on selling accountability, the
   measured (or missing) return, and a prioritized set of redirected actions, each tied to a
   measurable selling outcome.

## Inputs

- The spend or campaign under audit, its stated goal, the brand's selling situation, baseline
  and post-spend sales data (or comparable regions/segments), the budget, and what selling
  result the spend was meant to produce.

## Output

A pass-or-fail audit verdict: the selling result demanded, the measured return (or the fact
that none was measured), the cost per incremental sale versus normal cost-of-sales, the root
cause of any miss, and prioritized corrections — each tied to a measurable selling outcome.
