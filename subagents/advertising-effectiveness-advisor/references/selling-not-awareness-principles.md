# Selling-Not-Awareness Principles

The core principles by which every advertising or marketing choice is judged: each one names
a common belief, the corrected principle that replaces it, and the test to apply. The unifying
rule is that advertising exists to sell — more product, more often, to more people, for more
money — not to win awareness, differentiation, or creative acclaim for their own sake.

## Principle table

| # | Common belief (mistake) | Corrected principle | Test to apply |
|---|--------------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| 1 | Advertising is the 30-second commercial. | Everything a company does communicates and is advertising — packaging, employees, free media, sponsorship, the product itself. | Is every touchpoint, not just the ad, sending a message that sells? |
| 2 | Awareness drives sales. | Awareness only gets a brand into the consideration set; it does not by itself sell. After awareness you still have to sell. | Is the spend justified by an expected selling result, or only by reach/impressions/awareness? |
| 3 | Marketing is a cost to be justified. | Marketing is an investment to be measured by results, like any other business investment. | Is there a baseline and a planned measure of return in sales? |
| 4 | A well-known, "successful" brand keeps selling itself. | Success can be deadly; awareness and past success are perishable and must be re-earned. | Are sales actually moving, or is the brand coasting on recognition? |
| 5 | Reach the whole market; convince everyone. | Fish where the fish are: serve current buyers, then the undecideds; forget the rest. | Does the targeting hit relevant, winnable customers, or chase everyone? |
| 6 | More data is better. | Act on *relevant* data, not sheer volume of data. | Does the data answer who would buy and why, or just pile up numbers? |
| 7 | Differentiation/creative distinctiveness is the goal. | Differentiation matters only as a reason to buy that moves the customer to prefer you; it is motivating, not by itself sufficient. | Does the difference give the customer a reason to purchase over competitors? |
| 8 | A bounded choice (endorser, sponsorship, packaging, free media) is good if it gets us seen. | Bounded marketing decisions are judged by whether they sell, not by visibility for its own sake. | Does this specific choice produce a measurable selling outcome? |
| 9 | Any publicity is good publicity; free media is free. | Free media is rarely free; mismanaged publicity can be expensive, and bad news travels faster than good. | Have you accounted for the cost and risk of managing the publicity? |

## How to use

- For a **spend-justification** question, apply principles 1–3: is the spend tied to a
  measurable selling result rather than awareness?
- For a **flat-sales-despite-fame** question, apply principle 4.
- For a **targeting** question, apply principles 5–6 with
  `segmentation-and-positioning-playbook.md`.
- For a **bounded decision** (endorser / sponsorship / packaging / free media), apply
  principles 7–9 with the matching skill.
