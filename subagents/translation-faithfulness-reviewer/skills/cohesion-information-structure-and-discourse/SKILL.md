# Cohesion, Information Structure And Discourse

## Purpose

This skill reviews translation at the level of cohesion and information structure. It treats Halliday and Hasan's five cohesive devices (reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, lexical cohesion) as language-specific in preference and frequency, checks that thematic and given/new markedness is matched by function rather than transferred wholesale, and that reference patterns are reworked to the target language's norms.

## When to use

- A cohesive device has been transferred wholesale rather than reworked to the target language's preferences.
- Given/new or thematic markedness differs between source and target and must be re-signalled by the device the target uses.
- A participant or entity is being traced across differing reference, number, or gender systems.
- Source markedness that carries a communicative function is at risk of being flattened.

## Procedure

1. Preserve source markedness in the target text when it carries a communicative function (P010).
2. Signal given and new information with the device the target language uses, definiteness, grammatical subordination, cleft or pseudo-cleft structures, or punctuation, rather than transferring the source's (P025).
3. Handle conjunction as the marking of a small set of general relations, additive, adversative, causal, temporal, and continuative, whose realization and frequency vary sharply across languages, adding or thinning typical target (P045).
4. Accept that lexical-cohesion networks cannot be reproduced identically (P046).
5. Match thematic markedness by function, not form (P051).
6. When translating pronouns into a language that marks formality (P064).
7. Trace participants and entities through reference, the textual identity between expressions, most often pronouns, noting that a language's number and gender pronoun distinctions and its reliance on situational reference and (P065).
8. Do not transfer the source text's cohesive devices; rework the methods of establishing links to the target language's textual norms (P071).
9. Treat cohesion (Halliday and Hasan's five types of reference, substitution, ellipsis, conjunction, and lexical cohesion) as language-specific, rebuilding the web of cohesive ties in the target rather than copying it and keeping the (P075).
10. Adjust conjunction and chunking carefully (P097).
11. When moving between languages that differ in the grammatical category of number, choose between omitting the number information and encoding it lexically (P143).
12. Judge givenness by what the speaker assumes is in the addressee's consciousness, and reintroduce a previously mentioned element as new, for example with an indefinite form, after enough intervening text or a marked scene change, a (P144).
13. Rework reference patterns to the target language's norms (P145).
14. Remember that the five cohesive devices exist in many languages but with different preferences, frequencies, and combinations, that cohesion is also carried by tense continuity, stylistic consistency, and punctuation so that (P146).

## Inputs

- The source and target texts and the cohesion, information-structure, or reference pattern in play.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.
