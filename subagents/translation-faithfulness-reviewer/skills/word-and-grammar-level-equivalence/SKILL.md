# Word And Grammar-Level Equivalence

## Purpose

This skill reviews equivalence at and below the word and across grammar, rejecting cross-code identity and the one-to-one correspondence of words and meanings. It diagnoses the specific type of word-level non-equivalence, distinguishes propositional, expressive, presupposed, and evoked meaning, and checks that obligatory grammatical categories (gender, number, tense/aspect) the target forces are resolved by function rather than mapped mechanically.

## When to use

- A rendering rests on dictionary or referential identity and needs the signification critique applied.
- An obligatory grammatical category (gender, aspect, number) forces a resolution the target must make.
- A word-level non-equivalence needs diagnosing by type before a strategy is chosen.
- The receptor language requires additions or specifications the source leaves implicit.

## Procedure

1. Reject cross-code identity as the basis of translation (P006).
2. Diagnose the specific type of word-level non-equivalence (P014).
3. Distinguish the propositional, expressive, presupposed, and evoked meanings of a word or utterance when assessing a rendering, recognising that a charge of inaccuracy usually concerns propositional meaning while expressive meaning is (P041).
4. Handle grammatical gender by its function (P044).
5. Expect grammar to constrain the translation like a straitjacket (P063).
6. Never assume a one-to-one correspondence between words and meanings across languages (P070).
7. Always satisfy obligatory receptor-language features (P085).
8. Do not assume an idiom has a target equivalent (P095).
9. Do not map tense and aspect mechanically (P096).
10. Do not equate sameness of referent with sameness of signification (P099).
11. Resolve the categories the source and target languages obligatorily force (P112).
12. Distrust dictionary equivalence (P117).

## Inputs

- The source item, its rendering, and the word- or grammar-level equivalence claim it rests on.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to reject cross-code identity as the basis of translation (P006).
- The analysis fails to diagnose the specific type of word-level non-equivalence (P014).
- The analysis fails to distinguish the propositional, expressive, presupposed, and evoked meanings of a word or utterance when assessing a rendering, recognising that a charge of inaccuracy (P041).
- The analysis fails to handle grammatical gender by its function (P044).
- The analysis fails to expect grammar to constrain the translation like a straitjacket (P063).
- The analysis fails to never assume a one-to-one correspondence between words and meanings across languages (P070).
- The analysis fails to always satisfy obligatory receptor-language features (P085).
