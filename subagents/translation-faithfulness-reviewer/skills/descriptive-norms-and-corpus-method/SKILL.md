# Descriptive Norms And Corpus Method

## Purpose

This skill reviews the descriptive, norm-oriented, and corpus-based study of translation. It reconstructs translational norms from patterned behaviour at the receiving end rather than reading them off, treats textual products as primary and extratextual statements as secondary evidence, situates a translation on the adequacy-acceptability axis, and expects Toury's probabilistic laws — never assuming a translated text is representative of the target language.

## When to use

- A 'norm' claim is being made and needs checking against recurrent regularities rather than assertion.
- Translated texts are being used as evidence for target-language or textual-tradition claims.
- The adequacy-acceptability orientation of a translation or corpus needs situating.
- A corpus or Chesterman-style universal claim needs contextualized, verifiable evidence.

## Procedure

1. Never assume a translated text is representative of the target language or of any overall textual tradition thereof (P011).
2. Preserve the adequacy-acceptability axis by distinguishing source-norm orientation, target-norm orientation, and mixed compromises (P023).
3. Use Chesterman's S-universals (P066).
4. Understand that beyond observation sentences and truth functions translation runs on analytical hypotheses (P067).
5. Reconstruct the norms operating in a translation (P076).
6. Reconstruct translational norms from patterned behavior at the receiving end, using recurrent regularities in comparable products or practices as primary evidence (P086).
7. Treat textual products as primary evidence for norms and extratextual formulations as secondary interpretive evidence that must be controlled (P087).
8. Under globalization, test whether transported forms retain source function or acquire new local value through orders of indexicality and local uptake (P090).
9. Use carefully designed, contextualized parallel and comparable corpora to generalize and verify equivalence claims beyond individual cases (P091).
10. Take Quine's radical translation (P102).
11. Hold the inscrutability of reference in view (P103).
12. Expect Toury's probabilistic laws when reviewing translations (P114).
13. Explain shifts as norm-governed only when their realization is patterned (P133).

## Inputs

- The translation(s) or translation-studies analysis under review and its declared corpus, method, and norm claims.
- The reasoning offered for the decision under review: the orientation, strategy, brief, and any quality claim made.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw and the principle it violates, apply the correction, state the residual uncertainty and the trade-off it reflects, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. This skill reviews a translation, a translation choice, or a translation-studies analysis; it does not produce the finished translation or make the publication decision.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- The analysis fails to never assume a translated text is representative of the target language or of any overall textual tradition thereof (P011).
- The analysis fails to preserve the adequacy-acceptability axis by distinguishing source-norm orientation, target-norm orientation, and mixed compromises (P023).
- The analysis fails to use Chesterman's S-universals (P066).
- The analysis fails to understand that beyond observation sentences and truth functions translation runs on analytical hypotheses (P067).
- The analysis fails to reconstruct the norms operating in a translation (P076).
- The analysis fails to reconstruct translational norms from patterned behavior at the receiving end, using recurrent regularities in comparable products or practices as primary evidence (P086).
- The analysis fails to treat textual products as primary evidence for norms and extratextual formulations as secondary interpretive evidence that must be controlled (P087).
