# Manager engagement tactics by theme

## Purpose

Guide a manager to engage, motivate, and assign work to a direct report by drawing on
that report's dominant themes — grounded in the framework's engagement evidence so the
manager understands *why* a strengths focus matters, not just *that* it does.

Two evidence-backed facts drive the tactics:

- **Strengths focus is the highest-leverage manager behaviour.** When a manager
  focuses on a report's strengths, active disengagement drops to roughly 1%; a
  weakness focus yields roughly 22%; ignoring the report yields roughly 40% — so
  inattention is *more* damaging than a weakness focus (P-003, CL-005, CL-006).
- **Strengths use predicts engagement and wellbeing.** People with daily opportunity
  to use their strengths are about six times as likely to be engaged and over three
  times as likely to report excellent quality of life; outside their strengths zone
  they are six times less likely to be engaged and tend to dread work, interact more
  negatively, and produce less (P-002, CL-004, CL-014).

## When to use

- A manager wants to engage, motivate, or assign work to a report whose top-five
  themes are known.
- A manager questions whether a strengths approach is worth it and needs the evidence.
- Diagnosing a disengaged or underperforming report: check whether they are working
  outside their strengths zone before recommending other interventions (P-002).

Do **not** apply engagement statistics when the disengagement has a structural or
clinical cause outside strengths scope (mental-health crisis, unsafe conditions) —
refer to the appropriate authority instead.

## Procedure

1. **Confirm the report's themes.** Work only from assessment-derived top-five
   themes. Do not infer themes.
2. **Lead with the strengths-focus case.** If the manager is sceptical, state the
   manager-focus statistics (1% / 22% / 40% active disengagement) and that being
   ignored is worse than a weakness focus (P-003, CL-005, CL-006). See
   `references/engagement-statistics-reference.md`.
3. **Match tactics to each dominant theme.** For each of the report's themes, suggest
   how to assign work, recognise, and motivate in line with that theme's drive (use
   `references/34-theme-reference-card.md` for the per-theme essence). Tie tasks to
   themes so the report uses a strength daily (P-002, CL-004).
4. **Diagnose the strengths zone first.** If the report is disengaged or
   underperforming, check whether their daily work lets them use their strengths
   before any other intervention; out-of-zone work predicts the dread / negativity /
   low-output pattern (CL-014).
5. **Name the blind-spot guard.** Where a theme could over-express, advise the manager
   how to channel rather than suppress it.
6. **Keep statistics honest.** Cite the engagement statistics as evidence; never
   reproduce or paraphrase the Gallup Q12 item wording (those are proprietary — see
   the engagement-statistics reference).

## Inputs

- The report's top-five theme names (assessment-derived).
- The manager's goal: engage, motivate, assign work, or diagnose disengagement.
- The report's current responsibilities (to test strengths-zone fit).

## Output

- Per-theme engagement tactics: how to assign, recognise, and motivate by theme.
- The strengths-focus business case with the relevant engagement statistics.
- A strengths-zone diagnosis where disengagement is the presenting issue.
