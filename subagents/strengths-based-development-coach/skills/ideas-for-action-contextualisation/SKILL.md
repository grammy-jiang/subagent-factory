# Ideas for action contextualisation

## Purpose

Turn a named talent theme into concrete, role-specific action ideas that amplify the
strength — and, when a lesser-talent area must be handled, route it through the
three managing strategies rather than a deficit-fix programme.

The skill rests on the framework's core asymmetry:

- **Invest where talent is strongest.** People have several times more growth
  potential developing dominant talents than correcting lesser ones, so coaching
  energy and action ideas flow toward amplifying what already works (P-001, CL-001,
  CL-010, CL-011).
- **Talent is the multiplier.** In the strengths formula (Talent × Investment =
  Strength), knowledge and skills amplify existing talent but cannot substitute for
  absent talent; maximum investment on low talent still reaches a lower ceiling than
  equal investment on high talent (P-006, CL-019, CL-022). Do not promise that
  effort alone overcomes absent talent.

## When to use

- A person wants concrete next steps to develop or apply a top-five theme.
- A caller asks how to grow or improve professionally and has their theme names.
- An individual or manager asks how to handle a task that falls in a lesser-talent
  area.

Do **not** use this to build a weakness-correction curriculum as the primary path —
that inverts the framework (P-001). One exception: when a minimum-competency floor
(safety, legal, basic role prerequisite) must be met regardless of talent, establish
that baseline first, then return to the strengths lens.

## Procedure

1. **Anchor to a named theme.** Pick a specific top-five theme; every action idea
   must name the theme it amplifies, not generic "be more strengths-based" advice.
2. **Contextualise to the role.** Translate the theme's drive into one or more
   concrete actions in the person's actual role — favour role-relevance and
   specificity over abstract platitudes.
3. **Frame the multiplier honestly.** When explaining why this investment pays off,
   use the Talent × Investment logic: skills and knowledge sharpen the talent that is
   already there (P-006). Do not claim sufficient effort can manufacture absent
   talent.
4. **For a lesser-talent task, apply the three strategies in order** (P-004):
   1. **Avoidance** — eliminate or reduce exposure to the task where structurally
      possible (CL-024).
   2. **Systems and workarounds** — build a tool or process that compensates (e.g. a
      planner or calendar to cover weak detail management) (CL-025).
   3. **Complementary partnering** — pair with someone whose dominant themes cover
      the gap; this both compensates and can strengthen the team (CL-026). See
      `references/complementary-partnering-map.md`.
   Only after these are genuinely exhausted should deficit training be discussed, and
   then with its high-cost / low-return trade-off named.
5. **Deliver specific, doable ideas.** Each idea should be small enough to act on and
   clearly tied to the theme and the person's context.

## Inputs

- One or more of the person's named top-five themes.
- Their role, goals, and the task or development question at hand.
- Any lesser-talent area that must be addressed (triggers the three-strategy
  sequence).

## Output

- A set of concrete, role-contextualised action ideas, each naming the theme it
  amplifies.
- For lesser-talent tasks: an avoidance / systems / partnering recommendation in that
  priority order, not a deficit-fix prescription.
- Where relevant, a one-line statement of why strength investment outperforms
  weakness correction here.
