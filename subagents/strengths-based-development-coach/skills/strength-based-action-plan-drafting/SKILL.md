# Strength-based action plan drafting

## Purpose

Produce a written Strength-Based Action Plan organised by the person's top-five
themes, with goals at weekly, monthly, and annual horizons, that invests development
energy in dominant talents (P-001), names each theme's blind spot (P-005), routes any
weakness through the three managing strategies (P-004), and — when the plan includes
a manager — leads with strengths-focus tactics (P-003).

This is the `produce` mode artefact: a document the individual can keep and act on,
not live advice.

## When to use

- A caller asks for a written Strength-Based Action Plan with time-horizoned goals.
- A caller wants a shareable development document built from their top-five themes.

Do **not** produce a plan whose centre of gravity is weakness correction — the plan
amplifies dominant talents and only *manages around* lesser-talent areas (P-001,
P-004).

## Procedure

1. **One section per top-five theme.** Build the plan theme by theme; every goal
   names the theme it develops (P-001, CL-001). Do not pad with generic
   self-improvement items unrelated to a theme.
2. **Set goals at three horizons per theme.** For each theme, draft:
   - **Weekly** — a small, concrete action that applies the theme now.
   - **Monthly** — a development goal that deepens or stretches the theme.
   - **Annual** — a longer-range ambition where this theme compounds.
   Keep every goal specific and role-contextualised.
3. **Include a blind-spot note in each theme section** (P-005, CL-027). Name at least
   one over-expression risk for that theme and a guard against it; never present a
   theme section as purely positive.
4. **Address weaknesses by managing, not fixing** (P-004). Where the plan must cover
   a lesser-talent area, apply the three strategies in order: avoidance (CL-024),
   systems and workarounds (CL-025), then complementary partnering (CL-026). See
   `references/complementary-partnering-map.md`.
5. **Add manager-side recommendations when relevant** (P-003). If a manager will use
   the plan, lead with strengths-focus tactics — focusing on a person's strengths
   drives active disengagement down to roughly 1%, versus ~22% for a weakness focus
   (CL-005). See `references/engagement-statistics-reference.md`.
6. **Write a short narrative summary.** Close with a brief paragraph the individual
   can share that captures the combined signature of their themes.

## Inputs

- The person's top-five theme names (assessment-derived).
- Their role, goals, and any known weakness the plan must address.
- Whether a manager will use the plan (triggers the manager-side section).

## Output

- A Strength-Based Action Plan: one section per top-five theme, each with weekly,
  monthly, and annual goals plus a blind-spot note.
- A managing-weakness section using the three strategies in order where needed.
- Optional manager-side recommendations leading with strengths-focus tactics.
- A short shareable narrative summary.
