# Team strengths grid construction

## Purpose

Build a team strengths grid — a structured map of every team member's dominant
themes — so a leader can see talent coverage, overlaps, and gaps across the group,
and recommend complementary pairings by naming specific themes rather than offering
vague advice to "find someone different" (P-009, CL-036).

Partnering is the team-level form of the third managing strategy: pairing a person
with a colleague whose dominant themes cover their gap both compensates for
individual weakness and can build a stronger team (P-004 strategy 3, CL-026).

## When to use

- Extract mode: a team leader provides several members' top-five theme names and asks
  for coverage mapping or pairing advice.
- Produce mode generates a team-level document built on the grid.

Do **not** build a full grid for a single individual — for one person, use
individual-level complementary partnering (the third strategy in
`ideas-for-action-contextualisation`) instead.

## Procedure

1. **Collect each member's themes.** Gather every team member's top-five theme names
   from their completed assessments. Do not infer themes for anyone unassessed.
2. **Lay out the grid.** List members down one axis and their dominant themes across
   the other, so each member's themes and the team-wide spread are both visible.
3. **Mark coverage and overlap.** Identify themes that appear across multiple members
   (concentration / overlap) — strengths the team has in depth, and where everyone
   may share the same blind spot.
4. **Surface gaps.** Identify domains of talent thinly represented or absent across
   the whole team — coverage gaps that could affect performance (CL-036). A gap that
   could affect delivery is itself a lesser-talent area to manage via P-004.
5. **Recommend complementary pairings by name.** Where a member has a gap another
   member's dominant theme covers, recommend that specific pairing and name the
   themes involved (P-009, CL-026). Avoid generic "pair with someone diverse" advice.
6. **Flag concentration risk.** Where every member shares a theme, note the shared
   over-expression risk so the team can guard against a collective blind spot.

## Inputs

- A roster of team members with each member's top-five theme names (assessment-derived).
- The team's purpose or the coverage question the leader wants answered.

## Output

- A team strengths grid: members × dominant themes.
- Coverage overlaps and gaps identified explicitly.
- Complementary partnering pairs recommended by name, with the themes that make each
  pair complementary.
