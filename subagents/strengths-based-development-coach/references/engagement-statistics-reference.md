# Engagement statistics reference

The citable engagement statistics from the framework's research base, plus the rights
note on the Gallup Q12 instrument. Use these to ground the business and human case for
a strengths-based approach.

## Strengths-zone outcomes (individual)

| Measure | Effect | Source |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Daily opportunity to use strengths → engagement | ≈ 6× more likely to be engaged at work | P-002 / CL-004 |
| Daily opportunity to use strengths → wellbeing | > 3× as likely to report excellent quality of life | P-002 / CL-004 |
| Working outside the strengths zone → engagement | ≈ 6× *less* likely to be engaged | P-002 / CL-014 |

Out-of-zone behavioural pattern (CL-014): people working outside their strengths zone
tend to dread going to work, have more negative interactions with colleagues, treat
customers poorly, achieve less, and have fewer creative moments. Check strengths-zone
fit before recommending any other intervention for disengagement.

## Manager-focus and active disengagement

What a manager primarily does with an employee predicts active disengagement
(P-003, CL-005, CL-006):

| Manager primarily… | Approx. active disengagement |
|--------------------|------------------------------|
| Focuses on strengths | ≈ 1% |
| Focuses on weaknesses | ≈ 22% |
| Ignores the employee | ≈ 40% |

Key implication: being **ignored** is more damaging to engagement than having a
manager who focuses on weaknesses (CL-006). Lead manager coaching with strengths-focus
tactics, and flag inattention as the worst option.

## Q12 rights note (P-008)

The Gallup Q12 engagement items are proprietary information protected by law; they may
not be reproduced, quoted, or used in a survey without written consent from Gallup
(CL-041).

- **Citable:** the engagement *statistics* derived from Q12 research (the 6× / 3× /
  1% / 22% / 40% figures above).
- **Not citable:** the Q12 *item wording* — never reproduce or paraphrase the items
  themselves, item by item.
